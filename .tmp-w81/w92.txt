TAGLOG 1. THE WORLD BEFORE THE WALLS-
By the time the 22nd century had limped halfway through, humanity had already collapsed — twice.

The First Collapse was fire and famine. The Second was data and delusion.
Fossil-fueled governments, corporate monarchies, synthetic democracies — all failed, their power decomposed into tribal syndicates governed by predatory AIs and militarized black-market labs. Currency splintered. Nations crumbled into memory. Reality itself blurred — fragmented across augmented overlays, deepfake identities, and privatized simulation loops.

But somewhere beneath the neon fog and the hollowed-out megacities, something primitive endured.

In the silence of forgotten malls, beneath crushed rail lines and weed-choked tunnels, they re-emerged.
The GraffPUNKS.

They weren’t an army. Not then.
They were whispers in aerosol.
A movement. A mutation.
A memory refusing to die.

TAGLOG 2. THE PAINTWARS-

Between 2200 and 2300, every city on Earth — what was left of them — was hit by a wave of encoded defiance.
The PaintWars.

It started with glyphs — encrypted tags blasted onto walls, train cars, and the bones of fallen towers. These weren’t just ArT. They were GK data bombs — broadcasting signals, resistance logs, and psycho-memetic codes through magnetized ink and photonic pigments.

Every wall became a screen.
Every mural, a message.
Every mark, a memory weapon.

That’s when they invented GK Writcode.

A visual language that could only be decoded under precise frequencies, moods, or intent. It merged sigil magic with QR-depth layering and neuro-sensitive spray. GK Writcode could hijack drones. Collapse surveillance nodes. Unlock doors to the forbidden archives. It was Graffiti turned guerrilla code.

Governments tried to scrub the walls.
But every tag erased was replaced tenfold.
This wasn’t a war over land anymore.
It was a war over memory.

Who gets to remember?
Who gets to rewrite?

TAGLOG 3. THE TAG HIERARCHY-
Press enter or click to view image in full size

The GraffPUNKS weren’t lawless. Not exactly.
They moved in Cells, but lived by an unspoken law of respect, lineage, and ink:

Tag Lords — The ancients. First-glyph legends from the Data Famine of 2191. Their tags were so rare, so potent, they said one could still crash a server or erase an identity from the chain if reactivated.
Burn Crews — Tactical bombing units. Masters of large-scale takeover. They could blanket a superstructure in minutes — armed with drone swarms and vertigear paintpacks.
Ghost Writers — Solitary mystics who never spoke, only sprayed. Rumored to be neural-linked to a forgotten AI rebel called CHAINFIRE. Some said they painted while dreaming. Others said they no longer existed in this timeline.
Stencil Witches — Urban shamans. They merged arcane geometry with bleeding-edge tech, projecting glyphs that haunted AR feeds and rewrote local memory through recursion pulses. They were feared and revered.
Each generation formed a Tag Epoch — defined by color philosophy, style density, and overlay rituals. They didn’t just just paint on concrete.
They tagged the subconscious.
They left marks on dreamscapes, memory vaults, and orphaned blockchains.
