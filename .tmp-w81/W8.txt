Darren Cullen (aka SER)
Darren Cullen (aka SER) is a pioneering London-based professional graffiti artist, street art entrepreneur, DJ/music producer, and Web3 innovator from Croydon, UK. He is one of the UK’s earliest graffiti writers (active since 1983) and the founder of Graffiti Kings, a trailblazing commercial graffiti and street art agency that operated for 25+ years before closing in late 2024. Cullen has since pivoted 100% into the GraffPunks / GKniftyHEADS ecosystem—a multi-chain blockchain project blending 40 years of UK hip-hop/graffiti/pirate radio culture with NFTs, play-to-earn gaming metaverses, phygital designer toys, AR street art, and a 24/7 online radio station. His work emphasizes “legacy over hype,” decentralization, community ownership, and “banking the unbanked” through tokenized street culture. He has no connection to any other public figure of the same name.

Background & Biography
Darren James Cullen grew up in Croydon, south London, and began graffiti in 1983 (as a child/early teen) amid London’s BMX, skateboarding, and nascent UK Hip Hop scene. He was part of the original “lil Acorn GraffPUNKS” crew, engaging in classic 80s illegal activities like tagging trains, bombing, and running pirate radio stations from tower blocks. His tag SER started as a crew name (”South East Rockers”) for south London writers; he adopted it permanently when others moved on. Early vandalism led to council interventions, which he turned into paid commissions and youth workshops—marking his shift from “vandal to professional.” By his teens he was already doing legal murals for play centers and train stations. He credits Banksy with helping legitimize graffiti as an art form. Cullen has described himself as a “veteran writer and consummate businessman” who built a global brand from underground roots. He is also a DJ, music producer, digital creator, and blockchain consultant. Current base: London/Kent area. Strong emphasis on mentorship, female empowerment in graffiti, and turning rebellion into positive community impact.

Graffiti Kings (1999–2024)
Cullen founded Graffiti Kings in 1999 as an informal movement of friends teaching graffiti workshops after local councils (Merton, Croydon, Sutton, Lambeth) requested more youth programs to reduce vandalism. It formalized into a limited company around 2007, headquartered at Leake Street (the famous Banksy Tunnel) in London. The agency represented ~30 artists and specialized in commercial graffiti, street art, murals, live painting, branding, event activations, and workshops. It served worldwide clients including major brands (Nike, Adidas, Red Bull, Netflix), Hollywood stars, central banks, royalty, public institutions, and events. Key milestones:

2012 London Olympics: Painted stage sets for the opening ceremony; customized the Olympic cab for BBC London Tonight (described as “artistic royalty”).

Notable public works: Hyperrealist mural of Kate Middleton and the “royal baby” at Brookland Lake, Snodland, Kent; sea-themed classroom murals at Rockmount Infants School (Upper Norwood) with artist Paul Barlow; eco-bus paint job for a green campaigner (chip-fat powered, toured globally).

Workshops & community: Anti-vandalism programs across the UK (e.g., painting Wallington Train Station with 50+ young people involved); corporate executive graffiti sessions; live events at Trinity Leeds and for Sony; features on BBC Young Apprentice.

TV/media: Covered extensively; positioned as the “bridge” between street culture and legacy brands.

The company closed/dissolved in December 2024 so Cullen could focus fully on Web3. His original artworks (spray on board/canvas) have appeared at auction with estimates £800–£1,700+ and have sold through galleries.
