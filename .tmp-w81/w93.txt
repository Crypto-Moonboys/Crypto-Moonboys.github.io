To most, they’re a spectacle. A marvel. A sacred, blood-stained ritual dressed up as progress. They call it tradition. I call it control. Deception. Selection by algorithm. The winners? They don’t just get a prize — they vanish into the stars, into the Crypto Moongirl empire, into her program.

Every solar cycle, 40 Graff-Knight factions — GK sets from across what’s left of the Earth — receive a coded transmission from the Queen’s orbital satellite array. A summon. A demand.

Each gang must offer up ten male champions and two female emissaries. Not volunteers. Not requests. Selections. Chosen for their bloodline, street score, data legacy. If they refuse? Their entire zone gets wiped off the map. I’ve seen it happen. We all have.

It’s not just a competition. It’s a culling. calibration of what the Queen believes humanity should be.

The Queen — Sarah P-Fly, high sovereign of Block Topia — runs it like clockwork Bitcoin orange. She calls it the Hard Fork Space Program. But it’s more than that. It’s a chain-bound meritocracy, a cosmic mating algorithm disguised as ascension. And worst of all… people worship it.

Within the Walled Garden of Queens, it’s no longer a man’s world. That died long ago. The Crypto Moongirls rule now — elite, interstellar bounty hunters bred from Earth’s best and brightest. Trained to lead the HODL Warriors & Crypto Moonboys into combat across the solar network, they serve as judge, jury, and gene-selector of humanity’s future.

All men who survive the Games become Crypto Moonboys. If they climb high enough, they earn the title of HODL Warrior — and a golden ticket: the chance to “seed” with a Moongirl. That’s their dream. That’s the prize. A life of luxury behind the walls of Block Topia. Until they’re summoned again to fight.

But the Program isn’t about warriors. It’s about filtering. Distilling.

It’s not military training. It’s a cultural filter. A spiritual crucible. A genetic firewall. Pfly doesn’t care about love. She’s breeding the next era of Earth — selecting men not by strength, but by submission, survival, and utility to the chain.

Only HODL Warriors may Seed.
Only with Crypto Moongirls.
The rest of us? We’re just data points.

The pairings are cold. Calculated. Run through chain-compatibility matrices. Overseen by Pfly herself. No affection. No choice. Children born of these engineered unions are known as the Bitcoin Kids — pure in ideology, encoded at birth, chain-ID hardwired into their irises.

People call it salvation.
I call it synthetic eugenics.

We don’t start as warriors. We start as Nomad Bears — street-born or exile-blooded rejects tossed into the system to be “corrected.” Most of us never make it past this tier. Emotional suppression. Loyalty testing. Crypto-logic under pressure. One mistake, and you’re erased.

Then comes the All City Bulls — strong, stable, chain-aligned. You fight in AR combat rings, float through low-grav evasion mazes, build chainshields by thought. It’s not training. It’s rewriting your brain.

At the top? The HODL Warriors — less human than code. They’re implanted with Bitbone chips, which let them see blockchain fragments in real time. Their minds are open sockets. Moongirl tech flows through them. They’re loyal to Pfly — not the Earth.

The men love the Games. To them, it’s legend. Legacy. A chance to climb out of exile and into Moongirl arms.

But for me? It was a nightmare.

I never trained. Never wanted this. But the GraffPUNKS selected me. They said it was a test. A trial. A show of loyalty. I think it was a sacrifice.

I was one of the 480 selected this year. 400 men. 80 women. We were shipped in armored skytrains and crypto-choppers to the outer zones of Block Topia.

They gave us a week of paradise before the games — they called it Feast Week.

But paradise always comes at a price.

They call it a privilege. A blessing. An honour.
But I know better now.
It’s a leash — gilded, perfumed, plated in gold, but still a leash.

I’ve seen what happens when they say no. No one ever hears from them again.
So we came, dressed like guests but treated like test subjects.

The first thing that hits you when you step inside Block Topia is the perfection.
It’s unnatural.
The air tastes cleaner, but it makes your lungs ache. The buildings gleam too much. The smiles stretch too wide.
The city is a mirage — a glittering code-scape designed to make you forget what lies outside those walls.

We were fed foods grown on orbital gardens, sprayed with spices no one could afford to name.
Wine poured endlessly.
Synthetic grapes. Real temptation.
Every chair plush. Every corridor pulsing with sensory overload.
You think it’s paradise… until you feel the eyes.
Moongirl drones. Watching. Logging. Deciding.

There were banquets and staged performances.
Ancient Earth operas. Holograms of long-dead queens.
Music from centuries past warped into sleek, glitchy anthems of submission.

They weren’t feeding us.
They were grooming us.

Even the HODL Warriors — men who’d earned their way through fire and ruin — sat among us, gleaming like trophies.
But I saw through the polish.
They weren’t free.
They were caged wolves, dressed in silk, trained to smile.
They were here because the Moongirls allowed them to be.

Feast Week wasn’t about celebration.
It was about surveillance.
A test of loyalty, obedience, and control.

I tried to blend in at first.
Tried to laugh at the right moments. Toast when expected. Smile on cue.
But every second I stayed, I felt my grip loosening.
This world — it was beautiful, yes.
But it wasn’t ours.
It was a mirror, held up by those who wanted to see if we’d break under the weight of their perfection.

They told us we were special.

They told us girls we’d been chosen for something greater. That the Queen wanted street-born women in the next generation of Crypto Moongirls. That our strength, our minds, our rhythms were needed in Block Topia.

That we wouldn’t have to fight.

Drones watched from the vents. Neural scanners pulsed from our beds. They tracked our dreams. Every thought, every word, every twitch was logged. No one told us. No one warned us.

They categorized us silently. Three layers:

Green Layer: Stable. Adaptable. Candidates for psychic encoding.
Yellow Layer: Tribal. Loyal. Dangerous in groups. Good for genome harvesting.
Red Layer: Unstable. Rebellious. Too independent. Flagged for memory mapping. Or erasure.
I don’t know what category I’m in. But I’ve seen the others vanish. One by one.
Some got trinkets, medals, fake invitations to royal houses.
Others just… disappeared.

They say the Queen encodes our remains into the Sovereign Algorithm.
That our thoughts, memories, souls are fed into her chain.

I don’t know what scares me more —
The lie they told us.
Or the fact that most of us believed it.

Pfly knows that one day, a girl from outside — born in the chaos, raised by graffiti and dust — might rise.

One who doesn’t need the chain.
One who can’t be scanned.
One who fights not for selection — but for freedom.


When the ceremonies ended, they didn’t warn us.
No announcement. No kindness.
Just a silent shift.
Feast Week was over.
The Hard Fork Games had begun.

They threw us into an arena the size of a city block.
The ground was cracked.
The air stank of metal and sweat.
Somewhere high above, the Moongirls watched from their spires, perched like birds of prey in neon thrones.

The first challenge was chaos.
They called it The Initiate’s Maze.
Traps. Shifting walls. Exploding floors.
They told us to reach the center. That the prize awaited.

I didn’t care.

I wasn’t there to win.
I was there to survive.

The other Knights ran like they’d been programmed to do it — fast, clean, calculated.
But I moved different.
I listened. I watched.
I let the maze reveal itself, step by step.

I didn’t trust the rules.
I didn’t trust anything that gleamed.

Most fell in the first hour.

The maze didn’t care how strong you were.
It cared how well you obeyed.

I kept going.
Through the smoke.
Through the screaming.
Through the silence that came after.

When I finally reached the center, I didn’t find a prize.
I found nothing.
Just a blinking sensor, and the sound of drones humming far above.

The Moongirls weren’t rewarding us.
They were recording us.

Endurance. Strategy. Submission.
Every move we made was another line in our profile.
Another tick in the box of their perfect world.

I had passed, but I didn’t feel like a winner.
I felt like bait.

The final trial came next.
Fewer of us now. Tired. Hungry. Angry.

The arena transformed into something worse:
A battlefield.

The ground shook with impact mines.
The air choked with static fog.
Our bodies were already failing.
But this round wasn’t about strength anymore.

It was about choice.

The Moongirls wanted to see who would kneel.
Who would fight for a place beside them.
Who would trade freedom for favour.

I watched the others line up, desperate for the prize they still believed was salvation.
They didn’t know what I knew.
The winners would be cloned, reprogrammed, bred for the Moongirl machine.
Soldiers. Seed-bearers. Obedient nodes in their perfect blockchain society.

I couldn’t be part of it.
I wouldn’t.

When the signal to begin sounded, I didn’t raise my fists.
I didn’t draw my blade.

I ran.

Not away. Not toward the prize.
But through the system.
Through every weak spot I’d memorized.
Through every blind zone I’d mapped.

Because the real test wasn’t surviving.
It was refusing to become what they wanted me to be.

And I passed.

Barely.

I’m writing this with cracked fingers.
My legs are shaking.
I haven’t slept in what feels like days.
But I’m still here.

And I’m still me.

The Moongirls may control the world.
They may write the rules.
But they can’t code my soul.
Not unless I let them.

And I won’t.

The Games aren’t over.
But neither is the fight.

Not while I breathe.
Not while I remember who I am.
