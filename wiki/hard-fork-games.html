HARD FORK GAMESThe institution that turns a shared generation into competing futures

The Hard Fork Games are a major Year 3008 institution associated with Block Topia, faction rivalry and the public sorting of young identities into different futures. The archive agrees that the Games matter; it does not preserve one perfectly consistent rulebook.

Some records describe formal trials and three broad outcomes. Others emphasise forty-faction spectacle, recruitment, seeding, infiltration and political theatre. The convergence canon keeps the shared structure and labels the variable mechanics as era-specific or disputed.

Convergence canon / mechanics vary by archive strand

Why the Name Matters

A blockchain hard fork creates incompatible futures from a shared history. The Games borrow that image and apply it to people. Candidates who grew up inside the same system can leave the event serving different institutions, factions or ideas of freedom.

The name does not mean the Games caused the 2880 Triple Fork. It means the civilisation built after that disaster turned “fork” into a cultural word for irreversible choice.

What the Archive Consistently Preserves

  • The Games exist in the Year 3008 Block Topia / faction world.
  • They combine spectacle with selection, status and political meaning.
  • Young people are evaluated in public and their outcomes affect faction power.
  • Crypto Moongirls and Queen Sarah P-fly are closely associated with Block Topia's official Games tradition.
  • Bitcoin X Kids represent the inside generation shaped by Block Topia.
  • Bitcoin KiD's rebel movement turns the Games from controlled sorting into an opportunity for infiltration and defection.
  • The Games sit inside the wider 40/40 faction mythology, although exact participation counts vary by source.

The Three-Path Tradition

One strong archive strand describes three broad outcomes: City Service, Space Programme and Escape. The first accepts a recognised place inside Block Topia. The second leaves ordinary city life through an approved mission. The third rejects the authorised future and attempts life beyond the city's protection.

The exact names and procedures may change between Games cycles. What matters is the underlying choice: remain inside verified order, extend that order, or accept the uncertainty of leaving it.

The Forty Around the Games

The wider archive repeatedly connects the Games to the Forty Paths. Factions scout, recruit, manipulate, sponsor, observe or wait for candidates who emerge from the event. This turns the Games into more than a Block Topia ceremony; they become a recurring pressure point across the whole civilisation.

Not all forty faction names are currently locked. The Games should not be used as an excuse to invent missing factions merely to complete a bracket.

Bitcoin X Kids

Bitcoin X Kids belong to the controlled inside lineage of Block Topia: engineered, trained or socially formed to represent the city's preferred future. Older pages sometimes collapse them with the Bitcoin Kids or Bitcoin Kid Army. The current canon keeps those groups distinct.

Their role makes the Games a cultural mirror: the X Kids demonstrate what a successful Block Topia generation looks like when viewed from inside the walls.

The Bitcoin Kid Army Infiltration

W81 preserves a major 3008 story in which Alfie Blaze's Bitcoin Kid Army infiltrates the Games disguised as Bitcoin X Kids. They move through Block Topia during the spectacle, are eventually identified, and clash with an X Kid force beneath the city.

Later retellings add exact troop counts, named champions, portal mechanics and increasingly mythic combat details. The convergence reading preserves the infiltration, exposure and ideological confrontation while treating ornamental details as story-layer variation unless separately locked.

HODL and the Games

Older pages sometimes describe “HODL Warrior” as a rank awarded by surviving the Games. That is too narrow. In the current convergence canon, HODL Warriors are primarily the collective defence response that emerges when faction rivalry threatens to destroy the shared record itself.

Individual competitors may later become HODL Warriors, but the Games do not own the title and HODL Warriors are not simply the Games' champion class.

The NULL Crisis

NULL changes the Games because systems built to reward predictable compatibility become vulnerable when the underlying record is corrupted. People previously treated as unstable, rebellious or difficult to classify may become useful precisely because they do not behave as the model expects.

This reversal is one of the setting's central ironies: the perfect system may need the people it rejected in order to survive.

Lore Is Not a Live Game Claim

The Hard Fork Games are established narrative canon. Old W81 material also contains crowdfunding plans, proposed NFT rewards, token mechanics and prototype game systems. Those plans are archive history, not automatic claims about what the current website or blockchain contracts provide.

Runtime features should be documented from deployed code. This page explains the lore institution.

What the Games Test

The deepest question is not who wins a tournament. It is who gets to define a young person's legitimate future. Block Topia calls classification civilisation. Rebels call self-authorship civilisation. The Games force that argument into public view.

A hard fork is frightening because both sides still remember being one thing before the split.
