Book I — The First Witness

First WitnessCanonical scripture · Book ILast updated: October 2026

Book I establishes the Covenant’s first discipline: do not demand that the future trust your authority when you can leave it evidence.

The text

1 Speak what you know as knowledge, and what you believe as belief. Do not make certainty from fear.

2 A witness is not made true by rank. A beggar may remember what a king denies.

3 Write the act beside the name of the one who ordered it. Power without record grows a second face.

4 Keep more than one account where the matter is grave. Agreement can be corrupted; disagreement can preserve the shape of what happened.

5 Do not erase a person because their presence troubles the story you prefer.

6 When you correct a record, leave the scar of correction. A clean lie is more dangerous than an admitted error.

7 Do not worship the ledger. A record can preserve a lie perfectly.

8 Do not despise the ledger. A memory without witness can be stolen by the loudest survivor.

9 Let evidence travel beyond the house that created it.

10 Keep the names of the dead where possible. To strip a name from history is to kill without ending.

11 Where proof is absent, say that proof is absent. The unknown is not an enemy.

12 Where witnesses conflict, preserve the conflict until better evidence comes.

13 Do not force two testimonies into one merely because uncertainty is uncomfortable.

14 Those who inherit your records inherit your mistakes. Mark them clearly.

15 Give the accused a record of the accusation and a path to answer it.

16 Give the ruler no private history of public acts.

17 Give the child a way to learn what the parent chose not to say.

18 Truth does not become yours because you carried it.

19 Memory is custody, not property.

20 A witness who cannot be questioned is only an authority wearing another name.

21 Leave proof for those who distrust you. They are part of the future too.

22 Let no single keeper hold the only copy of a matter that can bind many lives.

23 When the witness fails, seek another. When all witnesses fail, do not manufacture one.

24 A people who cannot admit what they do not know will soon be ruled by those who claim to know everything.

25 Your final duty is not to be believed. It is to leave enough that those after you may judge.

Reading rule

This Book belongs to the 2030 Covenant layer. Later pages may interpret it through Year 3008 events, but those later names are not retroactively part of the original text.

The wording is kept direct because the tradition had to survive translation, quotation, song, argument and wall transmission rather than depend on one ornate liturgical language.
