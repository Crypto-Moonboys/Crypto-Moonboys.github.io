Book VII — The Hold

First WitnessCanonical scripture · Book VIILast updated: October 2026

Book VII gave later centuries the language that became HODL doctrine. Its original subject was not price. It was endurance under pressure.

The text

1 Hold what must survive the panic.

2 Do not hold merely because you are afraid to admit loss.

3 Endurance is a virtue only when the thing endured for deserves continuation.

4 Hold the child when the crowd runs.

5 Hold the record when the ruler orders it burned.

6 Hold the bridge while others cross, then cross yourself if you can.

7 Hold your word when keeping it is difficult and breaking it would harm another.

8 Release a promise obtained by fraud.

9 Hold enough reserve that tomorrow is not purchased entirely from today.

10 Hold culture through practice, not slogans.

11 Hold knowledge by teaching it.

12 Hold a community by making it capable of surviving your absence.

13 Hold a boundary when crossing it would destroy those behind you.

14 Open a boundary when keeping it serves only pride.

15 Hold grief long enough to learn from it; do not build a throne from it.

16 Hold doubt beside belief. Certainty without doubt can become cruelty.

17 Hold courage beside caution. Caution without courage can become permission for cruelty.

18 Hold the line together when the threat would erase every side.

19 Do not demand unity for ordinary rivalry. Emergency unity used every day becomes permanent command.

20 Know the difference between a price falling and a principle failing.

21 Know the difference between patience and denial.

22 Hold the name of the forgotten until someone can speak it again.

23 Hold the evidence that your own side behaved badly.

24 Hold long enough for another hand to take the weight.

25 Nothing living is held forever. The final act of keeping is knowing when to pass it on.

Reading rule

This Book belongs to the 2030 Covenant layer. Later pages may interpret it through Year 3008 events, but those later names are not retroactively part of the original text.

The wording is kept direct because the tradition had to survive translation, quotation, song, argument and wall transmission rather than depend on one ornate liturgical language.

Hold without clinging

Book VII deliberately contains a tension found across older spiritual and philosophical traditions: endurance matters, but attachment can become another prison. The strong person is not the one who never lets go. The strong person knows what deserves holding and what must be released.

This is why “Hold what must survive” sits beside “Do not hold merely because you are afraid to admit loss.” Later HODL cultures often quoted the first line and quietly forgot the second.

Real-life reading: endurance is not staying in harm

People misuse endurance language in abusive relationships, failing businesses, addictions, dead friendships and impossible promises. Book VII gives no medal for suffering that only protects the source of the suffering.

Hold the person who needs you. Hold the evidence. Hold your values. Hold a difficult course when fear is the only reason to quit. But release the lie, the sunk cost, the promise obtained by coercion and the identity that exists only because others expect it.

The question is never simply Can I endure this? It is What will my endurance keep alive? See Life Readings.
