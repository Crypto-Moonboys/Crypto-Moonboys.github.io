Life on the Waterfront

The Blue Electric Bot is a floating survey orb whose visible arcs make it easy to pick out after dusk. The harbor’s maintenance crews send it around shopfronts and dock approaches to compare the behaviour of fixtures under ordinary load. It became more interested in the moment before a light fails than in the impressive flash afterwards.

The noise below the music

Blue first learnt to separate two electrical sounds in a workshop whose owner hummed in the same key as a failing sign. It recorded both and tagged the owner’s voice as an issue for tomorrow, which everyone in the room agreed was fair. On the next shift it was assigned a chain of shopfronts. It spent so much time listening outside the record shop that the clerk made it a shelf-height docking spot where it would stop obscuring the opening hours.

It has a competitive streak where Green Electric Bot is concerned. Blue can hear a failing fixture before the sensor logs a drop, and Green can get an entire row of readings while Blue is still listening to one lamp. They swap notebooks every seventh round. Blue holds on to any page the Boombox marks with a note, including the humiliating transformer that made the entire practice room lose the beat.

The record-shop window

The orb used to treat every interruption as a maintenance call. A record-shop clerk showed it how the owner deliberately dims the window before the evening listening session. The orb added the shop’s working hours to its rounds. It still keeps an extra pass for the places where a failing lamp can quietly turn an open route into a dead end.

Off duty

Its hobby is collecting the hum of old signs. It sends the quietest ones to the Walking Boombox, which rates them on whether a drummer could keep time to them. Blue has never received a favourable review for a transformer.

The last lamp before the turn

When the second exit went dark, Blue entered the fitting’s buzz on the slate. Green’s slower sample showed that the garden sensor’s delay was a different event. Blue took that personally until Nobby brought back both pieces from the bench and asked which fault Blue could reproduce. The orb caught itself wanting to be right about both. The useful outcome was a working lamp and a note telling the next shift how to report a dark patch without guessing at the cause.

It wants to test the corner during a quiet shift and a crowded one before promising the same result at both. It also wants the apprentice to have time on her sheet for checking the readings. It expects to be asked to automate that part. Blue’s first question to a visitor is when they noticed the light change. Its second is what they were trying to see.
