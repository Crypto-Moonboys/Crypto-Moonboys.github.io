A mark that survives by being made again

Graffiti’s resilience begins with exposure. A mark can be weathered, covered or removed, and its maker still has to decide whether to return. The essay places creativity in that repeated act rather than only in an object’s permanence. A culture survives through people learning, responding and carrying its methods into another place.

Digital publication changes how work is copied and encountered, while the questions of credit and context continue. Read the piece beside GK’s street-art history and artist records. The strongest bridge between wall and screen is a visible practice: recognisable decisions, a traceable maker and enough surrounding story for a new audience to understand why the mark matters.
