An island with a shift change

The Ninth Berth is a working landing and a meeting place on Lumina’s Waterfront, the island district of Block Topia. Its name comes from a chipped number over a service door, left from an allocation grid the district stopped using. People still say they are going to the Ninth even when their job is up the lane at the hot counter or under the stair where the riggers keep spare rope. After dark, the bright shops give way quickly to wet stone, working lights and hulls that catch the rain. Every shift sends people past the blue lantern who depend on different parts of the city for the money they will eat tomorrow.

A berth fee buys use of the landing for a time. The sheds, lights and stairs each have their own arrangements. Block Topia’s depots can keep equipment going as long as a responsible person is prepared to sign. Outside crews can bring food, spare tools and knowledge of a route without granting City Hall any power over the rest of their work. Small stallholders can advance wages as long as there is enough trade left to make it back. A meeting about keeping a light on often sounds like a meeting about who owns the island. It ends with somebody checking whether the tea urn has been paid for.

The night the tide came in twice

The first plans were for a timed rigging exhibition. Jack the Bull wanted to show how quickly his crew could unload a relief haul. Jimmy Rugpull booked the labour on money still in transit. Teddy Boi Jones booked the stage and a sound operator. Above the lower walk ZARAH8 had signed a reading she trusted. The night began with rain coming hard against the water, a crowd gathering and three different people convinced their part of the show was ready.

A short power interruption made the berth bell repeat its warning and the gauge display replay its last reading. The water climbed. The story that a tide had come in twice was more memorable than the story that one tide had kept coming after the instruments stopped helping. Chada Boi recorded the two bells and kept the distinction alive at the hearing. Nobody could dry out the bottom two stalls by deciding which name would stick.

Zarah closed the lower walk. Nobby brought the reserve gear up. Nine Toes Joe led people away from the flooded walk and showed Eddie where loaded handcarts could make the turn. Eddie Stax put the directions on the wall. Teddy took the show off the air and announced those directions. Jack and Jimmy’s crews moved relief supplies in the same rain that had ruined their exhibition. By morning some of the material was where it needed to be, one porter had a damaged wrist, and the people whose stalls had taken water were already adding things up.

A hearing beside the ice

Danny Rankz convened the hearing around a borrowed fish counter. The inquiry found a failed display, an inspection that depended on it too heavily, a late closure and wages promised against money that had yet to arrive. Jimmy and Jack made good the wage balance. The repair fund paid for part of one stallholder’s losses and ran dry. The people whose money was still missing did not get a more generous settlement because the story made a good broadcast. Their claims remain on the public board as amounts owing, with their names withheld at their request.

The local agreement that followed gives the upper landing two equipment custodians on separate shifts, requires a physical reading alongside the gauge, keeps a second exit marked, and gives crews a place to post wage balances and challenge a charge. The agreement is small enough to be argued over in one room. It lasts as long as the signatures, money and people behind it keep turning up. On the first review evening, half the meeting went on a dispute over whether an apprentice should be paid to hold the spare key. At the next meeting she brought her own timesheet.

The nine people at the table

The berth meetings bring together ZARAH8 of the Crypto Moongirls, Eddie Stax of the GraffPUNKS, Chada Boi of the Crypto Stoned Boys, Nobby of the Hard Fork Rockers, Jimmy Rugpull of the Rugpull Miners, Danny Rankz of the Blockchain Furies, Teddy Boi Jones of the Blockstars, Jack the Bull of the All City Bulls and Nine Toes Joe of the Nomad Bears. Their authority comes from particular crews and jobs, and costs them something when they use it. Each can go home, lose an argument, or find someone else has already got the necessary work done.

During ordinary weeks Eddie keeps a space below the notices for corrections. Nobby works out of the repair bay. Chada records the sounds of odd objects at the listening table. Danny takes private claims at the end away from the crowd. On performance nights Teddy tries to find a good place for a stage that does not obstruct any of it. Jimmy makes sure the performers have somewhere dry to queue for their pay. Jack has already offered to mark and time that queue. Joe thinks there is a site further uphill where everybody could sit down first. Zarah would like an answer about who pays the apprentice.

The first trial at the upper landing

By the third review the apprentice had been paid for the hours on her timesheet. Nobody had budgeted for her to do the same job again. ZARAH8 persuaded the depot to cover one trial shift at the upper landing, on the condition that the berth send back a count of the people it served. The repair fund was still empty. Jack wanted to show a full crossing before the shift ended. Danny wanted a reason on the record for everyone who turned back.

Sailor Bear arrived with three crossing times in his pocket and gave Jack the longest. Jack asked which one belonged on the board. Sailor asked whether Jack would pay the difference if a worker took the quickest time literally. They were still at it when the Police Robot walked to the rear of the queue and found a porter sitting on an upturned crate. The upper route was open, but the porter could only carry with one hand since hurting his wrist on the double-tide night.

The Police Car pulled out of the freight lane and made its cabin available to anyone who needed to wait off their feet. Its first passenger was a woman who kept missing her place when she sat down. The Police Bike took the relief-shift instructions to the bakery door and waited for the incoming crew to say them back. When the rider returned, the Robot entered the woman’s arrival by the time she joined the line, and Jack’s neat count gained a name he had nearly lost.

The dark turn by the garden

A lamp went dark where the second exit turned between the garden beds. Blue Electric Bot drifted low enough to hear the fitting buzz. Green Electric Bot waited for the dusk cycle to finish and brought back a reading from the garden side. The shade had delayed one sensor, while the dark turn had stayed dark through two tests. Blue marked both times on the slate. Green drew a circle round the earlier reading so Nobby could see why someone in a hurry would have replaced the wrong part.

Nine Toes Joe had tested the curve with a loaded handcart on a dry day. That evening the Nomad Bears Mech brought a crate carried by two people and asked them to try the turn in the rain. Its orange balloon kept bobbing above the hedge after the crate stopped. The person walking backwards could see the next lamp but had nowhere to put a heel.

Camo Bear offered to lift the edge bed after a tenant came to take her seedlings. She had paid for that corner in labour and wanted to see where they were going. They settled on a turnout made with boards for the evening. The two orbs marked the limits of the lit ground while the Nomad pilot wrote down how much room the crate actually needed. Camo wrote the tenant’s collection hours underneath. Both entries went into Joe’s route notes.

The ink that stayed

Eddie’s directions from the flood night still covered one shutter. The owner wanted the arrows painted in permanently and offered Graff Bear a morning’s wage to finish it before opening. Graff could match the blue. Under the first arrow he found the heel of a letter that belonged to the shutter’s older sign. He took the brush off the wall and asked Posh Bear how much of the old paint was still there.

Posh used his monocle to trace a shallow cut in the grain. It gave them enough of a border to move the arrow without crossing the name. The Graffiti Kings Mech’s crew traced the surviving letters and left a copy for the former signwriter. The GraffPunks Mech lifted Graff and a second painter to hang a removable direction board on the metal rail above them. Its operator read the credits back at ground level where the shop workers could hear every name.

The owner paid for the board and kept the wage offer open for the morning. Eddie added a blank strip beneath the new arrow. It gave the next shift somewhere to record a correction without having to cover a worker’s name or ask a painter to donate another morning.

The repair bay and the spare key

When the depot sent a spare lamp, its receipt still named the closed lower service room. The Moongirls Mech brought the copy to ZARAH8 before anyone signed for it. They corrected the room while both shifts were present. Nobby and the Hard Fork Rockers Mech set the damaged fitting out on the upper bench so the incoming custodian could see what had come away from the wall.

Burglar Bear was offered an extra fee to put the spare key in the proprietor’s pocket and let him sort out the shifts himself. He asked the apprentice what had happened the last time she had needed it. She had waited forty minutes with the lower walk closed and been paid for none of them. He fitted a lock both custodians could operate and held the handover open until they had each tried their key. The apprentice logged the time.

The Waterfront Samurai had been hired to accompany the new custodian between the depot and the bench. When she heard about the key dispute she asked to be accompanied through the handover as well. The Samurai named a price for the extra time and offered to take it at their next booked crossing. By the time they left together, both keys had been tried and the form carried the two names that would have to answer for them.

The table that stayed open

Danny and the Blockchain Furies Mech set a folding table far enough from the main line that people could tell a story without the queue hearing it. A claimant from one of the flooded stalls brought two soaked drawer handles. Gentleman Bear remembered one by the copper wire wound under its grip. He took a description and returned the handle he had in his ledger. Only one of her two missing drawers turned up that night.

The HODL WARS Mech had offered to move a pallet of recovered property. Its operator found that a crate listed as empty held smaller packets someone had put inside it to dry. They went back through the slips, giving each owner a chance to decide whether their name could go on the outside. Danny kept the public entries anonymous while the people involved confirmed the handovers in private.

Jack put the Allcity Bulls Mech’s clock over the landing. Jimmy asked the operator to log the time spent helping at Danny’s table as well. A worker had agreed to stay because her colleague could only meet Danny after closing. The operator added the time to a second column rather than folding it into the crossing score. Jimmy could pay the trial shift, and Danny could file the outstanding losses. A recovered drawer handle made one person’s stock easier to identify. She still had a ruined counter to replace.

The ten seconds after the song

Teddy wanted a broadcast people would keep listening to between route announcements. The Blockstars Mech pointed its lamps at the stage and left enough light for people to coil the cables afterwards. Chada brought the two-bell recording. Walking Boombox asked what would happen if somebody in the queue heard it halfway through and took the second bell for a new warning. Teddy handed the recording back and asked the Boombox for something it would lend willingly.

Jamaican Bear brought a record from the case and a sleeve with the lender’s note still inside. He asked for the note to be returned before he agreed to play the record. While the track went on, the Boombox left a gap for a late route message at the end. In the silence everyone heard the apprentice ask whether the second custodian was expected to stay through closing. Teddy read the hour from her timesheet before he read the sponsors’ names. The lamp crew heard their own names while they were still there to correct one.

The first game after shift

The upper landing still had a strip of open ground after the trial, and the No Ball Games crew rolled out its lines. St Patrick’s Day Bear walked the marked exit as the last person in a procession of two, with Straight Outta Ghetto Bear dragging the equipment bag. At the turnout he stepped aside and watched the bag take the whole gap. They moved the near line uphill. One regular team had already warmed up and called it a stitch-up.

Straight Outta Ghetto Bear handed the first round to the closing workers. A stallholder asked to play for ten minutes and went back to tally her losses with her shoes wet. The No Ball Games Mech stayed while the workers put the grounds back. St Patrick’s Day Bear handed over the last route board to the bakery shift himself and told the late music group when they could use the space without holding that crew back again.

At closing the upper route had stayed open through one paid trial. The wage balances from the flood had already been made good, and the repair claims still hung on the public board. The ninth berth number was visible from the new turn if you knew to look back. Nobody had put money down for another shift. The next meeting would have to decide who paid for one before anybody called the arrangement permanent.

The black tow

At the next meeting Jack brought a figure for reopening the lower landing. It included new timber and two hours of inspection. The apprentice put a wet strip of rope on the table. It was fresh out of the reserve locker. Somebody had offered the evening launch enough cash to take a butcher’s waste out past the ninth marker and lose the sack on the way home.

Sailor Bear was already on the upper steps when the launch came back. It had its lamp on and no one at the tiller. In the gap between two reflections, Nessie lifted the top of her head. She breathed once where there should have been an engine noise. Then her neck rose and a second swell followed it, travelling on the same line. The apprentice reached down for the painter. Sailor caught her by the belt. The launch went sideways so fast that the iron ring came out of the stone with its mortar still on it.

One crewman had made it onto a mooring float. His mate had gone under with the sack line around his boot. When the neck came up again it was lower, the jaws open, the lamp burning straight into an amber eye. The tow had swung across her route, and she bit through the timber holding it there. The man on the float took a piece of the broken gunwale through his palm. The man on the line was never brought back.

Chada laid the recording beside the old two-bell tape. The first file had a motor cutting out, solid timber breaking and someone shouting for a knife. The second still had two bells and an electrical fault. Danny kept them in separate envelopes. The original flood claims stayed where they were. The dead man’s brother asked for the employer’s name. The boat’s papers named a broker who had never been aboard.

The north cut burns

Two nights later the watch saw a row of black points offshore. The harbor marker disappeared behind a head that was still rising. The Harbor Kaiju walked across the outer reach with water falling off its chest in sheets. The woman on watch had time to call the north road clear before she saw how shallow it was coming.

Nine Toes Joe had spent the paid trial arguing for a second way home. Now a depot driver tried to use that way to get his rig back out. Joe stood in his lights and made him reverse while the Police Bike ran the message to the uphill turn. The rig stayed in the yard. Three workers who would have met it head-on came through with a stretcher and nobody had to get off the road to let them pass.

The Kaiju put one foot into the north bank. The mud split under the kerb and stones hit a shed hard enough to punch the tin inward. It went west along the low apron. When it turned back east its mouth opened across the yard and fire crossed the side of a shut freight office. Nobby found two men in the passage behind it. One had crawled the length of the wall with his coat burning. The other died before a stretcher could reach the upper landing.

Eddie’s board could still be read at the north turn. He changed the arrow while there was light enough to see the edge of the brush. Zarah entered the closure by hand and read it back to the apprentice before she rang the depot. The Kaiju went back to the sea after nightfall. At dawn the yard owner wanted a reopening time, and the two men were still on the same shift roster.

What the berth kept

At the hearing the claims took up two different sides of the counter. Nessie had left a boat broken in the water and a man missing. The Kaiju had left foot wells north of the berth, a burned office and a death on land. Witnesses who had only seen the fire had already acquired a story about two beasts coming in together. Danny could put the tide readings, watch times and separate pay sheets in front of them. He could not make a widow wait any less by proving she had come to the right side of the room.

The first money available paid night-watch wages and a replacement radio. The lower walk stayed shut. Sailor refused to carry a private load across the black tow. The depot refused to price the north cut until the foot wells had stopped taking water. The apprentice wrote both refusals on Eddie’s board, with the date and the names of the people who had actually said them. In the morning a broker had scratched his own name out. She put it back in.
