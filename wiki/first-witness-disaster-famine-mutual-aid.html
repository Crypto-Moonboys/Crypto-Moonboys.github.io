Disaster, Famine and Mutual Aid

First WitnessEmergency tradition · Section 76Last updated: October 2026

Disaster is where every principle becomes concrete: who gets water, who gets rescued first, what records matter, who commands, and when emergency authority ends.

Life before property

In immediate life-threatening disaster, rescue takes precedence over ordinary property boundaries where reasonably necessary. This does not erase later restitution.

The triage problem

Scarcity may require choosing who receives limited medicine, transport or shelter first. The tradition has no painless formula. Decisions should use published criteria rather than hidden status where possible.

Emergency ledgers

Supplies distributed during crisis are recorded when doing so does not delay urgent aid. Records prevent later theft, double-counting and propaganda about who helped whom.

Mutual aid

Local networks often act faster than large institutions. Large institutions can move resources local networks do not possess. The tradition prefers interoperability rather than romanticising one scale.

Disaster profiteering

Price increases can ration scarce goods or exploit desperation. Economic schools argue fiercely over the boundary. The strongest criticism targets actors who create or withhold scarcity deliberately to increase profit.

End the emergency

Every extraordinary emergency power should include a review point. Rebuilding after disaster is not permission to govern permanently as if the disaster never ended.
