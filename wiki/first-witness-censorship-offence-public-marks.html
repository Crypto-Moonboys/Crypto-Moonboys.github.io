Censorship, Offence and Public Marks

First WitnessExpression ethics · Section 65Last updated: October 2026

Book IV protects public expression without pretending every mark deserves permanent protection. Shared surfaces force communities to decide when removal is stewardship and when it is censorship.

Offence is not one category

A political insult, racial threat, obscene joke, commercial advert, memorial, harassment campaign and ugly drawing can all offend for different reasons. Treating them as one problem creates bad rules.

Threat versus insult

The tradition distinguishes speech that attacks dignity from speech that credibly signals intended harm. Both may be condemned; the second creates a stronger case for immediate protective action.

The public wall problem

No public surface can contain every mark forever. Removal is unavoidable. Witness Wall systems therefore focus on preserving a record of what was removed and why rather than promising permanent physical display.

Private platforms

A private venue may set rules, but the problem changes when one platform becomes functionally unavoidable for public participation. Book V dependency language then enters the argument.

Compelled speech

Forcing somebody to repeat a belief they reject is treated differently from requiring professional conduct or basic factual disclosure. The boundary remains heavily disputed.

The censorship test

One question asks: Would you trust this removal rule if your least favourite faction controlled it next year?
