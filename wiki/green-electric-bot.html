Life on the Waterfront

The Green Electric Bot was built from a charge shell recovered from a retired greenhouse. Its present circuit follows garden lights and moisture posts where the harbor meets the nature extension. When it hovers around a dark fixture it is usually waiting for the automatic dusk cycle to give it a fair test.

The garden clock

Underglass work taught Green to wait through shadows moving across a bench. As a replacement shell it still carried the old labels for irrigation rows when it was sent out to check public lights. It spent several afternoons recording faults on equipment that was doing precisely what someone needed at that hour. Camo taught it to distinguish a person watering after their shift from a leak. Green has used the next season to learn which names people prefer for the same growing bed.

It suspects Blue calls every ambiguous hum a promising lead because Blue enjoys listening. Green is almost as bad with plant names. It can lose a conversation by politely reading all three names for clover before answering a question about a garden gate. It gives St Patrick’s Day Bear the cups that sprout crooked shoots first, on the grounds that his table receives the most invitations.

The branch over the sensor

The orb once asked a grounds crew to replace every light that failed on the same evening. Camo Bear walked it back along the trail and pointed out where a newly grown branch covered the light sensor. They freed the sensor and left most of the branch. Green subsequently learned to log shade and weather before it fills out the parts order.

Off duty

It collects the names people use for the same plant and repeats all of them when asked for directions. The habit can double the length of a route announcement. It has earned the orb an invitation to every seed swap on its round.

The reading taken too early

The dark-turn trial brought an old embarrassment back. Green had blamed a whole row of lights on hardware once and somebody else had carried the parts. This time the orb waited for the cycle, circled the early reading and watched Blue turn its good ear toward the dead fitting. It was pleased when their logs could both stand, and uneasy when a visitor saw the two timestamps as proof that the exit had closed twice.

Green wants to print the time of the next test and the reason for waiting at eye level by the garden. Camo has suggested using the space for the seedling collection hours. They may have to learn to share one piece of board. Green now asks people which plant or shadow they use as a landmark after dark. It listens for the answer before offering directions.
