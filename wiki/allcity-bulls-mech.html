Life on the Waterfront

The Allcity Bulls Mech brings the Bulls’ love of public rankings down to dock level. Its timing lights were designed for exhibition heats. On the Waterfront they mark how long a relief team actually takes to load and clear a landing, including the time spent resting or helping a slower carrier.

A clock above the loading line

The mech’s operator was a timekeeper at the edge of a yard before they got a seat on a loading frame. They remember what the crews said about a disputed result while the person controlling the clock went for a hot meal. The operator loved speed enough to take those arguments personally. It took the unpaid volunteer from the exhibition run to make them ask whose work was included in a fast time and whose had been treated as a gift.

They restore mechanical gym clocks off duty. Jack knows which one gains four seconds and uses that fact to wind them up before a trial. Jimmy prefers a clock the operator can read back to him at the end of a shift rather than trust whoever gets their figures to him first. The operator will defend a good contest to either of them and grumble when a prize comes out of the timekeepers’ pay.

The second timed run

On a demonstration day the chassis posted an exceptional run after a volunteer shifted a crate while it waited at the line. The score stood until the scorer heard the volunteer ask whether she would be paid. The crew ran the exercise again with the whole loading shift on the clock. It took longer and gave them times they could actually plan around.

Off duty

While it stands in for repairs, the operator rebuilds the little mechanical clocks that hung above local gyms. The oldest one gains four seconds every afternoon. They keep its rate written on the back because correcting it would erase a measurement they have kept since their first paid run.

The second column for the Ninth

During the trial the operator kept a crossing time on one side of the slate and the extra time spent helping at the claims table on the other. If they had left assistance off the slate, one worker’s late finish would have gone missing again. The operator was pleased until somebody from the depot asked whether the extra column belonged on the display at all. They have copied both columns into the notes they keep behind the clock.

A fair comparison between crossings may let Jack make a stronger case for a funded slot. The operator is prepared for someone to publish the faster number by itself. Ask them who won the run and they may tell you which worker had to get home late and whether she got her money.
