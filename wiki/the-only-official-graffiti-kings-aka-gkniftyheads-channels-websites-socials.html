Finding the publisher behind a familiar name

A channel register helps someone follow a project across sites, publications and social accounts. Its usefulness depends on provenance: a familiar logo or copied biography is weaker evidence than a route linked from an established project property. An old register can still explain how the network was organised at the time it was written.

The original list belongs to October 2025 and its claim to exclusivity belongs to that notice. For a present route, start from the current owned site and follow its live links. That keeps the historical list useful as a record of publishing relationships while allowing names, platforms and destinations to change without treating an old announcement as a permanent exhaustive directory.

Following the record across channels

Following a project across channels is easier when each destination has a clear purpose. The main website supplies the present entry points. A publication carries an authored argument or a story. An artist’s own pages document their practice. A collection record identifies a particular set of works. These destinations can support one another while answering different questions, so the best route depends on what a reader is trying to establish.

An announcement is especially sensitive to time. It may describe a campaign, rename a programme or link to a service that later moves. Keeping its publication period visible lets a reader understand why an old address appears without assuming that every instruction remains current. When a route changes, a useful record retains the original context and gives people a direct way into the present project.

Attribution matters within the creative network as much as at its boundary. A collaboration should leave its contributors recognisable; a shared character should retain the difference between its creator and the fictional role. The same care helps a new reader move from a social post into a substantial story without losing who wrote it or where its images came from.

For a route into today’s work, use the project guide or the Creator Studio linked below. For a particular story, use its current topic article and connected readings. The historical channel notice remains available here because it is part of that publishing history, with a scope and a date a reader can understand.
