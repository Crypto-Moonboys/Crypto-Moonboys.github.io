Calendar, Feasts and Days of Remembrance

First WitnessCalendar tradition · Section 40Last updated: October 2026

There is no single compulsory First Witness calendar. Shared dates emerged anyway because communities need recurring moments to remember what daily life makes easy to forget.

Concord Day

Observed in communities that accept the 2030 date, Concord Day remembers agreement without sameness. The traditional practice is to share a meal with somebody outside your usual social or faction circle.

The day is often criticised when institutions perform symbolic diversity while leaving ordinary power untouched.

Witness Day

A separate observance marks the Bitcoin provenance event. Technical communities verify hashes, restore backups and test old archives. Nontechnical communities review wills, emergency information and family records.

The day is deliberately practical. A ceremony about preservation that does not test whether the backup works is considered embarrassing.

The Day of Repair

On the Day of Repair, communities focus on maintenance rather than new construction: broken tools, public furniture, code, roofs, relationships, records and neglected promises.

Businesses sometimes hate the custom because repair does not photograph as well as launches.

The Release Day

Some economic schools use a yearly or multi-year Release Day to review old debts, penalties and obligations. This is not automatic cancellation. It is a forced audit against permanent extraction.

The date remains politically controversial wherever debt is central to power.

The Empty Chair Meal

Families and councils occasionally leave one place empty for somebody absent from the decision, dead, excluded or unable to attend. Unlike the parable, the ritual requires participants to name whose absence the chair represents.

An unnamed empty chair is considered decoration.

Chainfire Remembrance

Post-2880 communities remember the Triple Fork by displaying conflicting records side by side: three maps, three identity files, three ownership claims, three histories of one event.

The purpose is not spectacle. It trains people to understand how quickly administrative disagreement can become human catastrophe.

The Unwritten Night

Some communities end the year with an intentionally unscheduled evening. No official programme is permitted. People may gather, work, sleep, paint, argue or do nothing.

The custom exists to remind institutions that not every meaningful hour requires governance.
