Ageing, Retirement and Elderhood

First WitnessLife-course tradition · Section 74Last updated: October 2026

The Keeper eventually confronts the hardest handover: recognising when experience has become an excuse not to leave the chair.

Elder is not ruler

Elders may hold memory and judgement earned through long experience. That does not create automatic command over younger adults.

The last apprenticeship

Some traditions encourage older experts to take one final apprentice specifically to preserve tacit knowledge that was never written down.

Retirement as transfer

Retirement rituals focus on what authority, access, knowledge and unfinished obligations are being transferred. A farewell speech without a handover is considered incomplete.

The founder's second life

Founders are encouraged to build an identity after leadership so leaving office does not feel like social death. People cling to power partly because they cannot imagine who they are without it.

Ageing bodies

Care traditions resist treating loss of speed, strength or memory as loss of moral status. They also resist pretending decline is always beautiful or easy.

The elder question

What are you still protecting—and what are you now preventing somebody else from learning to carry?
