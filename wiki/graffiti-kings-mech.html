Life on the Waterfront

The Graffiti Kings Mech carries a painted tribute to the street-art lineage behind Crypto Moonboys. In the Year 3008 setting a local crew uses the chassis to carry mural supplies and give older signwriters room to lay out full-size lettering. The pink and gold panels generate arguments about colour before a can is open.

The letter cut around a hinge

The operator of the Graffiti Kings tribute chassis learnt big lettering from someone who painted between shifts and charged by the day. Their first useful tracing copied a letter that bent around the cupboard hinge instead of pretending the hinge was missing. They still show it to signwriters who ask how close a restoration should sit to an old surface. The mech makes room for older painters and the argument they bring about what to keep, who remembers the first version and who can change it.

The tracing album has one empty page for a sign whose writer asked them to keep the drawing out of it. They lend the album with that page intact. Graff Bear is one of the few people who knows why. The operator will happily debate colours with the GraffPunks Mech while their own paint is peeling at a seam they have been meaning to seal for months.

The name on the wall

A commission offered the crew good money to repaint a wall and hide all traces of its earlier marks. The surface included lettering from a worker who wanted her name kept, even after the shop closed. The crew brought her back to pick a section to preserve and had to negotiate the size of the new work around it.

Off duty

The operator copies old signs by hand. They keep an album of letters that had to bend to fit around pipes, shop hinges and other people’s work. Anyone can borrow it if they leave their own tracing between its pages.

The copy that went home

At the upper landing they traced the letters underneath Eddie’s direction arrow and left a copy for the former signwriter to keep. They wanted to add the tracing to their travelling album. The signwriter asked to see it first, and the operator spent the walk back worrying they had made a present she would feel obliged to let them borrow. She asked for a second copy without the later arrow, a version the operator had assumed was less valuable.

They now have permission to display one sheet with its later markings included. The signwriter’s clean copy stays with her. The operator hopes the owner will pay for a proper mounting rail before a new direction board turns up. If a visitor asks about the battered mech panels, they’ll give the name of the person who painted each repair they can remember.
