A border drawn through a joke

The Kent migration fable turns a geographical boundary into an absurd instruction. Its humour depends on the distance between a map’s clean line and a person’s complicated sense of home. A longitude can classify a place while explaining very little about the lives organised around it.

Read the directive as satirical world-making, with its own voice and exaggerated purpose. Its useful connection to the saga concerns belonging: who gets to define a safe side, what someone is asked to leave behind and how an official-sounding instruction can conceal a fragile argument. Desperate pings flooded in from Parisian servers and London auction houses — the so-called “gatekeepers” of the art world. These were the men who once sneered at a JPEG, the galleries that treated crypto like a parlor trick. Now, they were drowning. They saw the “Melt Up” beginning in the East — a violent, vertical ascent of asset classes outside the US dollar’s cage — and they wanted in.

They begged for liquidity. They offered us their “blue chips.” They held up their 500 limited-edition Banksy prints and their dusty bags of Bored Apes like holy relics, praying for a swap. They thought 10,000 units was scarcity. They thought they held power.

The High Hats — our Digital Aristocrats — didn’t even look up from their screens. They just laughed. “You think your bubble from 2021 is currency here?” they transmitted back. “You are trying to trade statues for water in a desert. Your scarcity is artificial. Your time is over.”

The Information Mercenaries intercepted the remaining calls, cutting the lines. There would be no lifeboat for the galleries. They missed the boat because they were too busy polishing the brass on the Titanic.

Deep within the Holly bunker, The Code Alchemists initiated the sequence. They unlocked the 1 Million+ WAX Reservoir. The Old World thought value came from exclusivity — the “1 of 1” mindset. We proved that in the brave new world, value comes from ubiquity, velocity, and sheer industrial scale.

My WAX collection wasn’t just a “bag.” It was the raw material for the next civilization. It was digital clay.
