Life on the Waterfront

Burglar Bear earned the name during a run of early-morning shutdowns when residents repeatedly found their own keys trapped behind workshop shutters. An apprentice with a steady hand soon became the bearer of everybody’s bad news about their locks. He kept the striped shirt and mask he wore when he first took on the nickname.

The price of a quick opening

Burglar Bear’s first locksmith jobs came from people who were supposed to be at work while their keys were still inside. He used to quote a price for getting the door open and take whoever had called him at their word. After the tenant was locked away from her belongings, he began asking who would be inconvenienced by the new fitting and who was still owed a key. He sometimes keeps a prospective customer talking for so long that they decide to pay somebody else.

He wears the mask to work and takes it off at Gentleman Bear’s counter when he wants to be thanked without feeling like he has come to collect a fee. He likes the useless prop keys better than the useful ones. A child in an amateur show can use the biggest one to open the smallest door. He asks the costume crew how they made that work and is impatient with people who assume the question is a joke.

The shutter job

His work goes best when someone with authority will meet him at the door. One landlord hired him to change a clasp while a tenant was on shift. The tenant lost a night’s access to her belongings. Burglar Bear paid her for his part in it and still takes the long way around the yard where she works.

Off duty

He collects useless keys, threads them on coloured string and lends them as props for amateur theatre. Gentleman Bear supplies old labels. Every few months they disagree over whether a key ought to be displayed with the story of the person who had to hand it back.

Two custodians, two working keys

At the repair bay the proprietor offered extra for a simple handover. Burglar asked the apprentice about the last time she had been kept waiting and heard a precise account of forty minutes she had never been paid for. Letting her try the key in front of both shifts cost him the extra fee. He remembered how quickly the landlord’s old job had gone and how much longer the tenant had taken to get even part of her loss back.

He expects the duplicate key to outlast the two names currently defending it. He wants the next apprentice to inherit a right to use it, with the handover hours on a paysheet, instead of inheriting a story about a kind locksmith. Around the island he asks whose name is missing from a door before he makes a guess about who ought to open it.

The lost fee put him back to selling weed between locksmith jobs. The Bike rider repeated a customer’s words about a missing key exactly as she had said them. Burglar heard the fear the depot had left out of its copy. He can still talk himself into taking the fast money, and he knows whose face he will see if he does.
