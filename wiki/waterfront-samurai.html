Life on the Waterfront

The Samurai took the name from a painted mask kept above the door of a lost freight office. Today the figure works out of Lumina’s Waterfront escorting old carriers and first-time traders whose crates are more expensive than their insurance. The armoured silhouette is conspicuous enough to make a meeting point out of an unmarked turning.

The price of staying beside someone

There are years the Samurai rarely speaks about between the loss of the freight office and taking escort work around the harbor. The old mask hanging over that office was a sign a late carrier could identify at a distance. The Samurai recreated its shape after a customer asked how they would recognise the escort in a crowd. They can wait on one corner long enough that passing workers begin to use them to give directions.

Their closest thing to a luxury is a box of sound paper from a bookseller who pays them in remnants for mending torn jackets. Sailor Bear carries a bookmark from the box through every shower. The Samurai once repaired a book he wanted to throw away and was privately offended when he kept using the worst of its damaged pages as note paper. They ask for payment up front when a trader wants an impossible arrival time.

Where the job ends

The Samurai once walked a valuable package through a market and left its exhausted owner behind. The owner had paid for company all the way home. They spent the following afternoon working out which part of the promise had been broken and what a fair refund would cover. The Samurai now starts a job by asking where they will part company.

Off duty

As a pastime the escort repairs torn book corners with scraps saved from parcel paper. The narrowest remnants become bookmarks left at Sailor Bear’s table. The Samurai charges for a night walk and will spend all morning repairing a stranger’s book for free.

Company through the handover

At the trial the new custodian had paid for company across the harbor and then asked the Samurai to stay beside her while the spare key changed hands. The Samurai quoted the extra time. Later they wondered aloud whether naming a price there had made the custodian feel she should apologise for asking. She came back to pay at the next crossing and said knowing the cost meant she could decide for herself.

They expect the next shift to ask for a route all the way from the depot to the berth once the paperwork is ready. They are thinking about writing that end point on the agreement instead of leaving it to the person with the least power to ask. Around the island they ask visitors where they want company to stop, then answer questions about the best book corner they’ve repaired.
