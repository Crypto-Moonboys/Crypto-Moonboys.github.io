👤 Delicious Again Pete

👤 Lore 📅 Last updated: October 2026 📂 Lore

Delicious Again Pete is the name used by this wiki for Delicious Again Peter, the artist identity associated in the supplied archive with Peter Clark. His strongest real-world source record concerns hand-crafted resin art toys and collectible sculpture, with hip-hop, science-fiction and pop-culture references. The older page’s claim that he co-founded Graffiti Kings is not established by that record.

An artist’s identity and a fictional association

Peter Clark’s creative identity should be distinguished from the event-hosting counterpart developed in the Crypto Moonboys fiction. The archive places his physical work within the culture encountered by Graffiti Kings and Moonboy collectors. That association does not make every object an official project product or establish a legal role in Graffiti Kings.

Within the fiction, the Pete figure gathers people through art, music and celebration. The earlier page associated those gatherings with GKniftyHEADS and GraffPUNKS, among other participants. These are story relationships rather than evidence of a real appointment, label contract or responsibility for every event carrying a connected name.

The resin artist in the archive

The W81 subject register identifies Peter Clark as the maker behind deliciousagainpeter.com, connecting hand-crafted resin work with hip-hop nostalgia. The longer creator compilation gives the same identity and describes independent collectible figures, sculpture, hand-painting and retro presentation. These sources supply a concrete artistic practice that the old music-only description obscured.

The compilation describes small-run work rather than mass production, with sculpting, resin casting and finishing as part of the process. That account is an archive description of the practice, not a verified specification for every release. Edition size, material details, articulation, signature and packaging can differ by work and should be described from the individual item.

Art toys, nostalgia and packaging

The profile’s recurring visual references include hip-hop, science fiction, comics and the toy culture of earlier decades. The figure and its packaging work together: a small sculpted object can recall the way an action figure was displayed, collected or imagined, while changing the character or cultural reference inside that familiar format.

The source lists works under names such as Darth Dunks, X-Ray Boba and Run the Jabba, alongside hip-hop-themed figures. These are examples recorded by the archived profile, not a current stock list. Naming a work preserves its creative reference without claiming an official licence from every musician, entertainment company or brand evoked by the design.

The term bootleg toy appears in the archive as a scene description for independent mash-ups and custom figures. It is not used here as a legal conclusion about any particular object. The relevant artistic question is how a recognisable collectible format is reworked into a different image, joke or memory; the rights attached to a commercial release remain a separate matter.

Physical work within a digital culture

The wider archive repeatedly uses Peter Clark’s resin pieces as examples of physical objects carrying the same musical and visual memories that digital creators reference. A sculpture on a shelf and a character on a screen can participate in a shared cultural vocabulary while remaining different works with different makers, editions and ownership conditions.

Some broader manifesto passages claim official physical-digital pairings or universal collector benefits. The reviewed material does not establish those terms for every Delicious Again Peter work. An artwork’s appearance in a Moonboy story does not automatically give it an NFT twin, a redemption route, a game bonus or a licence to use a project trademark. The phygital reference explains why the exact release matters.

Fiction: the host of a contested gathering

The inherited Year 3008 page imagines Pete organising underground raves and art gatherings in the forgotten corners of Block Topia. These places bring factions into contact outside formal confrontation. Music and visual work give people a reason to share space; conversations, exchanges and uncertain alliances can occur while a gathering is under way.

The source does not make that shared space automatically safe or politically neutral. It also describes rivalries, secret meetings and the possibility that celebration conceals competing intentions. Those tensions are the distinctive fictional contribution worth preserving after the repeated paragraphs are removed. A host can open a door between groups without resolving the interests that brought them there.

Rumours of messages carried inside music remain part of this inherited story tradition. They are not claims that Peter Clark ran a real covert operation or encoded secret instructions in a real DJ set. The related Whale Lords chronicle’s invented “Whale Song” episode is likewise not treated as a documented recording or performance credit.

Fictional hospitality and remembered objects

Other supplied narrative cycles use the Delicious Again Peter name for food shared with fighters or for a fantastical helper encountered in dreams. These are imaginative adaptations, not evidence of a culinary profession or a second real biography. They show how a creative identity can acquire different symbolic jobs as stories are retold.

The resin-art connection offers a less literal way to understand those adaptations: a familiar object can carry comfort, recognition or a memory of belonging. That is an interpretation of the source material, not a new power assigned to every sculpture. The main fictional era remains Year 3008 and the Final Fork remains unresolved; accounts announcing a completed universal victory do not close the current saga.

Making, gathering and responsibility

First Witness teaching on performance recognises gathering as a human practice, with attribution, participation and the possibility of propaganda all present. Read beside the fictional host, it asks whether people can speak and leave freely, not merely whether an event is exciting. A shared beat can create an opening without proving that everyone inside the room agrees.

The Keeper provides a parallel for the collector: caring for an object includes keeping its maker and history intelligible for the next person. These are comparisons through the fictional Covenant. They do not assign Peter Clark a doctrine, political belief or authorship of the First Witness.

Present creative context and rights

The real basis of this reference is the artist’s resin work and public creative identity. Crypto Moonboys supports both people building Moonboy character identities and people keeping the art, name or project they already have. SWARMSY supports both paths; GKniftyHEADS is the primary Moonboy foundation. An independent toy artist’s practice can be part of the surrounding culture without being reduced to a game reward.

Buying a physical figure, holding a digital collectible and obtaining rights to reproduce an artwork are separate matters. Qualifying unique 1/1 Moonboy character licences have a published scope and allow their holder to retain 100% of royalties from approved character use, with no royalty percentage taken by Graffiti Kings or Crypto Moonboys. Those terms do not automatically cover Peter Clark’s other work, its cultural references or another creator’s intellectual property.

Sources and remaining limits

This revision uses the actual W81 subject register, creator compilation’s Delicious Again Peter section and relevant dream/battle passages, together with the original Delicious Again Pete page and its legacy bible. The real-person register in the supplied archive corroborates the Peter Clark/resin-artist identity. The artist website is retained as the public source trail, without a fresh check of its stock, prices or current release schedule.

No new real co-founder claim, client relationship, licence, commission, biography or fictional event is introduced. Exact current availability, individual edition specifications and commercial rights remain release-specific. The independent maker, the wider cultural association and the fictional host are kept visible as three different subjects rather than merged into one invented career.
