Songs, Chants and Call-and-Response

First WitnessOral tradition · Section 43Last updated: October 2026

The Covenant was written to survive quotation. Music and call-and-response made it survive people who never read the whole text.

The Keeper Response

Caller: Who owns the thing entrusted?

Response: No hand owns the future.

Caller: What proves the keeper?

Response: What survives the keeping.

The Witness Response

Caller: What did you see?

Response: Only what I can answer for.

Caller: What do you not know?

Response: I will not fill the gap with fear.

The Wall Response

Caller: Who gets the last wall?

Response: No generation.

Caller: Who gets remembered?

Response: Whoever we refuse to erase.

The HODL Response

Caller: What do we hold?

Response: What must survive us.

Caller: For how long?

Response: Until another hand can carry it.

The Crownless Response

Caller: Who speaks the final word?

Response: Not the living for the unborn.

Caller: Who carries the fire?

Response: Whoever refuses the crown.

Street versions

Street crews shorten the responses until they become rhythm rather than teaching. “WHO HOLDS?” / “WE HOLD.” “WHO OWNS?” / “NO ONE.” “WALL?” / “KNOWS.”

Orthodox teachers complain the short versions lose nuance. Street teachers reply that nobody chants a footnote during a raid.

The danger of chanting

Book VIII schools warn that communal rhythm can make weak ideas feel certain. Several gatherings therefore include one deliberate broken response where the crowd is expected to remain silent.

The silence is meant to remind participants that unity should never eliminate the ability not to answer.
