Life on the Waterfront

Straight Outta Ghetto Bear wears the name on his own terms. The blue GK cap and grey camouflage shirt are part of a familiar figure on the walk between the open games and the old yards. He came to games through chalking the lines for other teams and found there was always somebody ready to claim the match started when they turned up.

The person who chalked the line

The old yard is where he first saw somebody get away with declaring the game open before the lines were down. He learnt to draw a challenging pitch on poor ground, then had to hand it over to people who had never looked at the wall where the supplies came from. Claiming a good first round for the people who had worked the ground got him invited to fewer official games. It also made him the person youngsters asked when they wanted to know who could use a square after hours.

He and the No Ball Games Mech still bicker while carrying things together. He admires the way Graff Bear redraws a shop price without losing the old shape of the letters. On his own he likes making a sign from memory and comparing it with the real one later. He always gets the width of the last letter wrong and can name every shop by how wrong it comes out.

Taking the gear back

He has a long-running argument with the No Ball Games Mech about who carries supplies back when rain stops play. The argument got serious after he left early and a groundsworker went home alone with two loads of gear to shift. He spent the next week arriving ahead of everyone and discovered how few people had even seen what it took to open the space.

Off duty

He likes redrawing worn shop signs by memory and comparing the missing details with the real thing. For a good game he asks whether somebody who missed the first round can still get ten minutes before everybody goes home.

Ten minutes for a stallholder

During the trial he carried the bag through the turnout and saw the last route narrowed to the width of his load. On the moved pitch he gave the opening round to the workers and let a stallholder leave in the middle without demanding she finish it. Hearing her talk about sorting ruined stock made him wish he had offered a few dry minutes indoors as well as the game. He stayed to carry the shared gear back while she counted her losses.

He wants the first round reserved for the grounds and closing crews at the next trial. A few people who used to play alongside him are likely to arrive late and expect their place. He has yet to decide whether he will do the explaining himself or let the sign carry it. Ask him when a game should begin and he will ask who’s been there since before the lines were drawn.

He sells weed around the yards and hears about it whenever he asks for a proper wage for marking a pitch. One of the old regulars tried to turn a debt into a claim on the first round. He told the man he could come back for the next one. The man took his ball home. Getting enough gear together for a second game is now part of the argument.
