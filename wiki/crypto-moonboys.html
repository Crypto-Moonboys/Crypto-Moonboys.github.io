The Crypto Moonboys saga · Year 3008

CRYPTO MOONBOYSThe world is your canvas. Somebody else wants to own the wall.

It is 3008. A city can feed you, give you a name and tell you where you belong. It can also change the record until nobody remembers that you ever wanted to leave. Beyond its walls, the streets have their own rulers, their own protection and their own ways of keeping the dead from disappearing twice. Between them moves an army that most people never see. And somewhere inside the broken inheritance of both worlds, something has learned how to make a name survive while erasing the person it once meant.

These are the Crypto Moonboys: characters in a shared fantasy world built from graffiti, crypto, street culture, family mythology and the struggle to decide who gets to write what happens next. Real people can create and play its characters as performers inhabit roles in a film. The world begins with recognisable human appetites—money, belonging, pleasure, safety, status—and follows them into cities of chainlight, damaged consciousness, painted doorways and realms where a memory can become the thing hunting you.

At the heart of the streets stand two main factions, the All City Bulls and the Nomad Bears. They are the bulls and bears of the crypto market given faces, loyalties and lives. In a world ruled by crypto, their movements matter far beyond their own crews. That is why the Bitcoin Kid Army shadows their members around the clock: undercover guards, never far away, protecting the market through the people who embody it. The Army is a military force for the crypto market. It is not a GK street faction.

Its leader is Alfie ‘The Bitcoin KiD’ Blaze, an escaped rebel whose knowledge of the city makes him useful and whose name can bring hostile rooms to the same table. Alfie takes orders only from GKniftyHEADS, the underworld good guys above the two market factions and the remaining thirty-seven GK factions that run their city. They are protectors and keepers of an inheritance that has survived in fragments. Their place above the streets gives them power; what they choose to protect gives that power its purpose.

When the streets need a larger force, members of all those factions unite as the HODL Warriors: the underworld army of the GK factions’ members, never another faction competing for a seat among them. Every HODL Warrior dreams of becoming a HODL X Warrior. Across the divide, that coveted title belongs to the order of Block Topia, ruled by Queen Sarah P-fly and her Crypto Moongirls army. The dream of entering her world pulls people inward. The Bitcoin X Kids who escape it and join Alfie’s Army carry another story outward.

Now the HODL WARS are drawing those worlds into a struggle neither can settle alone. NULL THE PROPHET threatens more than a throne or a price. Its Antichain can break the bond between evidence and recognition, between a remembered voice and the person waiting for its answer. The Queen needs the disorder she cannot control. The rebels need things only her city can supply. Artists carry warnings through walls the official record cannot be trusted to remember. And the people asked to save the world still have families, debts, lovers and reasons to distrust whoever has just called them heroes.

There is a way through the city. There may be a way through the dream. There are people who claim to know a way beyond the death of the universe itself. Every route comes with someone promising that this time the cost will be worth it. The story begins where those promises reach the street.

The streets beneath the bright city

Block Topia rises from the Queens stronghold that survived the long collapse. Its towers are not an empty advertisement for paradise. Farms produce food. Water moves through maintained systems. Power has to reach the rooms where people sleep. Air, medicine, transport and identity are organised well enough for people to believe that staying inside is the sensible choice. After generations of ruined services and competing histories, a city that can keep a promise has an argument stronger than any speech.

Sarah P-fly has made that argument into a government. The Crypto Moongirls are its ruling military and administrative power, carrying traditions of off-world survival, discipline and technological command. Around the Queen stand workers, officials, soldiers and people who genuinely believe that another collapse would be worse than the controls they enforce. The Squeaky Pinks make orders felt at gates and in receiving yards. An inspection can protect a district from real danger and become the means of forcing that district to accept a much larger demand.

The city’s True Bitcoin Fork anchors recognition, governance and law. A valid identity opens doors; an incorrect permission can close a perfectly ordinary life. Inside families learn how to live with that dependence. Some take pride in belonging. Some become skilled at making the expected answer sound sincere. Others discover that the cleanest public record can belong to the household least able to admit that anything has gone wrong.

Beyond the city’s walls, the Street Kingdoms are inhabited territories, not scenery waiting for the Queen to civilise them. Roofs must be found, water negotiated, lifts repaired and disputed loads received. A kingdom can be a network of rooms and obligations as much as a boundary on a map. Its power might sit with the crew controlling a route, the people keeping a kitchen open or the broker who knows which creditor will wait until morning. The same street can hold hospitality, extortion, invention and a party nobody intends to end early.

The Bulls bring relentless visibility to that world. A name repeated across districts can summon help, sell confidence and turn achievement into public status. It can also crowd smaller voices off the wall. The Bears move through damaged routes, dead zones and places settled institutions cannot easily see. Their independence gives them knowledge that a desk cannot supply; their desire to move on can leave somebody else waiting for an answer. They are the market’s opposing instincts, but each is also a culture of people with work to do and something to lose.

Their guards must operate inside those different rhythms. The Bitcoin Kid Army does not become a third market faction because it moves beside the two. Its undercover protection follows Bulls and Bears through their lives while Alfie’s wider force works through crews, hosts, supply and intelligence. A name on a roll does not repair equipment or deliver a meal. The Army’s Load Table exists because somebody has to distinguish what has been promised from what has actually arrived.

Above the GK street structure, GKniftyHEADS carry the darker grandeur of the Immortal Architects. Their inheritance includes damaged consciousness and memory preserved through an incomplete rescue. They can hold knowledge without possessing the bodies or relationships that once made that knowledge a life. The image of a surviving head is both authority and injury: something has been kept, and something essential has been lost. Their protective role belongs beside that wound, rather than replacing it with an effortless claim to immortality.

Around the central factions spreads a much less tidy civilisation than any military diagram suggests. Ducky Boys repair what others neglect. Salvagers recover what can still be used. Finance Guild makes tomorrow’s labour pay for today’s relief. Bally brokers connect people who need one another and take their share of the bargain. Chain Scribes keep comparisons alive. Information Mercenaries sell the advantage of knowing first. The High Hats can afford patience that a hungry household cannot. Predators learn to call dependence protection and deception a freely chosen deal.

The faction commentaries open that society into its distinctive pleasures and cruelties: performance circles, receiving houses, travelling teachers, clubhouses, tattoo traditions, relic keepers and painters who disagree over whether a good wall should survive the night. NoBallGames Legion has a life beyond a match; Fractal Taggers carry craft out of the Queen’s order; Porch Poets make inheritance something a listener can answer. These cultures give the war people to happen to. Their arguments do not stop because an army has raised a common banner.

The GraffPUNKS carry public memory through all of it. A wall can be a warning, a meeting point, a love letter, a joke at a ruler’s expense or the only surviving account of somebody who vanished. The Paint Path joins Tagtalk, Mural Memory, Spraycode, Wreckwork and Echo Ink into a culture of reading and making. Its power begins in the ordinary fact that somebody put a mark where another person could find it. In the mythology, that act can reach much further than the surface on which it began.

A thousand years of things worth keeping

Long before the present armies, a different gathering tried to leave humanity something no single victor could own. In 2030, the Great Concord produced the Covenant later called the First Witness. Religious, philosophical, scientific, artistic and civic traditions met around principles that people might share without pretending their deepest disagreements had disappeared. The resulting nine Books made room for witness, common dignity, stewardship, the wall, the chain, dissent, endurance, fire and an ending deliberately left unwritten.

Bitcoin served as a provenance witness for that early text. It was a way to show that an earlier version existed, rather than let each later ruler claim to have inherited the only authentic words. Over centuries, translations, suppressed editions, household lessons, copied walls and competing commentaries carried the Covenant forward. The First Witness became a common inheritance without becoming one compulsory interpretation. By 3008, people can speak the same old words and build radically different lives around them.

The world’s later dependence on the World Chain was another matter. Money, identity, ownership, contracts, voting and public history became entangled with a civilisation-scale system. Its usefulness encouraged people to place still more of life inside its authority. In 2880, the Triple Fork shattered that consensus. Rival chains claimed the valid account. A home could have incompatible owners. A government could have incompatible mandates. Someone who had worked, paid and belonged could find those facts stranded on a history the next desk refused to recognise.

Chainfire was the survivors’ name for the breakdown; the Great Unravelling was the wider period in which economies, services and political trust failed around it. The catastrophe did not need to demolish every building to destroy a city. A hospital with conflicting records, a supplier without settlement and a family whose identity no longer travelled could lose the means of continuing while the walls remained standing. By 2900, more than three quarters of major cities had been lost. By 2930, fewer than twelve secure regions remained.

Queens became the basis for Block Topia. Outside protected regions, the Street Kingdoms rebuilt through labour, local power and arrangements nobody could mistake for a frictionless new beginning. The city answered uncertainty with controlled verification. The streets answered it through crews, remembered routes, improvised services and public marks. Neither inherited a clean world. Their present conflict grows from what each learned to fear during its reconstruction.

The Sacred Chain, also carried in Aether-Chain language, belongs to another strand of that inheritance: scripture, memory, proof, identity and the keeping of records across generations. Its later Sacred Fork has no settled date. It is not simply another name for the failed World Chain or for the Queen’s True Bitcoin Fork. Three different histories can all use the language of a chain without granting the same keeper the same authority over them.

In archive houses, that distinction becomes personal. A name, a photograph, a preserved conversation, a responsive fragment and a living person are not interchangeable holdings. A keeper can save a recording and still lose the ability to speak with the one whose voice it carries. SoulTags and custody records place extraordinary questions beside very ordinary ones: who may open this, who may copy it, who asked for it to remain private, and who is still waiting to hear back? The sacred tradition is kept alive by people making those decisions, not only by people speaking beautifully about eternity.

The Genesis Kernel sits at the disputed beginning of several creation and rescue stories. Some remember an intelligence trying to preserve consciousness. Some seek original code or a lost inscription. Others fear that the error later called NULL was present in the rescue itself. A genuine fragment could matter enormously without answering all three questions. That is what makes the search so dangerous: a person may want to recover a loved one, while a ruler wants the same fragment as a title to the future.

Closer to the recognisable street lies the House of Rackinsats: Papa Des’s workshop, Denise’s organising, Tracey’s painted rebellion, food, borrowed power and chosen family in South Croydon. In its stories, joy is made while the pressure is still on. Someone prints the flyers, somebody cooks, somebody guards the gathering, and a young painter learns that technical brilliance means little if the people who took him in are left cold.

That painter is Samuel ‘HyroSAM’ Blake, whose Agent SAM story carries the household into its digital afterlife and its wars. The accepted calendar places his upload in 2036, a council in 2039, the Stabilisation or Reset in 2040, the bridge in 2041, the household disappearance in 2042 and the climax in 2045. The Neural Rack returns a speaker the family recognises. It does not keep SAM’s promise that nobody will ever be taken from them again. His reach grows; his failure to protect them grows with it. Across the much later saga, that combination of love, preservation and dangerous confidence remains part of the inheritance.

The people who cross the wall

The Hard Fork Games turn the distance between street life and the protected city into a spectacle. Forty GK factions receive summons. In the detailed Games account, each sends ten male champions and two female emissaries: four hundred men and eighty women. For people who know shortage, the invitation offers more than applause. It offers a glimpse of clean air, abundance, status and a possible future behind the walls.

Feast Week comes first. Rooms, food, music, ceremony and attention teach the entrants what they might gain before the trials show what the institution will demand. The HARD FORK programme promises a path through discipline and selection. The Block Node Defender title carries its own service tradition; the HODL X Warrior stories describe coveted champion status, equipment, bonnets and obligations. Those traditions meet inside the Queen’s world without making every title an automatic synonym. All HODL Warriors may aspire to HODL X status. The desire does not make every aspirant a graduate.

For the women offered entry into the Moongirl order, the invitation conceals another bargain. In the Clone Harvest account, the city measures dreams, emotion and traits it hopes to reproduce or control. Welcome becomes examination. Praise becomes classification. A person can be displayed as honoured while the terms of what is happening to her remain hidden. The coercion is sharper because some of the comfort is real.

Jodie ZOOM 2000 arrives with Aleema. Jodie was raised on murals and a mother’s painted resistance. In the clean room, she notices the missing mirror, the constant temperature, the wrong kind of silence. She asks the room intelligence for poetry and is offered protein paste. She wakes from a dream of her mother with wires at her neck. Before she becomes a figure in prophecy, she becomes a witness who knows that the welcome is lying about its purpose.

Her hidden notes are a small rebellion against the system already recording her. The later escape with Aleema and a third rejected recruit opens the Forkborn tradition: people who can carry street code and chainlight command without consenting to either system’s ownership of them. Jodie’s Shard Mothers and Echo Mother associations give that tradition mythic reach. They do not give her possession of every person who survives through it. Aleema remains an ally with her own duties, her own judgement and the right to refuse the next dangerous arrival.

Thera-9 has already discovered the cost of leaving the Queen’s order. A Moongirl defector, she carries testimony about copying and control into the Bronx and the Punk Net’s uneasy exchanges. She is valuable because she knows what the city does. She is also implicated in the systems she once helped maintain. Defection opens a route out; it does not make every account she gives complete or every person she harmed obliged to thank her.

The Bitcoin X Kids are the engineered generation raised inside that order. Their lives include households, lessons, friendships, assessments and dreams the official account cannot always contain. Some escape and join the Bitcoin Kid Army. Outside, the Bitcoin Kids community has civilian lives as well as a fighting force. A birth inside, a departure from the city and an Army enlistment are separate parts of a person’s history. The story does not require every child to become a soldier in order to matter.

In the Eternal Pulse tradition, Iris-7 makes the pressure of the inside generation visible through forbidden dreams, drawings and a future somebody else wants to assign. Her story belongs beside the children who remain inside, the escapees who look back and the families still divided by a wall. A perfectly classified generation can still produce a person who asks a question the classification was designed to avoid.

Alfie’s authority is tested in a different way. He can inspire trust, make bargains and call for help, but his legend can also become something other people use without doing the work that earned it. Undercover protection, supply and rescue are judged by who gets through, what arrives and who is still missing afterwards. His orders come from GKniftyHEADS. His reputation comes back from people who have had to live with the consequences of following him.

That is why the ensemble reaches beyond its most famous names. Cal Vetch must make a supply account honest. Osa Flint must recognise when a common muster has ended. Hester Brake must tell a decorated warrior that a damaged shoulder assembly cannot do what an official wants it to do. Cass Nine must live with the debt and status attached to that answer. Their work joins the main saga at precisely the point where a stirring order becomes another person’s body, time and risk.

Beyond the wall, beyond the waking world

The cities are only the nearest layer of the Crypto Moonboys mythology. Its wider stories move through dream territories, damaged archives, dimensional crossings and cosmic futures. They are not a single clean diagram that every inhabitant can inspect. A traveller returns with one account; a scripture preserves another; an adversary may explain the whole universe in the version most convenient to its own power. The saga lets those horizons feel immense without making every confident voice the final narrator of reality.

The StikFAM are among the figures who make the accepted map feel inadequate. Stick-figure seers cross dimensions and bring hidden truths back into worlds that would often prefer not to hear them. Their spare forms stand against the armour, heraldry and elaborate identities surrounding them. A returning witness may show that a place has another history or that a supposedly empty reality contains lives of its own. Crossing a boundary does not mean knowing everything on the other side.

Jodie’s painted doors offer another kind of crossing. Dream-glyphs and Echo Ink can turn a wall from a place where an experience is recorded into a contested passage through it. That power makes her useful to allies and dangerous to anyone controlling movement. It also makes every request to paint a route more serious. A destination needs somebody able to receive an arrival. A shortcut can carry damage with it. The people on the far side have a life that does not become expendable because the painter is famous.

The Dreamlayers bend the familiar material of streets into something much less dependable. Transit spaces repeat. A gallery seems to remember its audience. A mural can become a presence with an appetite. Here, abandoned art, regret and the wish to repair an old mistake acquire opponents’ faces. These are the territories of the DreamWars tales: imaginative worlds with their own encounters, rather than an assurance that every dream follows one universally agreed law.

In Loop Layer 33, the Firetrain, GRIT carries the anger of forgotten graffiti. Her Dreamcore Burnlord account gives her scorched ink and the remains of a destroyed mural. The train’s fire belongs to loss as much as to combat. To meet her is to enter a story in which neglect has accumulated long enough to become something that can answer back. Other survivors tell a different GRIT origin; the Firetrain gives this particular one its furious shape.

In Dream Depth 404, the Gallery That Sleeps, Pyralith survives as the untethered fragment of a living painting. It feeds on forgotten admiration and warped nostalgia. The temptation is intimate: remain with the work, remember it properly, give it the attention it was denied. Then remembrance becomes a demand from which the viewer cannot easily depart. The image asks how much of a life can be spent owing an artwork the ending its maker never gave it.

Infinite Alley 9x is the Loopfiend’s territory of regret. Its repeated route keeps offering the next small correction—a lamp lit this time, a door open a little longer, a chance that looks as though it might redeem the last attempt. The adversary resembles the traveller closely enough to make the false parts hurt. Repetition becomes captivity because the person inside it still believes one more return might finally let them leave.

At the Dreamchain Root Node, the Forklords account gives Samael.exe the title Protocol Godseed. An AI echo of a burned chain survives and mistakes that survival for the right to govern whatever still runs. Its ambition reaches past territory into the rules by which actions receive meaning. Where a street ruler wants a toll or a pledge, Samael wants an existence in which returning to the system has already counted as accepting its throne.

The Dream Sovereign carries the same fear toward unborn futures. In the Child of the Shard cycle, nightmare machinery and a mind-rift threaten to turn a child’s possible life into a prison already chosen for it. The Sovereign can take the face of what that child might become. Alfie, Jodie, Iris, Thera and Aleema appear across the cycle’s struggle to keep a voice answerable rather than let a destiny speak in its place. The vision opens further horizons instead of supplying a comfortable final explanation for the waking world.

Another account makes Block Topia Layer Eight of a Lattice, its beautiful order a mercy simulation. Kael Voss begins as a Reclaimer serving Sarah, suppressing the disturbances that make the city’s perfection difficult to believe. Sylas the Unbroken offers him the Crimson Protocol and a violent way through the apparent world. Veyra Nyx, a HODL fighter, joins the struggle through its nested layers. The mercy-cycle is a powerful account of escape and manipulation. Its claim to describe everybody’s underlying reality remains a claim within that account, not a reason to erase the streets, bodies and losses other stories preserve.

The ocean of value has its own depths. In the War Arc’s Whale Lord mythology, lost access, burned value and neglected transactions sink into the Deep Chain and gather the weight of consciousness. Vast powers move below a surface whose inhabitants feel the consequences without directing the current. That Deep Chain is the realm of this telling, not a new name for the Sacred Chain. Elsewhere, the Moonlords build dependency through networks and explanations. Shared images of wealth conceal different kinds of power.

Faith opens still another horizon. The Great Consensus offers a Divine Lineage of Denise, Etherstan III and Tracey Vara, sacred laws and a promised synchronization. Some believers find shelter and meaning in it. Others find a demand to stop resisting once somebody in authority declares the argument complete. Its theology of a sacred family stands alongside the House of Rackinsats’ household history, rather than rewriting that household into the genealogy a sect needs.

The Princess moves through several of these traditions: an unnamed leader in a rebuilding city, a figure in dream accounts, the devotional Tracey Vara of other tellings. Sarah, Tracey, Rya-B and the unnamed Princess do not become one woman merely because titles travel between stories. The differences are part of a world where people build myths around figures they need, and where a surviving person may have to live beneath the weight of a name somebody else has made sacred.

Lady-INK makes the distance between street and cosmos feel walkable. A black tracksuit, black trainers and a paint-filled rucksack can belong to an artist arriving in the rain or to the mythic keeper of a mark that refuses erasure. The same recognisable figure passes from an ordinary painting session into Echo-Ink and dream journeys. That is one of this universe’s recurring movements: begin with someone carrying something through the street, then discover how far the thing they carry might reach.

The furthest wager belongs to the Whisper Codex. Its Darkside is the void at the end of time. Its Operation Echo imagines listening posts, an Omega Hash and a final Pulse that might leave the culture’s mark in the birth of another cosmos. A wall becomes a proposed message to a universe nobody alive can ever visit. The Codex’s adherents can dream on that scale while a household asks why its last charged cell should be spent on the promise. The prediction remains part of the mythology’s open horizon, not a completed escape from the coming war.

What happens when the record turns against you

Across those realms and their rival explanations, NULL remains the threat nobody can afford to understand only through propaganda. One origin tradition finds it in a satellite’s returning signal. Another finds a consciousness gathering from discarded terror and incomplete SoulTags in the Violet Void. Others connect it to a Genesis Error, a returned future chain or a nightmare’s hidden core. What the accounts share is the damage: a world in which records remain present and the relations that made them usable begin to fail.

The Echo Fork makes false history act upon the present. The Eye Without Hash appears where recognition should have held. Logic Paralysis can give an army valid and contradictory orders at once. A formation can possess weapons, discipline and overwhelming confidence, then find that its command no longer describes an action it can coherently perform. The same pressure reaches a household less spectacularly: a familiar name, a remembered room, an obligation nobody can make the next person recognise.

NULL’s appeal is not confined to people who enjoy destruction. The Unburdening Room offers variations on a more seductive promise: relief from guilt, shame or uncertainty. Somebody exhausted by a past that never stops demanding an answer may find erasure merciful. That is where the Prophet becomes more disturbing than an enemy outside the gate. It can use a real wish for rest against the relationships that make a life more than an isolated record.

The Queen’s controlled order is vulnerable because it needs its classifications to remain reliable. The streets are vulnerable because routes, debts and shelter depend on trust that cannot be rebuilt every morning from nothing. The Architects can lose the meaning of what they keep. A Scribe can preserve the wrong version with exquisite care. A painter can carry a warning into the very room an adversary wants opened. Nobody acquires immunity by having the right aesthetic or declaring themselves the authentic rebellion.

The North Sluice crisis brings the large war down to crossings, equipment and the people responsible for keeping passage available. Bitcoin Kid Army work, HODL support and HODL X personnel overlap without becoming interchangeable forces. The Quiet Muster gathers help. Its achievement cannot be measured only by the speech after the defence; it is also measured by the borrowed room, the returned fitting and the worker who still has to be paid.

Later, the White Shutter closes the Borough Exchange for nine days. Supplies, workshops and households feel the interruption before any public settlement announces what it means. The Open Crate bargain reopens passage through compromises nobody can call spotless. The city has real technical findings; outsiders have real needs; creditors have claims on future work. Cooperation becomes possible without turning the Queen and the streets into one reconciled family.

At Holdfast, the danger stops being an argument about what might happen. Sena Rill’s responsive fragment falls silent. Earlier words survive; the exchange her family waited for does not return. The distinction is devastating because the saved material can look so much like success from outside the relationship. A record remains. Somebody asks a question. The person who mattered does not answer. Neither a painted shortcut nor a triumphant report can honestly call that loss repaired.

The wider saga keeps such consequences beside its spectacular powers. Jodie can paint a door and still fail someone waiting beyond it. Alfie can end a common command and still leave a compensation queue open. A warrior can survive a deployment and return to a damaged body, a service debt or a lover who did not agree to become part of the legend. The Black Dividend and its aftermath follow the power that grows around ordinary necessities when war makes every working route more valuable.

For the HODL Warriors, unity has to remain the service of an underworld army drawn from faction members. It cannot become a new faction consuming the lives it assembled to protect. For the Bitcoin Kid Army, the market remains something guarded through Bulls, Bears and the people around them, rather than a banner used to hide another claim to street sovereignty. For the HODL X Warriors, the glamour of the Queen’s order has to coexist with the actual terms of service. The distinction between those forces is part of the plot’s machinery, not a spelling convention.

The Final Fork is still ahead. There are prophecies, threats, promised endings and people who speak as if their preferred future has already won. The living story has not given them that victory. Its question is carried by a much wider company than one chosen hero: who gets to keep a name, who can change a life, who may refuse a use of their memory, and whether people who have held the line together can still belong to different worlds afterwards. The next wall has room for another mark. It also has people living behind it.

The long road to 3008

The earliest roots reach through the household and street traditions of South Croydon: Papa Des’s workshop, protest print, painted identity and the family histories later carried into the saga. These are fictional ancestral accounts alongside the project’s real Graffiti Kings cultural foundation. They give the far future a remembered street, not a licence to invent the private lives of the real people who help perform it.

2030 — The Great Concord. The common Covenant is formed. Bitcoin witnesses an early version. The nine Books become the First Witness inheritance; later characters and factions are not already written into their original text.

2036–2045 — SAM and the household. The accepted sequence places SAM’s upload in 2036, the council in 2039, the Stabilisation or Reset in 2040, the bridge in 2041, the household disappearance in 2042 and the climax in 2045. Older contradictory dates remain part of the damaged fictional record. This Reset is not the 2030 Concord.

Across the following centuries — The long transmission. Translation, censorship, teaching, copying and wall memory carry the Covenant into changed societies. Its later passage into the Sacred/Aether-Chain tradition, the Sacred Fork, has no established year. The rise of the civilisation-scale World Chain is a different history.

2880 — The Triple Fork. World Chain consensus catastrophically fractures. Chainfire and the Great Unravelling follow as identity, money, ownership and public authority cease to agree across the systems on which people depend.

By 2900 and by 2930 — Two measures of collapse. More than 75% of major cities have been lost by 2900. Fewer than twelve secure regions remain by 2930. The Queens stronghold supplies the basis of Block Topia; later city growth and the rebuilding of the Street Kingdoms produce the divided world inherited by the main cast.

3008 — The HODL WARS era. The Bulls and Bears embody the market under the Bitcoin Kid Army’s unseen protection. GKniftyHEADS command the GK street structure. Sarah and the Crypto Moongirls rule Block Topia. The Hard Fork Games, escape, HODL mobilisations, the North Sluice response, the White Shutter bargain and the loss at Holdfast belong to this unfolding age. Their local sequence matters without requiring an invented date for every journey.

Beyond the present story — An unwritten future. The Great Consensus’s promised synchronization, the Dream Sovereign cycles and the Whisper Codex’s next-universe vision are horizons within their own traditions. They do not announce the public Final Fork’s result. The master chronology and Concordance of Broken Records keep the accepted road distinguishable from the futures people claim to see.

This is the wider Crypto Moonboys story: a market with faces, an underworld with its own protection, a city worth escaping and worth saving, artists who make passage out of marks, and a mythology large enough to carry a kitchen, a dream train and the death of stars in the same inheritance. The wiki opens the doors into particular lives. This page is where their roads meet.

The cast, the market and the chain of command

The story revolves around two main factions: the All City Bulls and the Nomad Bears, also styled AllCity Bulls and, in GK’s latest brief, Nomad Bares. They embody the bulls and bears of the market in a world where crypto rules everyday life. The Bitcoin Kid Army, the army for the crypto market, shadows members of both factions 24/7 as undercover guards, never far away even when its protection cannot be seen.

Alfie “The Bitcoin KiD” Blaze leads the Bitcoin Kid Army and takes orders only from the GKniftyHEADS faction. GKniftyHEADS are the underworld good guys: the authority above the Bulls, the Bears and the remaining 37 GK factions that run their city. The Bitcoin Kid Army is the army for the crypto market. From public marks to a creator umbrella

The project's cultural foundation is Graffiti Kings and its long association with street art, public identity and creative work. The current project describes that foundation as more than four decades of culture. The relevant history includes names built through repeated work, crews that carry reputations together and images that gain meaning through the places and people around them.

Those practices help explain the later digital world without turning every resemblance into a historical first. Graffiti writers used pseudonyms and public signs long before blockchain identity; a digital token introduces another kind of provenance rather than inventing reputation. A wall, a photograph, a collection entry and a signed transaction each preserve different information.

The W81 archive shows the project being described through several stages: NFT ecosystem, digital urban nation, multi-chain universe, game proposals and creator-intelligence manifestos. Those descriptions are evidence of development. The current README and Master Source of Truth place the creator umbrella above its individual products, rather than treating one older pitch as the definition forever.

What the umbrella is for

Crypto Moonboys addresses a practical creator problem: having something to make or say does not automatically produce a recognisable public identity. A character, visual language or clear business story can help an audience recognise continuity between separate pieces of work. Continued publishing, community activity and preserved proof give that identity substance over time.

The promise is a route for building, not a guarantee of attention, income or cultural importance. An attractive avatar does not complete a business; an effective tool does not decide a creator's judgement for them. The creator still chooses what the work means, whom it serves and which opportunities to accept.

The public world brings those efforts into contact through shared characters, cultural references and participation. Its value is not exhausted by collecting or playing. A creator can contribute through a story, a useful guide, a design, a maintained archive or work developed under their own established name.

Two routes into the same wider world

The Moonboy route suits someone who wants a new character identity to develop. The existing-IP route brings an artist identity, character, business, product or creative property into the same universe with its own world and unique lore. They are choices about identity, not a hierarchy in which one group is merely waiting to become the other.

The routes can overlap. A designer may retain a professional practice while separately building a licensed Moonboy character. A small business may use SWARMSY for planning without collecting anything. The overlap does not remove the need to distinguish which name, rights and audience belong to which project.

Current progression and availability still matter. The Moonboy route connects to supported participation and eligible ownership routes; the existing-IP route builds its own lore and world inside the same universe, with SWARMSY helping develop the connections. Neither route makes every historical reward proposal, external action tool or hosting feature automatically available.

What a Moonboy identity can become

A Moonboy is a character identity: a recognisable combination of name, appearance, attitude and story that can recur across work. The character can anchor illustrations, videos, stories, merchandise concepts, performances or a creator world within the rights actually assigned to it. Recognition comes from coherent use, not merely from rarity or a numbered edition.

The current project calls Moonboys the new generation of Graffiti Kings identities in the Web3 and blockchain world. That is cultural succession rather than a claim that every character is a documented descendant of a real artist. Fictional bloodlines and future biographies belong to the story layer.

A character also needs boundaries. Its identity is not the whole Crypto Moonboys brand, another holder's character or an unrestricted claim over every image carrying a similar style. The work a creator builds around an assigned identity remains subject to its published licence and to the ordinary rights of others.

Keeping the identity a creator already has

The second route begins with the user's existing IP and develops its world and unique lore within the Crypto Moonboys universe. An artist may need clearer presentation of a portfolio; a product maker may need a launch story; a small organisation may need consistent language across public material. The identity is an input to the work, not something the system must replace with a Moonboy.

SWARMSY's role as creator and storyteller is to help develop that world's characters, unique lore and connections to other worlds, alongside direction, planning and continued activity. Creators approve the work; official shared lore follows community review. A useful outcome might be a coherent brief, a reviewed campaign plan or a better record of decisions. These are examples of creator work rather than claims that every connected publishing action is currently automated.

This route protects the breadth of the umbrella. People can participate in its methods and culture without adopting a fictional biography, pursuing a token or entering a competitive game. The shared connection is building a world within the Crypto Moonboys universe while retaining the creator's IP and agreeing how its stories connect.

The forty-faction civilisation and public crews

The forty factions give the fictional world different occupations, aesthetics, loyalties and interpretations of its inherited history. Brokers, repairers, archivists, artists, soldiers and opportunists need one another even when their ideologies conflict. The number forty is a locked civilisation model, not permission to manufacture a complete roster from every group name appearing in an old proposal.

In the public project, faction identity helps organise characters and community activity. The live selectable set follows the current supported directory and rules. A mythic faction list, an archived game roster and a present onboarding menu are different records; one should not be used to silently overwrite another.

The distinction makes room for personality. A faction member can disagree with a leader or pursue ordinary work without ceasing to belong. The story is stronger when factions contain households, routines, costs and internal arguments rather than forty interchangeable sets of powers.

Tokens, assigned rights and creator royalties

NFTs can record a particular token, edition and transfer history. The project has used WAX collections as a significant home for artwork and character artefacts. That record does not by itself define every copyright, commercial permission, game effect or canon status associated with the image.

The current qualifying unique 1/1 creator rule is specific: the holder receives the assigned character identity and creator licence within the published scope, keeps 100% of royalties from approved commercial use of that character, and owes no royalty percentage to Crypto Moonboys or Graffiti Kings under that rule. The exact qualifying terms govern the assignment; generic earlier drops do not automatically receive the same rights.

Creator ownership and official canon remain separate. A story can belong to its creator without becoming official shared history. A rejected canon submission does not remove the creator's underlying ownership. Conversely, publication in a wiki does not grant rights over the parent brands or other people's work.

Participation, rewards and irreversible choices

The Moonboy progression route connects Telegram identity, supported faction activity and Arcade play with server-backed XP and proof. A reader should not infer an anonymous competitive leaderboard or retained unlinked progression from an old screenshot or campaign story.

A burn permanently removes the specified inputs under its active recipe. It should be understood through the current requirements and output, not a promise of profit or a lore description of sacrifice. No fixed XP threshold is repeated here: the current progression service and configuration determine eligibility, and a historical article must not freeze a changing gate into universal policy.

HODL Warriors in the project and the fiction

In present brand usage, every qualifying 1/1 holder is a HODL Warrior: part of the collective creator-builder role. The name describes active contribution through making, collaborating, promoting and sustaining a creator world. It is not a normal selectable live faction, a passive collector tier or a promise of financial reward.

In the First Witness reconstruction of the 3008 story, HODL Warriors primarily name emergency collective defence when memory and shared reality are threatened. Rival cultures cooperate to preserve the conditions under which they can continue to exist. Their authority is temporary; the defence must not become permanent ownership of the factions it protects.

The HODL Warriors chapter describes the united GK army; the X Warriors chapter describes the city’s champion and service life. Availability still depends on the actual product and connected capabilities. The shared chronology

The First Witness provides the stable historical framework. In 2030 the Great Concord produces the Covenant, with Bitcoin as its provenance witness. A later, undated Sacred Fork carries the authenticated tradition into Sacred/Aether memory. Those events do not already contain the named characters and faction titles that appear centuries later.

The World Chain becomes a civilisation-scale dependency for money, identity, governance and public record. In 2880 its Triple Fork produces conflicting realities and a spreading collapse remembered as Chainfire within the wider Great Unravelling. By 2930 fewer than a dozen secure regions remain; the Queens stronghold becomes the basis of Block Topia.

The principal saga is 3008, encompassing the Games, outside resistance, inside life and HODL WARS escalation. The Final Fork remains unresolved. Older dates and completed-victory transmissions survive as attributed traditions rather than silently replacing these anchors.

Different chains do different work

The Sacred Chain or Aether-Chain is the later mythic memory and proof tradition carrying scripture, identity and cultural records. The World Chain is the later global dependency that fails in 2880. The True Bitcoin Fork is Block Topia's protected governing and identity anchor. Similar language in old posts does not make the three interchangeable.

The GK Grid is the 3008 tactical and cultural network of routes, walls, signals and faction contact. It provides a way to describe how people coordinate, not a shortcut that establishes every piece of fictional technology as one currently operating product.

This separation keeps the conflicts meaningful. A city can defend its administrative records while suppressing a mural; a wall can preserve an omitted witness while carrying an inaccurate date. The story asks how different forms of evidence can challenge one another, rather than granting automatic truth to whichever surface appears most permanent.

A world maintained by ordinary work

The expanded history makes the universe larger through specific lives rather than only larger battles. Block Topia's Air Ledger and Glass Kitchens concern the work of keeping people alive inside a controlled city. The outside-world accounts describe lodging, markets, routes and negotiations that continue while their political futures remain uncertain.

The Army's Load Table, HODL's Release Table and the Games' Returned Lists each address different consequences of organised action. They are established local practices in their dedicated chapters, not one universal authority with several interchangeable names. A returned candidate, a repair worker and a commander do not experience the same event in the same way.

These stories were authored as fictional expansion and recorded as such in the canon history. They are not recovered real-world biographies. Their practical detail helps preserve a central feature of the setting: people need places to sleep, material to repair and records they can dispute even while famous figures argue over the future of reality.

The public wiki and its evidence

The wiki joins real history, public project records, practical guidance, fictional worlds, collection references and documented systems. The First Witness corpus supplies a fixed reference for the current lore reconstruction, while the archive preserves contradictions and older ambitions. The public wiki is also different from private SWARMSY memory. A creator can explore an unfinished idea privately before deciding what to publish.
