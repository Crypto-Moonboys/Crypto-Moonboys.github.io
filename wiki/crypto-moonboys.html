Lore

The core story begins in 3008, after the collapse of the World Chain and the Great Unravelling. Reality splits into competing records, cities fall apart, and Block Topia rises in Queens as the last major chain-lit stronghold. Inside the walls, the Crypto Moongirls govern order through Queen Sarah P-fly, while the outside world survives through the street logic of graffiti, memory, and faction allegiance.

The world is split between control and resistance. Block Topia represents sterilized safety, ledger discipline, and chain governance. The Street Kingdoms and GraffPUNKS represent the archive that cannot be erased, using murals, tags, and coded walls as the real public record. That tension is the spine of the setting: clean order on one side, messy truth on the other.

The HARD FORK is the filtering system that turns that conflict into ritual. Men who survive the Games are pushed through tiers of training until the strongest become Block Node Defenders. The path is not framed as privilege; it is framed as survival, discipline, and selection inside a controlled world that treats people as inputs to the chain.

The bloodline layer matters because it defines the next generation. Elite defenders and Crypto Moongirls are paired through chain-compatible logic, producing the Bitcoin X Kids, who carry on-chain identity from birth. The lore uses that idea to show how deeply Block Topia tries to control family, memory, and continuity.

Jodie ZOOM and the Forkborn arc push the story toward fracture and recovery. Jodie discovers that the system is not as clean as it claims, that Moongirl leadership is compromised, and that street-coded signals can break open sealed timelines. The Forkborn then become the response to that discovery: a rebel order that does not seek to rule, but to remember, reroute, and rewrite what the chain tried to lock down.

That is the long-form lore in plain terms: the chain collapses, Block Topia hardens, the streets keep the truth alive, and the next generation decides whether the future belongs to control, memory, or a new fork entirely.

Real World Basis

Crypto Moonboys borrows from real graffiti culture, crypto identity, fandom-driven worldbuilding, and the broader NFT era, then turns those ideas into a fictional system about memory, ownership, and participation. The street tag becomes identity, the wall becomes a public record, and the chain becomes the layer the story argues with instead of worshipping.
