Language, Translation and Sacred Words

First WitnessTextual tradition · Section 72Last updated: October 2026

The Covenant survived because it could be translated. Translation also guaranteed that no language could preserve every shade of the original forever.

No sacred monopoly language

The First Witness has no single compulsory liturgical language. The earliest text is preserved for comparison, but communities are expected to understand what they recite rather than treat unfamiliar sounds as automatically holier.

Words that drifted

Witness, keeper, chain, fork and hold all changed meaning as technology and culture changed. Translation notes therefore record historical meanings beside current ones.

Untranslatable terms

Some languages preserve one Covenant idea with a single word while others need a sentence. Rather than force one “perfect” equivalent, serious editions keep a note explaining the loss.

The dangerous synonym

Political translators sometimes choose words that quietly shift duty into obedience, stewardship into ownership or witness into surveillance. Chain Scribes treat lexical choices as evidence of power.

Street translation

Street sayings are not literal translations. They are compressions. “NO SINGLE KEEPER” may carry the sense of an entire paragraph while losing conditions around it.

The translation rule

If one word decides the law, publish why that word was chosen.
