Rug Pull Wars

🌙 Community Lore 📅 Last updated: October 2026 📂 Lore

The Rug Pull Wars are the recurring struggles over betrayal and drained resources within the HODL WARS. Their battlefields include unfinished repairs, broken sponsorships and households whose shared reserves have vanished. The conflict continues after a deception is exposed, through the slower work of finding what remains, hearing incompatible claims and deciding who will carry the cost of rebuilding trust.

What the Rug Pull Wars name

The name comes from the image of a support being pulled away while people still depend on it. Within the fiction, a promised store, prize, shelter or recovery fund becomes a means of taking the resources entrusted to it. The injury includes what disappears and what the contributors can no longer do afterwards: finish repairs, return home, pay helpers or believe the next invitation.

The W81 subject register identifies a continuing conflict between Rugpull Miners, Liberators and Diamond Hands over drained resources. These are narrative alignments and antagonists, not a newly complete military roster. The wider HODL WARS concern territory, care, labour and memory as well as market imagery. Rug Pull Wars names one recurring struggle within that world, without turning every failed venture or unpaid promise into evidence of deliberate theft.

A war without one front

In 3008 the conflict crosses markets, Games travel, work yards and temporary alliances. It can begin where people have good reasons to cooperate. A household contributes spare capacity to a shared repair; a champion accepts help getting home; a workshop extends credit because someone it trusts speaks for a stranger. The same dependence that permits collective life makes betrayal expensive.

This is why the conflict cannot be understood solely through clever predators and gullible victims. A person may recognise uncertainty and still lack a safer way to meet an urgent need. A delayed part can ruin paid work. Waiting for perfect evidence can itself be costly. The factional struggle concerns who uses that pressure, who benefits from the resulting loss and who is left responsible for people whose resources have gone.

Famous losses and the problem of a neat story

The older page mixed fictional chronicles with named real financial scandals. This account uses the literary setting to examine consequences; inherited external references remain a reading trail rather than proof of fresh allegations, exact losses or legal outcomes. The famous stories inside the war are often incomplete too. A memorable name can survive after witnesses disagree about what it originally described.

The Rugpull Miners page follows the Empty Dividend and Bent Receipt hearing around the Winter Counter. These local episodes concern particular promises and disputed losses. They do not identify a mastermind behind every failure across the Grid. Their importance lies in the effort to preserve an obligation after a fashionable explanation has moved on. A chronicle that remembers only the vanished promoter gives the people left behind another disappearance.

The Unpaid Roof

One later local dispute is remembered as the Unpaid Roof. A group of lodgings outside the city pooled materials and work for a roof repair before a run of difficult weather. Their intermediary repeatedly assured them that the remaining supplies were already secured. When the delivery failed and the intermediary stopped answering, the participating households discovered that each had been given a different account of what the others had contributed.

The work had already opened part of the roof. They could not simply return to the conditions before the agreement. A temporary covering required more material, and the most exposed room belonged to a household that had supplied labour instead of stock. Its contribution was initially missing from the list used to divide the remaining help.

The incident was called a rug pull immediately. Whether every missing item had been deliberately taken was harder to establish. Keeping that uncertainty did not erase the broken assurances or the urgent need to cover the room. It prevented a satisfying accusation from becoming an excuse to stop counting the actual damage.

Leto Scrip and Anni Rafter

Leto Scrip had copied delivery promises for the roof organisers. He had treated copying as a small paid task, not an endorsement. After the failure, people remembered his familiar hand on the notices more readily than the name of the person who had dictated them. Anni Rafter, a repair worker whose household had contributed time, asked him to preserve the different versions together.

Leto first wanted to issue one clean correction. Anni resisted because a clean account could make the earlier contradictions disappear. They kept the cancelled versions beside the correction and noted which households had relied on each. This produced a disorderly bundle that was harder to display and more useful to people tracing a specific promise.

Neither became an investigator for the whole war. Leto still needed paid copying work; Anni still needed a usable roof. Their cooperation grew from the fact that each possessed something the other lacked: the surviving wording of the promise and knowledge of the work done in reliance on it.

The Three Stools meeting

The Three Stools meeting took its name from a room with too few seats. One stool was reserved for the person describing a loss, one for someone able to answer the account and one for a recorder. Everyone else stood or sat on the floor. The arrangement was an improvised response to crowding, not a new tribunal empowered to settle all claims.

When the first claimant described missing materials, the meeting moved quickly. When Anni described unpaid labour and an exposed room, several listeners wanted to return to the easier inventory. Leto recorded the interruption. It was the first time the meeting had to decide whether the act of discussing a loss could itself repeat the hierarchy that had hidden it.

The eventual record carried separate entries for stock, time, temporary shelter and unanswered questions. It ordered no universal compensation. Some material was returned from a store whose keeper accepted that it had been set aside for the repair. Other claims remained open. The room acquired more chairs before the next meeting, a modest expense that changed who could endure being heard.

Who gains and who answers

The Rugpull Miners are the most explicit predatory faction in the dedicated W81 account. They exploit ambition, affiliation and emergency, including the prestige surrounding the Hard Fork Games. The account’s fantasy of an ultimate Final Pull remains their ambition, not an accomplished founding of an invulnerable new chain.

The Blockchain Furies pursue fraudulent obligations and the damage to shared trust. Their scrutiny can help claimants, but a dramatic accusation is not the same as a completed return. Bally Boys financing, Finance Guild debt and AllCity Bulls competition create further pressures around a loss. A party that did not cause the betrayal can still profit from the desperate replacement borrowing it produces.

Liberator and Diamond Hands language supplies resistance banners. It does not make those who use it incapable of error. A respected faction can repeat a bad claim, protect an ally too quickly or demand patience from people who cannot afford it.

Recognising a claim without pretending to know everything

The older heading asked how to spot a rug. In the war’s lived setting, the harder task is to distinguish what is known from what urgency encourages people to assume. A recognised emblem does not explain who will answer for a promise. An anonymous contributor is not automatically dishonest; a famous name is not automatically reliable. A delay can have several causes, and a confident story can survive because nobody has time to check it.

At the Three Stools meetings, people are asked which promise affected their decision and what happened after they relied on it. This keeps attention on events instead of requiring a claimant to prove that they were an ideally cautious person. The practice offers no perfect filter and no protection from every future loss. It does make blame harder to redirect toward the person who arrived needing help.

The Anti-Rug Covenant

The Anti-Rug Covenant is a local HODL Wars tradition preserved by the earlier article: a pledge to question promises, preserve evidence and resist the use of collective trust as cover for predation. It is described as an addition to the Satoshi Scroll. That relationship is literary. The Covenant is not part of the historical Bitcoin whitepaper and must not be confused with the First Witness Covenant born of the Great Concord in 2030.

The Unpaid Roof participants add a practical interpretation. Someone presenting a promise should remain available for its consequences, and a correction should travel to those who received the original assurance. Neither rule makes every intermediary liable for every possible failure. The questions are what they claimed to know, what they omitted and what they do when their words have helped produce harm.

For Leto, the difficult part is continuing to answer after the meeting stops paying for his time.

Restitution and the work after exposure

Exposure attracts attention; restitution needs storage, transport, records and repeated decisions about incomplete supplies. A recovered bundle might contain several households’ contributions mixed together. Dividing it by the easiest visible measure can reward those who supplied objects over those who supplied work. Holding everything until certainty arrives can leave useful material idle while people remain exposed.

Anni argues for a limited release of roof covering with the disagreement attached to the record. Another claimant fears that anything released will never be counted again. The compromise provides enough for the most exposed room and leaves the remaining distribution unresolved. The room becomes usable before the dispute becomes tidy.

These decisions do not undo betrayal. They show why the aftermath belongs to the war itself. If all communal effort ends when a culprit is named, the burden of making life workable returns to the people who have already lost most.

The NGMI problem

The NGMI Chronicles can preserve warnings or turn suffering into entertainment. A story that labels a whole household foolish is easier to repeat than a careful account of deadlines, unreliable deliveries and limited alternatives. In the Unpaid Roof telling, Anni refuses permission for the exposed room to become the emblem on a triumphal warning poster. The organisers publish the corrected promises instead.

The meeting also needs someone to watch children, carry water and explain the discussion to an absent neighbour. These jobs are easily treated as voluntary because they do not resemble testimony. Anni asks that the time appear beside the other work. She is not demanding that grief become an invoice. She wants the gathering to recognise the people whose effort permits a public account to exist. The first list misses a carer who left early to prepare a meal. Leto adds the name with the correction date, leaving the omission visible.

The refusal does not silence the history. It changes what the audience is invited to look at. The person who relied on the promise is no longer offered as the main spectacle. Responsibility moves toward the assurance, its circulation and the decisions that followed it.

WAGMI language can help people stay together through this work, but it can also demand cheerfulness before there is anything to celebrate. A household may accept assistance while refusing a slogan. That refusal does not make it an enemy of the community.

Chronology and the wider war

The 2030 Concord, 2880 World Chain Triple Fork and contraction to fewer than twelve secure regions by 2930 form the older background. Queens became the basis of Block Topia. The Unpaid Roof and Three Stools are local episodes placed within the unsettled 3008 world, without assigning a new global campaign date or rewriting the established North Sluice sequence.

Sacred or Aether-Chain, World Chain and True Bitcoin Fork remain distinct. The outside Bitcoin Kids and inside X Kids have different lives; neither generation exists merely as an example of credulity. HODL collective defence also remains distinct from the HODL X champion tradition. A fraud dispute can touch any of these communities without collapsing them into one organisation.

The Echo Fork introduces further uncertainty because damaged meaning and records can resemble ordinary deception. Invoking NULL does not establish a cause. The Final Fork remains unresolved, and no completed victory over all predation is claimed here.
