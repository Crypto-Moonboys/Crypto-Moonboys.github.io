Education, Schools and Apprenticeship

First WitnessEducation tradition · Section 55Last updated: October 2026

The First Witness treats education as transmission plus correction: teach enough for the next person to continue the work, and enough doubt for them to improve it.

The Four Competencies

Many schools organise basic education around four competencies: read the record, test the claim, make something useful, explain what you changed.

The formula produces very different curricula in different factions but keeps a recognisable educational spine.

Learning by maintaining

Students are often required to maintain an existing system before designing a new one. Repairing old code, cataloguing archives, restoring tools, tending public space or documenting somebody else's work teaches constraints that blank-page creation hides.

The strongest opposing argument

Older students are taught to state the strongest version of a position they reject. Schools that cannot teach an opposing argument fairly are accused of producing loyalists rather than thinkers.

Apprenticeship

Apprenticeship remains important in craft-heavy factions because knowledge exists in hands as well as books. The apprentice should eventually be permitted to alter the method. A master who demands permanent imitation has confused lineage with ownership.

Testing teachers

Students are allowed to ask what evidence would change a teacher's view. Teachers who answer “nothing” may still teach history or technique, but their statements are marked as commitment rather than open inquiry.

The examination problem

Scores are useful for measuring some abilities. They become dangerous when educational institutions start treating a score as the student. Book V language is frequently used against systems that make one exam the gate to an entire life.
