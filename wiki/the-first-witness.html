The First Witness

Convergence Canon Sacred Text Project Last updated: October 2026 Category: Lore

One scripture. Forty paths. No single keeper. The First Witness is the working name for the ancient covenant that the people of the Year 3008 world commonly treat as their Bible. Its oldest accepted form is traced to 2030, when representatives of many religious, philosophical, scientific, artistic and civic traditions attempted to record a shared human covenant without placing it under one church, state, ruler or institution.

What This Page Is

This is the master reading hub for the First Witness project. It separates four things that later archives often mix together: the original 2030 scripture, the cryptographic record that proves an early version existed, the centuries of transmission that changed how people understood it, and the later faction commentaries that tried to make the same words serve very different worlds.

The existing Crypto Moonboys archive contains conflicting dates, duplicated names, faction propaganda, old campaign copy, mythic retellings, incomplete chronologies and later historians attempting to reconstruct events they did not witness. Those contradictions are not being deleted. They are being treated as part of the history.

The First Witness project is therefore not a clean rewrite of the old lore. It is the place where those broken records are compared, weighed and connected.

The 2030 Covenant

The central tradition places the first completed Covenant in 2030. The event later becomes known by several names, including the Great Concord, the Human Covenant and the First Witness. The dedicated Great Concord chapter records the 2030 gathering and its place in the accepted chronology.

The Concord did not require older religions to declare themselves false. Instead, it attempted something narrower and harder: identify the principles that had to remain human property even when people disagreed about God, metaphysics, nation, law, culture and political authority.

The resulting text was deliberately designed without a supreme priest, hereditary keeper, central headquarters or single authorised nation. Later traditions condensed this principle into a warning found across faction culture:

No single keeper.

Bitcoin As The First Witness

The earliest authenticated version of the Covenant is said to have been cryptographically committed to Bitcoin. Later generations often described this as the Bible being “minted on Bitcoin,” although surviving technical accounts disagree about the exact mechanism.

The important distinction is that Bitcoin was not worshipped as a god and did not become the religion itself. It served as a witness: a public way to prove that a specific form of the text existed at a specific point in history and had not simply been rewritten later by a victorious authority.

The Bitcoin Witness page separates the mythic language of “minting” from the provenance principle and the technical details that remain disputed.

From The First Chain To The Sacred Chain

At a later point, the authenticated covenant was carried, mirrored or forked onto what later traditions call the Sacred Chain or Aether-Chain. The exact year remains unresolved in the current master chronology.

The working distinction is simple: the old Bitcoin witness proves provenance; the Sacred Chain carries living continuity. That difference becomes crucial after later civilisations place too much of human identity, law, memory and ownership into universal chain systems.

The First Witness distinguishes the Sacred Chain, World Chain and Block Topia's later True Bitcoin Fork despite the overlapping religious language in older records. The Concordance records their different roles and the relationships that remain unresolved.

The Nine Books

The canonical scripture consists of nine Books written as a text created in 2030. They do not directly name Block Topia, NULL, Queen Sarah P-fly, the Triple Fork Event, the Hard Fork Games, Alfie Blaze or any Year 3008 faction. Those connections belong to later history and commentary.

  1. Book I — The First Witness: truth, testimony, memory, corruption of records and the duty to leave verifiable witness.
  2. Book II — The Common Blood: human dignity, difference, family, stranger, enemy, mercy and the limits of inherited status.
  3. Book III — The Keeper: stewardship, custody, responsibility and the danger of treating temporary power as permanent ownership.
  4. Book IV — The Wall: creation, public mark, identity, expression and the human need to leave evidence that a life existed.
  5. Book V — The Chain: continuity between generations, distributed witness, technology and the danger of allowing one record to become the owner of truth.
  6. Book VI — The Fork: dissent, conscience, schism, corrupt authority and the boundary between necessary refusal and destructive fragmentation.
  7. Book VII — The Hold: endurance, sacrifice, loyalty, preservation and the ancient language later transformed into HODL doctrine.
  8. Book VIII — The Fire: warnings and prophetic images deliberately ambiguous enough to be argued over for centuries.
  9. Book IX — The Unwritten Block: an intentionally unfinished ending: no generation inherits the right to write the final word for every generation after it.

One Text, Forty Paths

The 40 GK factions do not begin in 2030 as forty ready-made religious denominations. They emerge much later from different survival cultures, political systems, professions, crews, economies, rebellions and interpretations of the same inherited Covenant.

One tradition may read stewardship as the protection of records. Another reads it as water, food or repair. Another as freedom from central rule. Another as disciplined order. Another as the preservation of art. Another as settlement of debt. Another as protection through secrecy. Another may distort the text deliberately to justify power.

That is the decentralised core: agreement on a text does not produce identical behaviour. Every faction must reveal what it protects, what it fears, which passages it emphasises and where its own interpretation becomes self-serving.

The Archives Are Witnesses, Not Perfect Truth

Existing wiki pages remain important. A page that gets a date wrong may still preserve a true name, ritual, route, fear, relationship or faction belief. A propaganda account may still reveal what the propagandist needed people to believe. A myth may preserve cultural truth even when the literal event cannot be verified.

First Witness sections distinguish witnessed and probable claims from faction accounts, late traditions, propaganda, mythic memory, disputed claims and unknowns. The Concordance makes conflicts between older records visible rather than silently erasing them.

The Year 3008 Reading

By 3008, the old Covenant has survived far beyond the world that wrote it. The Triple Fork, competing records, the rise of Block Topia, the Street Kingdoms, chain control and the spread of NULL THE PROPHET turn ancient arguments about memory into matters of survival.

Alfie “The Bitcoin KiD” Blaze is not named in the 2030 scripture. Later factions connect him to Book VIII's imagery of fire, children, divided keepers and a signal capable of crossing faction lines. Some call this fulfilment. Others call it political myth-making. Some believe the prophecy describes a generation rather than one person.

That disagreement is intentional. A thousand-year-old text should create argument, not remove it.

HODL Is Larger Than A Coin

The archive uses HODL Warriors and HODL X Warriors in overlapping and sometimes conflicting ways. This hub does not merge them.

The HODL Doctrine page traces how the Book of the Hold changes across centuries: from a moral command to preserve what must survive, into slang, faction language, creator identity, defence doctrine and other later meanings. The goal is to explain the naming drift historically rather than choose one archive page and delete the others.
