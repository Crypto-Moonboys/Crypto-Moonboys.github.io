Book VI — The Fork

First WitnessCanonical scripture · Book VILast updated: October 2026

Book VI treats separation as neither holy nor shameful. The question is what the split protects, what it destroys and whether return remains possible.

The text

1 Disagreement is not failure. A people unable to disagree without becoming enemies is already fragile.

2 Obedience can preserve peace and preserve abuse. Do not praise it without asking what it serves.

3 Refusal can preserve conscience and excuse selfishness. Do not praise it without asking what it costs others.

4 Before leaving a common rule, state the grievance where the ruled can hear it.

5 Before punishing refusal, answer the grievance where the ruled can inspect it.

6 When rulers forbid every peaceful correction, they teach the people that only rupture has force.

7 When rebels reject every common obligation, they build only smaller tyrannies.

8 A fork should carry the history it inherited, including the reasons for separation.

9 Do not rewrite the old record to make your departure appear inevitable.

10 Do not erase the old community to make the new community pure.

11 Keep bridges for trade, family, knowledge and return where possible.

12 Some divisions cannot be healed quickly. Do not force reunion merely to make a map look whole.

13 A truce is not surrender when it prevents a grief no victory can repair.

14 Where conscience is private, leave it private.

15 Where conscience commands harm to another, conscience enters public judgement.

16 No leader may claim that disagreement with the leader is disagreement with truth itself.

17 No rebel may claim that opposition to authority makes every rebel action righteous.

18 Ask what remains after the fork: food, water, law, records, children, graves, debts and neighbours.

19 Revolution that cannot maintain life soon becomes another argument about who gets to rule the ruins.

20 Sometimes the old structure must be broken. Break only what you are willing to help replace.

21 Sometimes the old structure can be repaired. Do not destroy repair merely because destruction is more dramatic.

22 Keep evidence from both sides.

23 Teach the next generation why the fork happened and what each side feared.

24 Leave open the possibility that your side was partly wrong.

25 Freedom without memory repeats its mistakes. Unity without dissent hides them.

Reading rule

This Book belongs to the 2030 Covenant layer. Later pages may interpret it through Year 3008 events, but those later names are not retroactively part of the original text.

The wording is kept direct because the tradition had to survive translation, quotation, song, argument and wall transmission rather than depend on one ornate liturgical language.
