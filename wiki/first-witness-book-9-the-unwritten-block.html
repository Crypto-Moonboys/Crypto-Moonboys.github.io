Book IX — The Unwritten Block

First WitnessCanonical scripture · Book IXLast updated: October 2026

Book IX ends the Covenant by refusing to end it. The text closes with obligations, not a final authority.

The text

1 You inherit a world already written upon.

2 Some marks are gifts. Some are wounds. Learn the difference before adding yours.

3 Do not curse the dead for failing to solve problems they could not see.

4 Do not worship the dead so completely that the living are forbidden to solve them.

5 Every generation receives evidence, debt, language, tools, mistakes and unfinished work.

6 You are a continuation, not a conclusion.

7 Write laws as though your descendants may discover reasons to change them.

8 Build archives as though your descendants may distrust you.

9 Build tools as though your descendants may use them for purposes you did not imagine.

10 Give them enough history to judge you and enough freedom not to imitate you.

11 The final block cannot be written by the dead.

12 It cannot be written by the living for those not yet born.

13 No council, prophet, machine, market, nation or church receives the last word on behalf of all future minds.

14 Add commentary without pretending commentary was present at the beginning.

15 Mark revisions.

16 Preserve discarded arguments where their loss would make the surviving answer look inevitable.

17 Let new evidence correct old confidence.

18 Let old warnings trouble new confidence.

19 Remember that decentralisation can distribute responsibility or distribute cowardice. Choose the first.

20 Remember that unity can coordinate protection or concentrate domination. Choose the first.

21 Your work will remain unfinished.

22 This is not a defect.

23 The open space is part of the covenant.

24 Leave it.

Reading rule

This Book belongs to the 2030 Covenant layer. Later pages may interpret it through Year 3008 events, but those later names are not retroactively part of the original text.

The wording is kept direct because the tradition had to survive translation, quotation, song, argument and wall transmission rather than depend on one ornate liturgical language.

Dust, seasons and unfinished work

The final Book carries one of humanity's oldest observations: generations arrive, work, build names, lose people, and pass. Kingdoms, platforms and movements speak as if they are permanent while time treats them as temporary tenants.

Some traditions called this dust. Some called it impermanence. Some described turning seasons, wheels, ages or the vanity of trying to hold what time will eventually move. The Unwritten Block does not choose one metaphysics. It keeps the practical conclusion: behave like a temporary keeper.

Real-life reading: leave instructions, not chains

Parents, founders and artists often say they are building a legacy when they are actually building a demand to be obeyed after death. Book IX draws the line sharply. Leave knowledge, tools, values and evidence. Do not attempt to control people you will never meet.

Write the will. Label the archive. Explain the passwords. Tell the family story. Record why the decision was made. Then leave enough blank space for somebody in fifty years to decide you were wrong.

A legacy that cannot be revised is only a ghost trying to govern the living. More appears in Life Readings.
