⚔️ The Squeaky Pinks

⚔️ Lore 📅 Last updated: October 2026 📂 Lore

The Squeaky Pinks enforce Block Topia’s order in Year 3008. Their conspicuous uniforms promise protection and warn of control; their local posts reveal the work, judgement and coercion hidden inside a claim of perfect compliance.

Adult lore — 21+: These fictional accounts include strong language, adult relationships, intoxication, coercion, violence and lasting loss.

The visible authority of Block Topia

The Squeaky Pinks are Block Topia's enforcement faction in the Year 3008 world. They patrol, inspect, restrain and carry the authority of the Theta Protocols into ordinary encounters. Their pink porcine forms deliberately make authority conspicuous. A person seeing them arrive is meant to know that the city has noticed.

The dedicated account places them under Queen Sarah P-fly's supreme command and describes their conviction that disorder is a moral failure. Their defence of predictable services and protected spaces can be valuable to residents. Their corruption is treating compliance as proof that a person is good and treating a rule's existence as proof that its enforcement is just.

The First Witness commentary frames them through Hold and Keeper: protection requires decisive action, but power must remain answerable for what it protects and whom it harms. The Enforcers name refers to the enforcement arm and service tradition of these same Pinks, not a second faction in the forty.

Derren Glaze and the polished snout

Captain Derren Glaze, 49, commands a small inside detail whose presence can make a service-quarter landing feel owned. He polishes his pink snout plate until residents can see a warped reflection in it. A white glove is his public flourish: he removes it before shaking a patron's hand, then makes an ordinary caller wait while he puts it back on. The gesture is small enough to deny and consistent enough that people remember it. He is a recognisable local officer, not a replacement for the Queen's command.

Glaze wants promotion, clean reports and evenings in rooms where nobody treats him as hired muscle. Ilva Rusk offers his detail an adult celebration booking with a service bonus that his superiors permit. He tells himself the arrangement rewards dependable work. Two subordinates know which homes receive slower attention after their residents quarrel with him. A third genuinely admires him for stopping an earlier assault. Glaze has done useful work and learned to spend that history as credit against later complaints.

Ressa Valeen's careful supervision remains part of the faction. Her existence does not turn Glaze into an anomaly nobody else will tolerate. Officers gossip, resent one another's favours, cover for tired colleagues and sometimes excuse a frightening action because the person responsible has been loyal to the unit. Residents encounter the Squeaky Pinks as that mixture of service and coercion. Their special place in the city is the power to make a decision immediately real: a door closed, a person removed, a route suddenly costly.

The Porcine Protocol

The Porcine Protocol in the dedicated account describes the faction's adoption of a caricature historically directed against police authority. The Pinks turn the insult into a chosen uniform of dominance: reinforced porcine forms, bright colour and a high, intrusive signature sound. The display says they will not hide their role to make it more palatable.

The uniform also allows the person inside it to disappear into the institution's image. Residents remember a pink presence where an officer remembers a particular instruction, a long shift and a failing piece of equipment. Those perspectives can explain one another without cancelling responsibility. An exhausted worker still has power over the person being questioned.

Source accounts name Code Cuffs, Neural Freeze, Theta-Shields and de-fragmentation procedures. Some claim impossible-to-evade detection or perfectly fixed effects. Those are the faction's feared equipment traditions, not universal guarantees. The life of an enforcement post is full of uncertainty that its most confident publicity leaves out.

Why the city calls it peace

The Pinks understand Block Topia as an achievement wrested from collapse. They point to reliable passages, maintained services and the absence of constant open faction conflict inside the walls. To an officer who has seen a route fail outside, regulation can look like the work that makes ordinary life possible.

Residents can depend on that work and still fear the institution performing it. A household may want a dangerous obstruction removed and object to the inspection that follows. A worker may report a genuine threat and hesitate because the resulting record could expose an unrelated problem. The Pinks' claim to protect the whole city does not mean everyone encounters protection on equal terms.

The strongest public challenge is therefore not that rules have no use. It is that people must be able to explain when a rule is producing the danger it was meant to prevent. A system that hears every objection as rebellion makes that explanation harder precisely when it is most needed.

A shift at Pinkline Post

Pinkline Post is a local enforcement office serving several inside routes. Its work includes ordinary reports, queue disputes, found belongings, service interruptions and complaints about earlier encounters. It has neither the reach of the Queen's command nor the freedom to treat every local judgement as a new city law.

Ressa Valeen supervises a shift there. Ressa values clear instructions because unclear ones can leave inexperienced officers improvising under pressure. She also knows that clarity can be mistaken for completeness. A rule that describes most cases will eventually meet a person whose situation does not fit the description.

Iven Measure works with return records and complaints. Visitors often arrive angry by the time they reach Iven's desk, having repeated an explanation elsewhere. Iven's patience is practical: a complaint that becomes intelligible may reveal a mistake the post is about to repeat. Ressa sometimes hears this as criticism of her staff. Iven answers that a post claiming to preserve order should want an accurate account of its own work.

The Amber Queue

The Amber Queue formed during a delayed review of permissions for a shared service route. Workers and residents received the same instruction to wait while the record was checked. Some could postpone their plans; others were carrying supplies or supporting somebody who could not remain comfortably in the waiting area.

Ressa initially defended the queue as equal treatment. Iven noticed that several people had left without resolution and were now recorded as having abandoned their requests. The entry made the system look less burdened while concealing the reason people could no longer wait. A household representative returned with witnesses and challenged that description.

The post corrected the affected entries and arranged a limited alternative waiting procedure within its remit. It did not settle the city's wider permission system or compensate every lost hour. Amber Queue remains a local example of how orderly processing can move a burden out of sight. The record had been tidy because it omitted the consequences of the rule.

The Unfastened Gate

During a local service failure, an ordinary gate was left open to allow people to leave an affected passage. The change solved an immediate problem and created a later dispute: the officer who permitted it had not completed the usual authorisation before acting. Ressa had to account for the departure from procedure to people who were not present during the failure.

The first report described only the unauthorised opening. Iven asked for the service condition, the people waiting and the time available for a decision to be recorded too. The fuller account did not make every judgement beyond criticism. It made it possible to judge the actual decision rather than a rule violation separated from its circumstances.

The case became known at Pinkline Post as the Unfastened Gate. Ressa used it in training alongside a different example where a casual exception had caused avoidable confusion. The lesson was not that initiative always wins. It was that authority should be able to explain why a decision was necessary and what followed from it.

Learning to wear authority

New Pinks learn reporting, shared work and the limits of their assigned duties as well as the faction's more intimidating symbols. At Pinkline Post, an experienced officer can be excellent at a difficult intervention and poor at explaining it afterwards. Ressa insists both skills matter. An unrecorded act of discretion can become impossible to distinguish from favouritism.

Iven asks trainees to read a complaint from the visitor's point of view before drafting a response. Some think the exercise assumes the officer was wrong. Iven explains that understanding an accusation is not agreeing with it. The same principle should apply when officers expect residents to understand an instruction they dislike.

The post's culture still rewards decisiveness more readily than patient correction. A quick resolution is visible; a problem prevented through careful listening is harder to count. Ressa has not solved that imbalance. Her own advancement came partly through the measures she now finds too narrow, and changing them risks making earlier success look less impressive.

The work behind a patrol

Pinkline Post depends on cleaning, repairs, meals, records and somebody checking that a tired colleague has actually gone off duty. Equipment taken for granted in a public encounter must be maintained somewhere. A change of shift can fail because one ordinary item has not been returned and nobody knows who accepted responsibility for it.

Ressa's staff sometimes volunteer longer hours when service is under pressure. The offer can be generous and can become a test nobody feels able to decline. Iven records the extra work because invisible overtime creates a false picture of what the post can sustain. A service that functions only through permanent exhaustion is less dependable than its reports suggest.

Residents' work sustains the institution too. They guide visitors, identify practical problems and explain local arrangements to officers who may have changed posts. The Pinks' uniform can conceal that dependence by making every successful outcome look like authority arriving from outside. A more honest account gives the people already present a place in the result.

The inside generation

The Pinks' monitoring of the Bitcoin X Kids is among the most troubling parts of the dedicated account. Anomalies and cultural drift are treated as threats to be reported for correction, memory mapping or purge. The rhetoric presents the institution as saving children from themselves, leaving little room for the subject's own interpretation.

The current inside history describes households, schools, care workers and young people whose lives cannot be reduced to compliance scores. An unusual response may be meaningful without being evidence of a dangerous contagion. Iven's local records work distinguishes what someone observed from what the observer concluded. That distinction cannot abolish the system's coercive powers, but it can prevent one unsupported judgement from masquerading as several independent facts.

Alfie's Bitcoin Kids are the escaped outside lineage. The Pinks' archive account describes efforts to recapture them. The split is not a universal life cycle in which every inside X Kid becomes an outside rebel. The faction's response to escape reveals its larger premise: leaving is treated as a failure of authority that authority must reverse.

Art, movement and unauthorised agreement

The GraffPUNKS are primary adversaries because their public marks challenge the city's control of space and memory. Nomad Bears become suspect through movement and proximity to damaged places. The Nice & Easy Bois are criticised when local truces appear to override formal commands. Each conflict brings the same question back to the Pinks: can something be useful without first being authorised by them?

The dedicated account also describes attempts to control High Hat enclaves and frustration with Rugpull Miners whose conduct does not fit straightforward enforcement categories. This asymmetry matters. An institution can be highly effective against visible disorder while struggling with well-resourced manipulation. Increasing pressure on the most visible people may make the statistics improve without addressing the more powerful harm.

Iven hears that complaint from residents who want both public safety and an explanation for uneven treatment. Ressa cannot answer by pointing to the uniform's good intentions. The post has to show what it did, why it stopped where it did and which limits came from authority above it.

The Games and the Feast Week strain

Hard Fork Games traffic brings extra inspection, arrivals, work and competing demands on the Pinks. The archive places them around the outer rings during Feast Week and in confrontations with black-market traders and disruptive interventions. The event's ceremonial order relies on labour that does not fit easily into its public celebration.

A delayed decision can reach far beyond the person at a desk. Equipment waits, a worker misses a shift, and a household does not hear when someone is expected home. Pinkline Post's experience of Amber Queue makes Iven wary of reports that count only the number of cases closed. A completed entry can hide a person who simply lacked the resources to keep asking.

The Games' other testimonies concern concealed evaluation and the Clone Harvest. Enforcement staff do not all possess the same knowledge or responsibility, but the institution's role cannot be erased by describing each worker as merely following a narrow instruction. An account of service must leave room to examine the system being served.

The crime without a statute

W53 and W65 describe NULL as a crisis the Pinks cannot resolve through ordinary rule enforcement. Their strongest response in those accounts is to apply existing rules more intensely, targeting visible anomalies while the underlying threat continues. The Eye Without Hash becomes a symbol of a problem their language of guilt cannot adequately describe.

At Pinkline Post, Ressa's fear is that hesitation will allow preventable harm. Iven's fear is that the institution will manufacture certainty because hesitation looks disloyal. Both concerns shape which records survive a difficult period. A cautious observation can be overwritten by a confident label long before anyone knows what caused the event.

The source's Ascension Space Program and Operation: HODL FORK imagine an orbital future under perfected control, pursued through Record Crates and Memory Arrays. They remain an ambition and campaign tradition. Neither the orbital vision nor collective HODL mobilisation establishes completed salvation. HODL Warriors and HODL X Warriors remain distinct; emergency cooperation does not make ordinary scrutiny disappear.

History and the wrong inherited portrait

The Pinks' origin account names rule-followers, auditors and enforcers who interpreted the collapse as a failure of sufficient control. That is faction mythology, not a verified genealogy. The present chronology retains the Great Concord in 2030, World Chain Triple Fork in 2880, Queens secure-region basis by 2930 and main saga in 3008. The older Sacred/Aether-Chain, World Chain and True Bitcoin Fork remain distinct.

The legacy wiki instead called the Pinks a GraffPUNK-aligned gang of decentralised resistance. That portrayal conflicts with the dedicated enforcement account and current First Witness framework. Its Chromatic Siege and secret pigment stories survive as older legends, but they do not define the faction's allegiance or establish the first use of visual effects in the world's warfare.

Pinkline Post offers only a local view of the larger institution. Ressa and Iven can make limited corrections while remaining part of a system capable of coercion and erasure. Their arguments neither absolve the faction nor make every individual identical. The unanswered question is how protection can become accountable when its own doctrine equates obedience with peace.

Fictional institution

The Squeaky Pinks are a fictional enforcement faction within the Crypto Moonboys creator universe. Their technologies, jurisdiction and officials belong to the Year 3008 setting. No real police agency, person or present service is identified by the new local characters and incidents.

A HODL FORK acquisition reaches Pinkline

The Citadel account calls Operation: HODL FORK a deep-routing acquisition effort for artefacts and record-like discs needed by the Space Programme. A local Pinkline shift encounters the programme through receiving instructions rather than a vision of everyone ascending. Ressa Valeen has to decide what the post can actually accept, how a claimed recovery is recorded and who will carry the object onward.

One returning crew brings a disc with readable delivery entries and an intermittent voice response. The crew calls it unclaimed integration material because no living owner accompanied it. Iven Measure records that absence as an unresolved relationship. The readable entries and the response are both present; neither establishes that the programme can use the holding as an experimental person. A recovery instruction has not answered the custody question it produced.

Ressa first asks for the disc to continue to the Ground Array Office so the next receiving team can decide. Hessa Line points out that such a handover would also pass on a purpose the post has not justified. They separate conservation from programme use and arrange a held comparison rather than the requested immediate transfer. The crew still receives credit for the object it brought back. It does not receive confirmation that the object is fit for integration.

The hold consumes a receiving place and another worker's time. A programme clerk complains that Pinkline is delaying scarce research material. A household asking about an unrelated item waits longer at the post. Iven preserves both the reason for the delay and the inconvenience it causes. Careful custody does not become harmless merely because it is preferable to the unsupported handover.

This shift supplies a local limit on the programme's recruitment language. Pinks can move crates, escort workers and enforce access within their accepted duties. Those acts cannot certify the contents as consenting travellers or demonstrate that the destination is safe. Their authority reaches far into a person's experience and still cannot make a proposition true by ordering it written on the label.

The landing Glaze keeps clean

During Split Current Night, Glaze treats the Bright Mouth entrance as the boundary between an authorised booking and an unauthorised gathering. The downstairs workers have legitimate reasons to be there. He keeps classifying them as a growing disturbance. When Nela Hask, 31, insists that her stored food is behind the protected landing, he orders her removed. She is hurt during the removal and loses carrying work. The party remains orderly enough for guests to praise the detail's professionalism.

An officer laughs that Nessa should have learned to ask more quietly. Another objects after the shift, with the door shut. Glaze calls the injury regrettable and the removal necessary. He accepts the agreed service bonus. A report records a fall during removal; it does not produce an immediate payment or undo the allocation. Ressa refuses a request to describe the encounter as an uncomplicated successful intervention. The narrower account survives, while Glaze stays on duty pending local review. The story does not declare a completed acquittal or conviction.

A cracked lamp below bright teeth appears on a service wall. Glaze calls it inflammatory; residents call his spotless gloves worse. Some workers avoid him, some seek him out because they know he can get a door opened, and one offers him information about a neighbour in exchange for attention to a personal complaint. Fear creates customers for authority as well as opponents. Glaze's career has acquired a public stain, a private profit and a reason to want the people who remember the night made less credible.

The Pardon Ball that pardoned nobody

Pinkline veterans remember an off-duty celebration nicknamed the Pardon Ball. It took place earlier in the 3008 season, in a hired room rather than an official court. Guests arrived in immaculate pink uniforms with one deliberately ridiculous accessory: a feather, a false medal, a silk tail. The entertainment mocked the complaints officers heard at work. People who spent their shifts being hated enjoyed a night in which the laughter belonged to them.

Glaze's detail turned the ball into a prestigious invitation. His favourite performance imitated a resident demanding that three incompatible emergency requests all be answered first. It was funny because the difficulty was real. It was vicious because nobody in the room had to play the resident waiting outside. Ressa attended once, laughed at a different joke and refused the next invitation. Glaze subsequently described her absence as humourlessness, a diagnosis he found more comfortable than the possibility that she understood the joke perfectly.

A service worker called Ulda Brass, 44, kept a discarded programme bearing a patron's handwritten request for favourable attention to a private dispute. Its appearance in another room embarrassed the host without establishing that the requested favour occurred. Glaze stopped printing programmes. Officers kept the ball, the invitations and the memory of whose behaviour was considered hilarious. A scandal altered an object and left a social machine intact.

The spotless glove and the dirty invitation

Pinks use neatness as a language of belonging. A scuffed boot can suggest a hard shift; a neglected cuff suggests that its owner no longer cares who has to stand beside them. Off duty, some collect obscene jokes and extravagant accessories precisely because on duty they are expected to look reproducible. Others resent the balls and prefer an ordinary room in which nobody salutes them. Neither taste proves how they will treat a frightened resident.

Among Glaze's admirers, private attraction becomes another rank signal: who is invited upstairs, who is introduced as a companion and whose affair everybody agrees not to notice. The new relationships are between consenting adults over 21. A partner's consent does not make the distribution of invitations fair. Glaze treats a lover's dislike of his colleagues as a request to embarrass him in public. A colleague who quietly ends a relationship loses dinner invitations and continues receiving the same assigned shifts. Formal authority and personal exclusion work at different speeds.

The faction's sharpest internal insult is 'a clean glove with no hand in it': the officer who performs correctness while refusing responsibility. Glaze uses it about others. Residents use it about him. Pinkline's practical workers cannot abandon enforcement whenever its symbols are abused; Glaze cannot make their real work erase his private use of those symbols. The polished snout remains attractive to people who want order and unbearable to people who remember what the orderly version left out.
