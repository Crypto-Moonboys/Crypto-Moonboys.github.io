What “unbuffable” asks us to remember

The unbuffable manifesto takes a street-art vulnerability—someone else can paint over the mark—and turns it into an ambition for durable cultural memory. Digital copying can extend an image’s reach, while preservation still depends on people retaining its context and recognising who made it.

Read the claim as a cultural demand for continuity. A surviving file is one part of that work; a readable history, a credit and an audience willing to answer are others. The strongest version of the manifesto leaves room for change, so a mark can acquire new meaning without making its origin disappear or treating permanence as a substitute for continuing relevance.
