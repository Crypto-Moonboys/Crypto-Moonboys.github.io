Intimacy, Sexuality and Privacy

First WitnessPrivate-life ethics · Section 60Last updated: October 2026

The 2030 Covenant intentionally avoided making one global sexual code. Later communities retained very different moral traditions while the First Witness layer focused on consent, privacy, coercion, honesty and possession.

No universal bedroom law

Because the Concord did not erase older faiths or cultures, communities continued to disagree about marriage, sex, modesty and family structure. The First Witness does not flatten those differences into one retroactive doctrine.

Consent

The strongest cross-community principle is that intimacy obtained through force, threat, incapacity or deception is not made legitimate by relationship status, payment, custom or past consent.

Privacy

Private consensual behaviour is not automatically a public matter. The Witness demand for evidence is directed especially at public power, not at turning every private life into an inspectable ledger.

Possession language

Keeper schools apply the custody/ownership distinction to relationships: commitment can create duties without making another body or mind property.

Secrets and deception

Privacy protects the self. Deception manipulates another person's ability to consent. The distinction matters when information would reasonably change the terms of an intimate agreement.

Shame as control

Several factions have used sexual shame to enforce wider obedience. The First Witness critiques the use of intimate reputation as a weapon while recognising that communities can still hold genuine moral beliefs about intimate conduct.
