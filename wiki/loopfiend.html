🎭 Loopfiend

🎭 Lore 📅 Last updated: October 2026 📂 Lore

The Loopfiend is the Repeater of Regret, a dream adversary associated with Infinite Alley 9x. In the Dreamwars tradition, it grows from failed returns, forgotten tags and actions a traveller cannot stop trying to correct. Its most frightening face resembles the person confronting it. The Loopfiend does not merely repeat a moment: it turns the wish for a different ending into another reason to remain.

The singular fiend and the Dreamlayers

Dreamwars names loopfiends among the beings encountered in fractured layers of memory, then gives the Loopfiend its own encounter in Infinite Alley 9x. The plural can describe a kind of dream adversary; the singular identifies the particular Repeater of Regret. There is no settled roster of individual fiends or evidence that one sovereign mind directs every recurrence.

It belongs to the dream conflict surrounding Nullsong and damaged memory, not to a named political faction. The Dreamlayers overlap distorted places from Earth’s past: drowned vaults, impossible galleries and transit spaces whose routes repeat. The Loopfiend makes such a place feel personally familiar. A stranger’s ruined alley becomes the location of a mistake the traveller recognises.

The principal future saga remains Year 3008. Dream time need not behave like the public calendar, but that does not give the fiend authority to move the World Chain Triple Fork from 2880 or to settle the future Final Fork. Its power is encountered through particular repetitions, not a demonstrated ownership of all chronology.

A body made from returns

The strongest image gives the creature an arm for every failed run or forgotten tag. Its body accumulates unfinished attempts. A movement that once belonged to a person becomes another limb of the thing confronting them, so the traveller’s effort seems to enlarge the adversary rather than wear it down.

The face is harder to separate from the witness. In the Dreamwars encounter, the traveller faces a version of themself controlled by a corrupted save: a figure carrying tags never placed and movements never learned. The resemblance is therefore incomplete. It knows enough to be intimate and invents enough to make the person doubt which memories belong to them.

Its defining speech warns that someone who never stops dreaming cannot wake. That line can sound like advice, mockery or a trap. The fiend’s danger lies in the fact that all three may feel identical to a person already trying to distinguish one return from another.

Infinite Alley 9x

The alley is not known through a stable municipal map. It is approached as a dream place: walls recur at the wrong distance, a familiar turn brings a traveller back to an earlier mark, and a passage may retain signs of choices the person does not remember making. Nine-x is its inherited name, not proof of a ninth city district or exactly nine repetitions.

Within the alley, repetition is persuasive because details change. A lamp that failed on the previous passage is now lit. A door seems to wait one breath longer before closing. The small improvement offers a reason to attempt the route again. The fiend does not need every version to be identical; it needs the next version to look capable of answering the last.

Accounts of the place often disagree about distance and sequence. A witness can report a long pursuit while another remembers a brief absence. The surviving record does not force those experiences into one clean duration. Something can be long for the person enduring it without producing an equally long gap in the world to which they return.

The stolen last movement

The Loopfiend’s encounter copies movements and mistakes in real time. A defensive gesture returns as pressure from another direction; a repeated correction becomes a recognisable rhythm. The traveller begins anticipating the imitation, and anticipation itself becomes another repeated act the fiend can reflect.

This gives the creature a different character from a straightforward pursuer. It appears to study, but no secure account establishes ordinary understanding or intention behind the copying. It may be malicious intelligence, a process shaped by the traveller or a dream form in which those categories cannot be separated.

The Mirror Chain Form associated with HEX-Tagger Prime also uses repetition, but similar imagery does not establish a shared being. HEX’s dedicated story concerns a captured artist and imposed service. The Loopfiend concerns an adversary assembled from recursive experience. One should not become the other merely because both return a movement to its maker.

Iven Trell and the Second Cup

Iven Trell, a keeper of a small overnight receiving room, recounts meeting the fiend through an ordinary unfinished task. He dreams of handing a warm drink to a traveller whose fingers cannot hold the cup. Each time the cup falls, he catches it slightly earlier on the next attempt. The room praises him with a small improvement: less spilled water, a cleaner cloth, a steadier hand.

Then a second arm reaches across the table, matching his movement. Its sleeve is his own. By the time he notices the stranger has not been allowed to speak, several versions of Iven are reaching for the same cup. Every attempted rescue occupies more of the space the guest needs to stand.

He returns from the dream with no answer about who the traveller was. The receiving room continues serving drinks, and Iven asks a colleague to take the next shift because he cannot look at the cup without replaying the scene. Later listeners call this the Second Cup account. Its importance is not a miraculous cure: the work continues because someone else can carry it while he rests.

Miri Oss and the marks that move

Miri Oss is a wall-letterer who records a different encounter. Her dream begins with a misspelled name on a remembrance surface. She corrects the letters and discovers that the correction has displaced another name. Restoring the second damages a third. Soon every line looks like evidence of something she has failed to preserve.

The Loopfiend appears with the lettering tools she discarded during earlier attempts. Its arms finish words before she has chosen them. Some belong to people she knows; others are convincing inventions. Miri recognises the most dangerous part of the scene when she feels relieved by a completed wall even though she no longer knows who several names describe.

Afterwards she keeps the account separate from the actual remembrance commission she was working on. None of the invented dream names is added to the public list. She asks the families to review the existing lettering instead. The dream has changed her attention, but it does not become authority to create a person or announce a death.

The Unclaimed Hour

The Unclaimed Hour begins when two travellers return from what they call Infinite Alley 9x and disagree about a third companion. One remembers the companion leaving before the alley. The other recalls holding their sleeve during the last return. No receiving record establishes that a third person arrived with them.

A listener named Rooket Pell writes the disagreement down without choosing the more detailed version. The missing name is kept as an unresolved claim, not entered as a confirmed death. Notices ask for corroboration without publishing intimate dream fragments the travellers have not agreed to share.

The inquiry does not solve the discrepancy. One traveller becomes angry that a vivid recollection cannot compel belief; the other fears being accused of abandoning someone who may never have been there. Rooket’s record preserves both experiences and the limit of what can be established. The title Unclaimed Hour comes from that absence of a shared sequence, not from a measured sixty-minute failure of time.

What “forgetting on purpose” means in the tale

The Dreamwars description says the encounter can be escaped by breaking one’s rhythm and “forgetting on purpose.” Within its story, the phrase means relinquishing the demand to replay the same response until it produces a perfect past. It does not require destroying the record of a harm or accepting the fiend’s invented version of events.

The distinction matters to Miri. She stops completing the dream wall, but preserves an account of why the completion became dangerous. Iven does not decide that the guest’s difficulty was unimportant; he stops treating his own endlessly improved gesture as the only possible form of help. Their stories give the phrase different practical meanings without making either a universal formula.

The term is specific to the fictional Dreamlayers. Painful recollection in ordinary life is not evidence that a person has created a demon, and a failure to leave a loop in a story does not make a sufferer responsible for it. The fiend embodies a coercive repetition; the people who endure it are not its moral authors.

The Return Room

The receiving room used by Iven develops a quieter arrangement for people who come back uncertain of sequence. It is simply called the Return Room. A chair, water, a place to leave wet clothing and an explanation of who is present matter more than an immediate demand for the complete adventure.

Visitors can give a short account, ask for it to remain private or decline to speak. The room keeps ordinary arrival times while allowing that the visitor may have experienced something very different. A clock can establish when a cup was served without establishing how long an alley felt.

The work is fallible. A volunteer once copies an emotional description of “years” into a duration field and makes the record appear more precise than the speaker intended. The error is corrected with the original wording retained. Iven uses the mistake when training others: the room is there to help a person return to shared life, not to turn uncertainty into a spectacular statistic.

What follows a return

Loop stories often end at the moment of awakening. The people who host the returned witness inherit less dramatic consequences. Someone misses a shift, loses a commission or cannot face a familiar corridor. A family wants reassurance the listener cannot give. These needs continue whether the encounter’s mechanism has been resolved or remains disputed.

Miri loses paid lettering work while another craftsperson completes her commission. She credits that person rather than presenting the finished wall as proof that the encounter granted her a new power. Iven’s colleague asks to be paid for the extra nights. Neither request diminishes the courage involved in returning.

The Return Room has no promise of complete restoration. Some visitors stop coming, and their absence is not automatically recorded as recovery or disappearance. The ordinary task is to keep obligations clear enough that an extraordinary account does not swallow the rest of a person’s life.

The gift of another attempt

The fiend’s greatest temptation is a second chance with no visible cost. A missed warning can be delivered sooner; an insult can be unsaid; a defeated fighter can choose differently. The earlier local account celebrates that possibility by describing battles restarted at the critical instant.

The darker Dreamwars account asks what is being traded for the attempt. If each correction creates another arm, the chance to revise becomes a form of captivity. The traveller’s agency narrows until the only available choice seems to be whether to repeat again.

No settled account shows the Loopfiend offering a reliable service to a faction or being persuaded to restore a lost historical event. Its apparent gifts remain part of the danger. A moment that resembles rescue can still bind the recipient more tightly to the place they need to leave.

The shattered timestamp legend

The older page gives Loopfiend a connection to a broken timestamp in the Sacred Chain and imagines a being able to skip through any defence or trap whole armies in defeat. These claims survive as a broader legend of time mastery. The dedicated Dreamwars entry supports a more intimate figure: a repeater of movements, errors and unresolved experience.

The shattered timestamp is not established as a physical relic or a fragment of the World Chain Triple Fork. Nor is the Sacred/Aether memory tradition interchangeable with Block Topia’s True Bitcoin Fork. Leaving the rumour unresolved preserves its atmosphere without giving the fiend an invented master key to the universe’s history.

The same limit applies to supposed appearances at the Hard Fork Games. No developed Games scene identifies an intervention by the Loopfiend with enough detail to settle its outcome. The rumour may explain why a competitor remembers a moment strangely; it cannot become a convenient explanation for every disputed result.

The Dreamwars encounter tradition

The detailed character entry belongs to the proposed Dreamwars game, one part of a potential trilogy. It names Infinite Alley 9x, the Repeater of Regret title, an endless encounter phase and a corrupted-save reflection. The surrounding design also imagines an Echo Bead Crown reward connected to confronting Loopfiend with ancestral sigils.

Those details establish the shape of a proposed story encounter. They do not verify a live reward, current playable mode or a canonical victory by a named hero. The source’s resetting structure is part of its dramatic design, not proof that the entire main saga repeats under the creature’s control.

The broader Dreamlayers include Pyralith, GRIT, Nullborn and Echo Daughter traditions. Their proximity gives the world several forms of damaged memory. It does not turn them into one species or supply a single explanation for every dream adversary.

Fictional dream adversary

The Loopfiend is fictional. Its recursive body and dream confrontations belong to the Crypto Moonboys story world. Its exact consciousness, origin and possible end remain unresolved. The present account leaves the Final Fork open and treats a returned person’s life as larger than the repetition through which they passed.

The Last Good Night

The Last Good Night is a new adult Dreamlayer account of a visitor who wants to return to the evening before a relationship ended. Both remembered partners are over 21. The room first gives back ordinary tenderness: a shared meal, a hand across the table and the belief that the difficult conversation can be delayed. The visitor sees the moment at which a better sentence might have saved them and begins changing the sentence on each return.

The other person's reply becomes warmer and less recognisable. A disagreement that once belonged to an independent mind turns into a prompt inviting another improved answer. The visitor recognises the theft and continues for several repetitions because the revised closeness feels wonderful. The Loopfiend's copied movements begin occupying the places where the remembered partner should have been able to choose.

On returning to the receiving room, the visitor cannot bear an ordinary cup set down carelessly. The sound resembles the imperfect evening the dream made seem replaceable. Iven finds somebody else to take the immediate shift and allows the account to remain incomplete. The visitor's former partner is not reported restored, injured by the dream or obliged to hear it. A fantasy of saving intimacy has consumed time without giving the other person any part in the supposed rescue.

The apology with no exit

A separate new adult encounter begins with an apology the traveller never offered after humiliating a friend. Infinite Alley 9x supplies the friend at the far end, apparently willing to listen. Every sincere sentence opens another detail the traveller should acknowledge. The successive accounts become more complete, then begin including harms they do not recognise. Refusing a detail feels like retreating into the original selfishness.

The copied face calls them a coward when they hesitate. It sounds convincing because cowardice was part of the real event. The traveller asks whether the friend can leave. No answer arrives. The perfect apology has become another meeting in which the injured person is kept available for the speaker's progress.

The returned traveller later sends a short ordinary request to the actual adult friend and receives no reply. They keep the refusal separate from the dream's promise of eventual completion. The Loopfiend remains frightening because it can use a true fault to make endless correction feel deserved. Responsibility can survive an unanswered apology; the fiend wants the unanswered part to become a place the person never stops trying to enter.
