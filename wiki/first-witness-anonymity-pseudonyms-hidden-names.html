Anonymity, Pseudonyms and Hidden Names

First WitnessIdentity tradition · Section 78Last updated: October 2026

A tradition that values names also learned why names sometimes need protection. The question became not whether anonymity is good, but what anonymity is doing.

The protected witness

Whistleblowers, dissidents and vulnerable witnesses may need anonymity to speak safely. Demanding a public name can function as a method of silencing.

The accountable pseudonym

A pseudonym can accumulate reputation over time without exposing a legal identity. Graffiti culture made this obvious centuries before later chain identities formalised it.

The disposable mask

Anonymity becomes harder to defend when identities are created solely to harm others and discarded before consequences attach. Reputation requires continuity even when legal names remain hidden.

Private identity escrow

Some systems allow a trusted process to verify uniqueness or eligibility without publishing the underlying identity. Critics warn that whoever controls the escrow becomes another keeper.

The right to disappear

Witness traditions conflict with privacy over whether people can intentionally withdraw from public life. Most schools distinguish public records needed for accountability from ordinary personal data that need not remain searchable forever.

The name test

Do you need the person's name to judge the evidence, or do you want the name so the person can be punished outside the argument?
