Life on the Waterfront

Camo Bear wears green and blue because clothes that already look busy hide a surprising amount of soil. He earns his keep tending the edges of paths on the nature side of the Waterfront. Some days he counts how many people carry something through a gate before he decides which growth to cut back.

Who gets the soil

Camo got his first paid garden job when a vendor gave him the strip behind her stall in exchange for keeping the gutter clear. He tried to talk her into a vegetable patch the first week. By the end of summer she had talked him into leaving a clear space for her trolley and given him paper cups for the seedlings. The useful part of that deal was that each could see when the other had failed to do their share.

People with quick plans for the nature trails can make him stubborn. He has carried soil back twice after being told that a bed would only move once. He trades seedlings with Green and old crate boards with the Nomad pilot. At dominoes he is willing to lose so long as somebody moves over to give the last person a place to sit.

The turn by the growing bed

A footpath near his first growing plot kept widening. He blamed impatient walkers until a carrier showed him how little room the old turn gave a person with a laden cart. They moved one bed and planted it up again. He still feels a twinge when he passes the replacement plot and sees how well it gets the sun.

Off duty

He trades seedlings in old paper cups and puts the name of the grower on each one. He loses at dominoes regularly and claims the game punishes a person for thinking about the ground when he should be looking at the table.

The bed at the second exit

During the trial he had to think quickly when the two-person crate could not turn. He offered the turnout before he asked the tenant who looked after that corner what she was ready to move. Her answer about collection hours mattered more to the final arrangement than the favour he thought he was doing. She had seedlings ready for people who could only stop after work. The boards bought them time to make a fairer plan.

He thinks a permanent turnout can fit if the tenant gets another patch with decent light and paid help lifting the bed. He is afraid that the route will get marked “done” while she is still carrying pots across the yard herself. Ask Camo where to go and he will tell you how wide the turn is first. People who wait for the rest of the answer usually leave with a seedling.

He sells weed on the side and has let people assume they know what grows in every bed he tends. During the trial the tenant asked him to use her name whenever he talked about her corner. He heard how often he had talked about it as his. When the patrol came by later, she gave her own account of the new turnout and Camo kept his mouth shut until she finished.
