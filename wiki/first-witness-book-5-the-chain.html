Book V — The Chain

First WitnessCanonical scripture · Book VLast updated: October 2026

Book V became the most argued-over text in the tradition because decentralists and centralisers both found language they could claim.

The text

1 A chain is a promise that the next link can inspect the last.

2 Continuity is not obedience. The next link receives history, not a command to repeat it.

3 Make records that can outlive the institution that created them.

4 Distribute what must survive. Redundancy is humility made technical.

5 Do not place every human need behind one gate, however efficient the gate appears.

6 Efficiency can hide fragility by making failure rare and consequence universal.

7 A common system is useful when it serves many. It becomes dangerous when many cannot live without its permission.

8 Verification is a tool against deception. Verification is not a replacement for judgement.

9 A machine may prove sequence. It cannot prove justice.

10 A machine may enforce a contract. It cannot know whether the contract should have existed.

11 Code can reduce discretion and also bury discretion inside whoever wrote the code.

12 Publish rules that bind the public.

13 Permit inspection where inspection does not expose the innocent to needless harm.

14 Do not make secrecy the default of power or publicity the default of private life.

15 One ledger may simplify settlement. One ledger must not become the only possible memory of a civilisation.

16 When a system grows too important to question, questioning it becomes a public duty.

17 When a system cannot be changed, prepare a way to leave it.

18 When a system can be changed by one hidden hand, it is not the shared system it claims to be.

19 Forking is not always betrayal. Sometimes the threat of departure keeps the centre honest.

20 Forking is not always freedom. Fragmentation can destroy the common ground needed to settle reality.

21 Preserve bridges between records even when communities separate.

22 Do not burn the old chain merely because you prefer the new one. Future generations may need the comparison.

23 No chain is sacred because people call it sacred.

24 What deserves reverence is the human responsibility to carry truth, life and memory forward.

25 The chain serves the people. The people do not exist to complete the chain.

Reading rule

This Book belongs to the 2030 Covenant layer. Later pages may interpret it through Year 3008 events, but those later names are not retroactively part of the original text.

The wording is kept direct because the tradition had to survive translation, quotation, song, argument and wall transmission rather than depend on one ornate liturgical language.
