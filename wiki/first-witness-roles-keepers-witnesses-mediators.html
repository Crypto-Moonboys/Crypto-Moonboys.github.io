Keepers, Witnesses, Mediators and Other Roles

First WitnessCommunity roles · Section 69Last updated: October 2026

The First Witness has religious specialists without requiring a single priest class. Roles exist because communities need skill, not because one office owns truth.

Keepers

Keepers maintain something entrusted: archives, funds, tools, buildings, water systems, communal keys or institutional memory. Keeper status is functional, not automatically spiritual.

Witnesses

Recognised Witnesses are trained in testimony, evidence handling and correction. They may certify processes without certifying the morality of everything involved.

Mediators

Mediators help opposing sides clarify claims, identify shared facts and negotiate boundaries. They are not neutral in the sense of lacking values; they are expected to be transparent about conflicts of interest.

Readers

Readers specialise in textual history and interpretation. Different sects produce different Readers. No central body licenses all of them.

Wallkeepers

Wallkeepers maintain public expression spaces, archives of removed work and local mark history. GraffPUNK cultures often resist the title because it sounds too institutional while performing the function anyway.

Passers

A Passer prepares succession: documenting systems, training replacements and ensuring key knowledge does not die with one expert. The role is especially respected in engineering cultures.

No automatic hierarchy

One person may hold several roles, but no role automatically outranks all others. A skilled Reader may know scripture and still be terrible at mediation. Expertise is local.
