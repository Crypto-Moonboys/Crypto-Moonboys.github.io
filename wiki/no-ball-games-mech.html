Life on the Waterfront

The No Ball Games Mech is recognisable by its painted colour panels and its conspicuous sign. It brings fixtures and markings for games that start after work and finish when the ground has to go back to its other uses. Its crew includes regulars who have spent more time preparing a space than actually getting onto a team.

The game that belongs to a place

The mech’s operator played their first full match on a square that turned back into a loading yard halfway through the afternoon. They remember finishing a point while strangers put the shop deliveries down beside the lines. As a fixture carrier, they have learnt which fence posts are available for a net and how expensive a damaged gate can be to a team that has no place else to go. They care about scores, enjoy a good grudge match and can get too caught up in one to notice who still has the last job.

The wear marks on old game balls mean as much to them as a record of winning sides. Straight Outta Ghetto Bear can identify the one they lost in the yard after they both abandoned a wet game. The operator still claims it was his call to leave. He claims the mech could have carried two loads in a single trip. They’re both very particular about who did the carrying when they tell the story.

The first round

A match once started on time while the people who set out the lines were still taking supplies back to the clubhouse. The score mattered to the spectators. The lost minutes mattered to the volunteers. The next weekend the crew gave the first round to the workers and kept the regular teams waiting until everyone had a turn.

Off duty

The operator keeps worn game balls and studies which surface left each scuff. Straight Outta Ghetto Bear once exchanged a patched ball for a spare marking brush. Both kept insisting the other one had come away better.

The first round after the trial

On the trial night they moved the lines uphill to leave space at the turnout. A regular player threatened to leave at the delay and the operator very nearly gave him the first round. They stayed with the workers who had just cleared the landing. Afterwards the operator helped restore the ground, long enough to hear how many of the workers had expected to finish the evening without getting to play at all.

A repeat will depend on the closing shift having time and the landing still being usable. The operator wants the next match book to list whose paid hours set the ground up. One of the regular teams is already threatening to run a faster game on another square. Ask about the old sign and the operator may hand you a scuffed ball and ask where you reckon it bounced.
