A body above the seawall

From shore the first sight can be a piece of crown advancing in open water. Then comes the whole head, the shoulders, and enough chest to put an ordinary boat in shadow. It can walk in water that would drown the tallest yard machinery. Men who drew it from the lamps made it taller on every copy. The north watch uses the height of the marker it passed behind and puts the observer’s position beside the figure.

Harbor Kaiju is the name the watch broadcasts. A salvage boss tried to get Breaker King onto the warning boards so he could sell badges with it. Eddie left the name of the watch clerk on the first board instead. She was the one who had to be woken when the next report came in.

Before the Ninth Berth

Outer-yard crews once laid stiff wire across the deep reach to catch loose freight before it went out to sea. During a fog watch three spans came back slack. One cable had pulled out of a bed of stone without tripping the powered winch. Another had split at a depth where the yard could barely afford an inspection. The owner charged the night supervisor for mishandling the gear. His daughter kept the invoice in the same envelope as his report of a ridge moving under the fog.

A salvage diver called Ro Vale later described three upright impressions on the shelf beyond the wire. He found crushed shell in each and clean stone between them. Farther out the line of impressions went over a drop his lamp could not reach. The owner paid for the depths and struck the impressions out of the report. Vale wrote them on the back of the job slip before he turned in his air bottle. The shift never got called back to take another look.

An old rigging song gives the thing a place in a drowned foundry. In one version it broke free when the furnaces were flooded. In another the heat under the sea saved it through a cold age. The chorus predates the name on the warning board in only one singer’s account. Nobby kept both recordings. He was more interested in the cracked flange Vale had brought up than in which verse drew the bigger crowd.

The offshore walk and the fire

Far out, the Kaiju travels upright with a rolling sheet of water around each leg. The direction of the body can change well before the old wash reaches the landing. The watch has seen it make an offshore circuit and disappear again for long stretches. At the north bank it can bring a foot onto the soft ground, drag its weight after it and walk across the mud and stone. The lamps were still upright after the first footfall. The ground that fed their cables had moved.

The landfall witnesses saw flame when the head came round across the shore. A burst west was caught on a wet office window; the worse burst east reached the opposite end of the yard. The smoke changed direction between them. The watch has evidence for fire during that run on land. Offshore footage shows steam, sea spray and a clear outline of the head. What makes the fire is open. A man with an exclusive contract to test the scrap says he knows and has published a price for telling anyone else.

Some witnesses thought it was hunting the yard lights. One of the men from the passage said the thing had kept walking after the lamps went out. Its pace eased where the ground held and quickened again where the edge gave. What the animal can see, what brings it onto land and whether it returns along the same bottom are things the crew still argue about.

At the Ninth Berth

The north-cut landfall happened two nights after the loss of the waste launch. The Kaiju approached from the outer reach and hauled itself onto the north bank away from the working landing. That distinction mattered to the men on the beer crates who kept telling people the berth itself had vanished. They hadn’t seen the damage. Workers who had could point from the upper route to the place where the kerb and cable trench came away together.

The north apron took both mud and fire. The freight office burned on the turn. A worker died before the stretcher made it to the upper landing. His colleague survived with burns and could only give evidence in short visits. Nine Toes Joe put the uphill route into use before there was a vote. By the time the yard manager came down to demand his driver back, the stretcher team were climbing the lane the driver would have blocked.

People asked Zarah to sound the old berth bell twice so everyone would know this was serious. She used the watch call. The district already had a tape of the bell repeating during a power failure. Chada knew exactly what a repeat would put into people’s heads. He left the whole watch call intact and refused to sell the last scream as an alert sound.

After the fire

The Kaiju returned offshore. At daybreak the foot wells filled from the ground up. The surveying crew tied a peg to the freight office marker and found it had shifted again by the time they came back from breakfast. They moved their bench uphill. The first reopening estimate assumed the apron would carry the same load it had the morning before landfall. Nobby refused to put his name to it.

The dead worker’s last recorded hours belonged to the yard. The yard’s insurer tried to count the fire as an offshore incident because the animal had come out of the sea. Danny asked them to locate the office on the map while the watch clerk was in the room. Losing the argument bought the family the right to file a claim. It didn’t buy them a date for payment. The other worker still had to go past the burned wall every time he was called in to prove where he had been.

At the next offshore sighting a yard owner wanted the watch to open the north gates on the strength of the animal having gone past. The clerk read out her log and left the closure in force. Offshore movement can be routine. A landfall is rare enough that a contractor can build a comfortable business betting against one. The ground behind the north bank still holds the evidence of what that bet costs when it goes bad.
