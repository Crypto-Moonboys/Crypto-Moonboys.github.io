The difference between an expanding world and a complete one

An editorial audit records where a project believes it stands at a particular moment. Its gaps can be more revealing than its totals: a named faction without a daily life, a timeline without consequences, a character whose relationships exist only in a list. Those absences identify where further writing might make the world easier to inhabit.

Read the March assessment as a planning document. Later articles, approved canon decisions and current publishing data describe what changed after it. A strong audit preserves that distinction because completion is a claim with a scope. A page can exist without reconciling every witness, and a graph connection can help discovery without proving a story’s truth. The value lies in turning an observed gap into an accountable next piece of work.
