Love, Family, Children and Elders

First WitnessHousehold tradition · Section 34Last updated: October 2026

Family is where the Covenant's best principles become hardest to practise because love, obligation, memory and power are tangled together.

Children are not unfinished parents

The Common Blood tradition rejects the idea that a child exists to complete the parent's abandoned plan. Parents may teach language, history, craft, belief and responsibility. They may not claim ownership of the adult identity that eventually emerges.

Several later schools describe children as “future witnesses, not future copies.”

Elders deserve honour, not immunity

Age carries memory and often wisdom. It can also carry old prejudice, family secrecy and habits nobody has challenged because “that is how it has always been.”

The Covenant's respect for inheritance therefore sits beside its insistence that rank does not make testimony automatically true.

The family archive

Some households keep a shared archive containing births, deaths, photographs, recipes, debts repaid, moves, arguments resolved, stories of migration and mistakes people explicitly asked future generations not to repeat.

The strongest archives include ordinary people rather than only successful ancestors. A family that remembers only winners teaches children that struggle is shameful.

Inheritance

Property can be inherited. Guilt cannot be inherited automatically. Neither can merit. A famous family name may create obligations because it created advantages, but it does not prove the heir earned the ancestor's reputation.

Several factions build entire political arguments around disagreeing with this distinction.

Chosen family

Street traditions expanded Common Blood to include crews, adopters, carers, mentors and people who performed family duties without biological relation. This did not erase kinship. It recognised that care can create real obligations where blood did not provide them.

Leaving home

A First Witness household is ideally one a child can leave without becoming dead to the family. The ability to leave does not guarantee the absence of grief, disagreement or consequences. It means belonging is not enforced through total social erasure.

One common blessing for an adult leaving home says: Take what helped you. Correct what harmed you. Do not confuse either with the whole of us.
