Mediation and Witness Circles

First WitnessDispute practice · Section 70Last updated: October 2026

Not every conflict needs a judge. Some need a room where people can establish what they disagree about before deciding what should happen next.

The Witness Circle

A small circle usually includes the disputing parties, a mediator and one or more recorders. The goal is first to create a shared list of claims, not a shared conclusion.

Facts, interpretations, requests

Participants separate statements into three categories: what they claim happened, what they think it means and what they want now. Mixing those categories often makes resolution harder.

The no-ambush rule

Evidence should be disclosed early enough for the other side to answer where safety permits. Saving decisive evidence for theatrical revelation is discouraged outside emergency contexts.

When mediation is inappropriate

Serious violence, coercive control and severe power imbalance may require protection or formal investigation before mediation. A victim should not be pressured into negotiation simply because dialogue sounds peaceful.

The unresolved record

Some circles end without agreement. The record then states exactly what remains disputed rather than forcing a fake resolution.

The return clause

Agreements often include a date or condition for review. A settlement that cannot be revisited may preserve peace temporarily while allowing resentment to accumulate invisibly.

The meeting that could settle payment but not violence

Orra Mote of the Nice & Easy Bois helps arrange the later Dry Step talks after the failed collection in the broken hoist case. The invitation states that the parties may discuss receiving recognition, a compensation reserve and use of the ferry approach. It does not ask Ira Noll or the injured messenger to bargain away a finding of harm. The knife incident has its own hearing and protection arrangements.

Venn Krail first asks for a joint photograph before agreeing to a reserve. Femi Brace declines to prepare the room for it. A picture would suggest a reconciliation no participant has accepted. The revised invitation instead lists what a carrier could actually promise: a share of specified receipts, a schedule and a way for receiving hosts to check the reserve. Ira's representative can examine those terms while Ira remains absent.

One Dry Step host refuses the market's first demand for automatic recognition of future Lantern orders. Orra records that refusal without calling it obstruction of peace. A smaller agreement follows: recognition of this compensation finding after review, a separate local hearing for violence, and no general right to seize at the yard. This is useful because its actual performers accept it. It leaves the wider jurisdictional conflict available for another day.

The circle's record distinguishes an accepted payment mechanism from trust in the carrier. Venn has reason to comply because a needed booking depends on guarantees. That motive does not invalidate the payments and does not establish personal reform. The mediator's achievement is an arrangement people can use, with the unaccepted claims still named beside it.
