🎭 GraffPUNKS Ecosystem

🌐 Ecosystem Guide 📅 Last updated: October 2026 📂 Lore Canon reference · revision 1

The GraffPUNKS ecosystem is the connected field of art, music, creator identities, collection records, community activity and fictional worldbuilding around GraffPUNKS. This page is a reading guide to those relationships. It does not describe a single company owning every linked project, a complete roster of live services or a ranking of which creators matter most.

Crypto Moonboys is the public creator umbrella. Graffiti Kings provides the real cultural foundation, GKniftyHEADS the primary Moonboy identity architecture, and GraffPUNKS a connected cultural voice through images, writing and sound. Understanding those roles makes the rest of the network easier to navigate.

What connects the ecosystem

The connection begins with identity rather than technology. A recognisable tag, character, voice or style allows an audience to connect separate works. Repeated activity gives that identity a history. Community then helps the work travel, while records preserve who made it and what has actually been completed.

The ecosystem brings different surfaces into that sequence. A radio programme can introduce a creator; an artwork can establish a visual signal; a collection record can identify an asset; a story can give a character a world; a public proof page can document participation. These functions reinforce one another without becoming interchangeable forms of evidence.

The foundation pages and their jobs

Graffiti Kings explains the real street-art foundation and cultural succession. Its relationship to later fiction should be read as influence, not as evidence that real people literally command the future world. Detailed biographies, premises and commercial history require their own sources.

GKniftyHEADS explains the primary Moonboy identity architecture and the associated collection context. Its fictional Immortal Architects chapter is a distinct memory-keeper tradition. GraffPUNKS explains the cultural network, the Unwritten Wall Codex and the fictional Paint Path. None of these pages needs to become a substitute for the other two.

Crypto Moonboys supplies the top-level purpose: helping people build identity, recognition, community, ownership routes and maintained momentum. Games, NFTs, radio, lore, the Wiki and Block Topia support that purpose. Entering through one of those surfaces should still lead a reader toward the wider creator model.

Reading route: build a Moonboy identity

Begin with what the character could become. A Moonboy can be a mascot, public alter ego, story protagonist, product identity, channel identity or part of a family of characters. A creator may build around one Moonboy or several across different factions. The point is a recognisable world with work and community around it.

The intended progression uses Telegram identity, a current faction, missions or Arcade play and server-backed XP where those systems are available. Eligible drops, keep or trade choices, burns, evolution and a qualifying unique 1/1 route depend on their published activation and eligibility. An archive page describing a possible sequence does not make every stage live.

The useful reading sequence is GKniftyHEADS, the current IncubatorHUB introduction and the relevant public participation routes. Collection data then helps identify particular assets. Lore can provide inspiration after the reader understands the identity they want to develop.

Reading route: strengthen an existing identity

The second path is equally valid. An artist, musician, business, product creator, organisation or existing alter ego can retain its name and history. This route does not require a Moonboy, NFT, faction, Arcade score or a commitment to writing future-world fiction.

SWARMSY is the private creator operating environment for both paths. An existing project can use it to clarify positioning, audience, voice, visual direction, plans and the work needed to maintain momentum. The person’s accumulated sources, permissions, proof and lessons help the workspace become specific to their practice.

A DJ arriving through the radio may want a clearer programme identity. A painter may want a coherent portfolio and campaign. A business may need a recognisable public promise. Those examples show the relevance of the cultural method without claiming that every deployment has a connected publishing, email or commerce tool. Actual releases and available tools govern execution.

Private development and the shared record

Crypto Moonboys and SWARMSY are connected but different spaces. The public umbrella holds community, knowledge, shared history and participation. The private environment supports the thinking and decisions behind a creator’s work. A proposed direction can be explored privately before it becomes a public claim.

Within SWARMSY, SPARKY is the visible guide and project-context manager. AnythingLLM supplies the underlying intelligence and workspace foundation, with a selected model helping SPARKY reason. Starter Wiki Packs provide small situational guidance; they are not encyclopaedias containing every location, law or answer.

Memory Locks protect approved decisions, while Proof Review distinguishes ideas, proposals, completed work and supported claims. This distinction is useful throughout the ecosystem. A convincing image does not establish a manufactured product; a draft announcement does not establish a partnership; and a page about a future game does not prove a running service.

Reading route: radio and cultural transmission

GraffPUNKS 24/7 Radio explains the station, its archived programme vocabulary, current site-player integration and reward proposals. A listener can enter through the music alone. Following the station does not require participation in a token economy or ownership route.

The Unwritten Wall Codex connects four foundations: chaos, graffiti, music and family. Graffiti makes a public mark; music carries recognition across distance; family and community provide continuity beyond a single broadcast. The Codex’s Fifth Dimension describes their interaction as a literary framework, not a new physical layer of the internet.

The radio reference also separates real listening from the fictional use of broadcasts in the Year 3008 memory war. A stream playing beside an Arcade game is a concrete integration. The idea that a song secretly grants a battle advantage is a different claim and requires its own evidence before it can be called a live mechanic.

Reading route: art, collections and object records

The GraffPUNKS Collection and AtomicHub collection reference sit beside the GKniftyHEADS NFT collection index. Their purposes are related but not identical: cultural context, marketplace reference and structured collection data answer different questions.

For the maintained GKniftyHEADS tracker, AtomicAssets data identifies templates and individual assets. Original mint number and surviving mint rank are separate measurements. Similar names or images do not establish that two identifiers are the same object, while scarcity does not establish a character licence or a guaranteed price.

Head Tags belongs to the fictional identity-artefact tradition and should not be used as a substitute for a verified asset catalogue. The same care applies to phygital language: a connection between digital art and a physical object may be an actual release, a limited redemption or a concept. The particular record must establish which.

Ownership, collaboration and canon

Under published creator terms, qualifying unique 1/1 holders own the assigned character identity and licence within its exact scope. They keep 100% of royalties from approved commercial use of that character; Crypto Moonboys and Graffiti Kings take no royalty percentage under the current public rule. These terms do not transfer the entire umbrella or another creator’s work.

Qualifying holders become HODL Warriors in the active creator-builder role. They develop worlds, support other builders, share work and collaborate across faction identities. HODL Warriors is not a normal selectable live faction and does not merely mean someone who refuses to sell an asset.

Creator-owned lore, community-approved lore and official canon remain different statuses. A holder can own a character and create a story without that story already belonging to the shared official universe. A rejected canon submission does not remove underlying ownership. The Wiki records approved status; it does not create unlimited rights by mentioning a person or their work.

Reading route: games and participation

The current Arcade, Telegram launcher and Block Topia route belong to maintained participation systems. Competitive Arcade activity requires linked Telegram identity; score and persistent accepted XP are different signals. Block Topia’s gate belongs to current configuration rather than a hard-coded figure copied into a hub.

GAMES4PUNKS Telegram and Gang Signs preserve older community-game references and inherited fictional motifs. They are useful context, but their names are not proof of complete current rules, token rewards or cross-game powers. Readers should follow a verified present entry point when the goal is to play.

This distinction preserves the value of proposals. A proposed card game, short challenge or story mission can be worth developing without being described as already released. A public record is more useful when it shows what is built, what is being tested and what remains a design idea.

Token and burn references

The network includes references to $PUNK, NBGX and burn routes. Their presence in a shared index does not establish that they are interchangeable currencies, mandatory entry requirements or simultaneously active reward systems.

The w75 $PUNK greypaper explicitly describes future plans for listening rewards, events and artist funding. Other promotional texts connect several chains, NFTs and games in a larger ambition. Each specific claim still needs current rules and implementation evidence. Old market figures and sweeping statements about utility do not become live facts merely because several related pages repeat them.

An earned asset also does not require a particular financial decision. Keep, trade or burn choices belong to supported routes and their published conditions. No page in this reading guide promises profit, automatic yield, universal redemption or a compulsory purchase as the price of joining the creator movement.

Reading route: the Year 3008 world

For the future setting, begin with the First Witness chronology, then Block Topia and the Street Kingdoms. The chronology fixes the Great Concord in 2030, World Chain Triple Fork in 2880 and the Queens secure-region basis of Block Topia by 2930. The principal saga is set in 3008.

The Sacred/Aether-Chain memory tradition, World Chain infrastructure and Block Topia’s True Bitcoin Fork have distinct roles. Queen Sarah P-fly belongs to the city’s Authority story. NULL introduces the threat of erasure. GKniftyHEADS’ memory keepers are not automatically the city’s rulers simply because their name is central to the present project.

Bitcoin Kids are the escaped or outside lineage associated with Alfie Blaze. Bitcoin X Kids remain the engineered inside generation. Their similar names require more care, not fewer distinctions. The same applies to HODL Warriors and HODL X Warriors.

Forty factions without a false corporate map

The shared world retains forty factions with different philosophies, roles and conflicts. They make the setting capable of disagreement: memory keepers, artists, repairers, competitors, traders and enforcers do not all want the same future. Their connections are cultural and narrative before they are a list of current interface options.

The real project connects Moonboy factions through OG Graffiti Kings artists and the wider GK cultural network. That does not establish forty registered subsidiaries, a complete ownership chart or an identical creator licence across all work. Likewise, this guide does not fill missing faction slots by inventing an apparently authoritative roster.

The HODL WARS reference explores the wider fictional conflict; the Hard Fork Games reference explains its distinct competitive and coercive traditions. Neither settles the Final Fork’s outcome. Future victory accounts remain prospective or disputed where the merged canon leaves the ending open.

How to read graph connections

The graph can reveal useful paths between pages. A line may arise from a link, shared topic or other relationship signal. It does not by itself say that one person founded another project, that two titles are aliases, that a licence transfers between them or that one event caused the other.

The previous hub’s repeated rank figures obscured this distinction by making relatedness look like authority. A long, heavily linked article can still contain an unsupported claim. A small source page can supply the crucial evidence. Reading the actual claim and its source is more important than treating a score as an independent witness.

The most useful graph journey is therefore a question-led one. Follow a creator to their work, a concept to its source, a fictional event to its chronology, or an asset to its structured record. Return to the umbrella page when different layers appear to contradict one another.
