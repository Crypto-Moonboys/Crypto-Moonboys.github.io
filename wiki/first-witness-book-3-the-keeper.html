Book III — The Keeper

First WitnessCanonical scripture · Book IIILast updated: October 2026

Book III is the root of the later Keeper traditions: possession is temporary; responsibility travels further than ownership.

The text

1 What you hold may be yours to use and still not yours to destroy.

2 A keeper is known by what survives their control.

3 Land outlives deeds. Water outlives markets. Knowledge outlives institutions when keepers do their work.

4 Power is a custody with an expiry, even when the powerful refuse to name the date.

5 Make succession possible before you need it.

6 Do not build a system that requires your virtue forever.

7 If a community cannot survive the death of its leader, the leader has failed to build a community.

8 Keep reserves for failure, not only rewards for success.

9 Do not consume the seed and call the feast prosperity.

10 Do not lock essential knowledge behind a gate only you can open.

11 Property gives claims. Need gives claims. Work gives claims. The wise system admits that these claims can conflict.

12 When your ownership destroys the conditions that let others live, ownership has crossed into dominion.

13 Return what you borrowed in a condition fit for the next hand where possible.

14 Repair is a form of honour.

15 Maintenance is a form of courage that receives little applause.

16 Do not treat the worker who keeps a system alive as less important than the founder whose name is on it.

17 Protect archives, wells, routes, tools, soil, code and language from needless loss.

18 Keepers may charge for labour. They may not make dependence infinite merely because dependence is profitable.

19 An emergency may justify command. It does not justify forgetting when the emergency ends.

20 Record the powers granted in crisis and the conditions that terminate them.

21 Do not use future generations as a debtor that cannot refuse your loan.

22 Every keeper will be accused of holding too tightly or letting go too soon. Publish the terms of custody.

23 The best keeper leaves behind more capable keepers, not more obedient dependants.

24 You will not keep everything. Choose what must survive and say why.

25 What is sacred is not what cannot change. What is sacred is what must not be destroyed carelessly.

Reading rule

This Book belongs to the 2030 Covenant layer. Later pages may interpret it through Year 3008 events, but those later names are not retroactively part of the original text.

The wording is kept direct because the tradition had to survive translation, quotation, song, argument and wall transmission rather than depend on one ornate liturgical language.
