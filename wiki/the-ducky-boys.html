⚔️ The DUCKY BOYS

⚔️ Lore 📅 Last updated: October 2026 📂 Lore

The Ducky Boys keep the Year 3008 world working after its grand promises have failed. They mend broken networks, restore damaged equipment, recover readable records and maintain systems whose owners may hate one another. Their mobile workshops operate between the Street Kingdoms and the controlled economy of Block Topia. A repaired pump, a functioning communication line or one more night of cooling is their strongest argument. Their unresolved question is who receives that extra night when there are too few parts, too little time and several clients holding signed contracts.

Adult lore — 21+: These fictional accounts include strong language, adult relationships, intoxication, coercion, violence and lasting loss.

The fixers among the Forty

Within the Forty Paths, the Ducky Boys treat the Keeper as a worker’s instruction: maintain what other people depend upon. Their pride is competence, expressed through dry humour and impatience with speeches that consume power while explaining why power is unimportant. The First Witness commentary also names their weakness: neutrality can become service to whoever controls the parts.

They are a faction culture, not every mechanic in the setting and not a single maintenance ministry. Crews differ over customers, prices and the point at which a job must be refused. Their common name is a reputation carried between workshops. It can bring a caller to the door; it cannot guarantee that the person answering knows how to solve the particular fault.

Bea Socket and the stolen hour

Around Patch Yard, Bea Socket has acquired the nickname Socket Queen. She dislikes the title and still enjoys the moment a room recognises her. Adult workers copy the bright square patch she wears over a repaired sleeve; some add marks for jobs completed, while others simply want to look competent. Her public appeal is the promise that a problem can become a thing somebody knows how to hold. The faction's essential leverage is physical dependence. Beautiful doctrines still need working boilers, fittings, power and people who know where the last usable part went.

During a new local shortage, a parts broker offers Patch Yard an advance in return for priority on its next five jobs. Iven Drift rejects the commitment; another Ducky crew accepts it and starts calling the agreement a sensible queue. A washroom repair then waits behind a patron's ornamental cooling installation. The crew buys better boots and loses two ordinary customers. Its members resent being called corrupt because they have finally earned enough to stop sleeping beside their work. The dependency Bea warned about has become somebody else's comfortable bargain.

Patch Yard also has jokes, romances and petty theft. A returning adult worker finds that somebody has borrowed his best tool and made a filthy joke about its disappearance on the shift board. Bea demands its return, laughs at the joke and gets accused of favouritism because the culprit is useful. Her competence cannot settle every relationship the workshop contains. A later demand for a free emergency hour divides workers who remember being helped from workers tired of seeing help charged to their bodies.

No faction-wide strike or new monopoly is declared. A particular crew has sold future attention, and its clients now experience the price. Ducky skill keeps ordinary life possible while giving skilled workers the opportunity to decide whose ordinary life matters first. The Socket Queen can be admired, relied upon and resented in the same visit.

The work the archive preserves

W81’s dedicated account calls them the Digital Fixers of the Grid. It places them on city life-support contracts, True Bitcoin Fork hardware maintenance, GraffPUNK Paint Path repairs, AllCity arena work and recovery work associated with HODL defence. At Games time they operate armoured mobile hubs and charge for urgent repairs. These are the fictional faction’s occupations, not evidence of a real company’s clients or services.

Their two best-known tool traditions are Digital Duct Tape and Wrench-bots. Their greatest unsolved problem is NULL’s Antichain. The old wiki’s lethal pranksters, rubber-duck decoys and miraculous glitch-born powers preserve a different, exaggerated portrait. Humour remains central to workshop life, but restoration rather than random destruction explains the faction’s place in the wider society.

A world that became a job site

The Triple Fork of 2880 fractured the World Chain’s shared record. Systems could disagree about ownership or identity while still requiring cooling, parts and somebody willing to inspect the damage. In the Ducky account, practical minds emerge from the Datapocalypse recognising a vast unfinished job. The claim that they were collectively formed from all humanity’s best mechanics is an origin tradition, not a complete personal history.

By 2930, the surviving secure regions were fewer than a dozen; the Queens stronghold became Block Topia’s basis. The Ducky role developed across the unequal institutions that followed. Some crews worked beside restored city systems. Others kept outside settlements alive through improvised replacements. The faction’s 3008 culture inherits both the confidence of a successful repair and the knowledge that another failure is already waiting.

What counts as fixed

A Ducky job is not always complete when a machine starts. A door may open but no longer close against rain. A restored archive may display words while losing their order. A communication line may carry messages while confusing who sent them. Experienced fixers therefore argue about function in terms of the people using a system, even when a paying client prefers a simpler completion mark.

Temporary repairs are ordinary. The best crews name their limits and leave the next worker enough information to continue. Less careful crews treat a successful demonstration as the end of responsibility. That divide cuts deeper than the contrast between neat work and ugly work: an ugly patch with known limits can serve a neighbourhood better than a polished repair that nobody can maintain.

Digital Duct Tape and Wrench-bots

Digital Duct Tape is the archive’s name for adaptable patchwork that joins systems never designed to cooperate. Its boast that it “always holds” belongs to faction pride. In practice, the patched object remains dependent on material condition, available power and the skill of the next repairer. A visible join can be a sign of good documentation rather than incompetence.

Wrench-bots are small fictional repair assistants used for delicate work in difficult spaces. They reduce some dangers while introducing others: worn parts, limited perception and the tendency of spectators to mistake movement for understanding. Ducky apprentices are taught to describe what a bot actually inspected. No worker wins respect by blaming a silent machine for a conclusion the worker chose to sign.

Patch Yard

Patch Yard is a shared outside workshop that illustrates the faction’s local organisation. Salvaged tables form separate areas for intake, cleaning, repair and waiting collections. A kettle occupies the most contested surface. Wet coats, uncertain deliveries and clients trying to watch the work make the yard less orderly than its diagrams.

Bea Socket, a shift organiser, assigns bench time and keeps the record of promised returns. She cannot compel every Ducky crew or commandeer the neighbourhood’s supplies. Her authority depends on knowing which worker is exhausted, which part has already been promised and which apparently small job will stop several households from working. She is often blamed for a shortage she merely makes visible.

The Second Shift board

The Second Shift board records work that continued after a client’s celebrated repair: follow-up visits, cleaning, failed replacements and parts borrowed from another job. It exists because a contract can look profitable while quietly exhausting the people who keep returning. Bea uses the board when setting the next day’s queue.

Iven Drift, a repairer and patient teacher, maintains its plain-language notes. A former client calls his return visits evidence of failure; he calls them evidence that the first estimate was incomplete. He is not always right. Sometimes he keeps an unpromising machine alive because he has become attached to solving it, consuming hours that another customer badly needs.

The Amber Ticket

An Amber Ticket marks an item returned to limited service. It states the use the crew observed and the fault still unresolved. At Patch Yard, the ticket stays with the object when it changes hands. Traders dislike it when a caution reduces the resale price. Users appreciate being told why an apparently working device needs attention.

The ticket is a local repair record, not a universal safety certificate or citizenship document. Its value depends on a reachable crew standing behind the description. When a Bally broker asks Bea to remove one before a sale, she offers a new inspection instead. The inspection costs more than the broker expected; the resulting argument lasts longer than the repair.

The city pays, and the city asks

W81 associates the Ducky Boys with maintenance of Block Topia’s life-support, satellite-linked systems and ageing hardware serving the True Bitcoin Fork. A city contract provides dependable materials and access to specialist equipment. It also exposes a crew to supervision, restricted movement and demands that the work serve the authorities’ priorities.

The system that distributes food may share institutional control with the system that constrains a resident. Fixers disagree about whether maintaining the first makes them responsible for the second. Some accept any work that keeps people alive. Others withdraw when access to a repair is conditioned on an unrelated disciplinary measure. Neither position makes them independent of the city’s resources, and both can carry painful consequences.

Repair across outside jurisdictions

Outside work replaces a single demanding authority with many partial ones. A route keeper grants access, a household owns the damaged object, a faction supplies power and another crew claims the salvage. The Nice & Easy Bois sometimes mediate disputes when incompatible claims prevent useful work.

The Ducky Boys and Salvagers depend on one another. Salvagers recover and move the material; Ducky crews make it function in a particular system. Neither job can erase the other’s labour. The archive’s hardened-node projects grow from this collaboration, but their existence does not prove a universal shield against the Antichain or make every outside settlement part of one network.

The Two Boilers stoppage

Patch Yard’s Two Boilers stoppage begins with ordinary overbooking. A wealthy customer has paid to restore a private heated room. A neighbourhood washhouse has waited longer, but its account lacks the deposit needed to reserve the same scarce fitting. Bea initially follows the paid order while Iven argues that the washhouse serves many more people.

Neither boiler is repaired that day. The disagreement exposes that the fitting has already been promised twice by different members of the crew. Bea cancels the false second promise, returns the private client’s advance and accepts a delayed delivery arrangement brokered locally. The washhouse opens first after further waiting; the crew loses the profitable contract. Some workers support the decision and others leave. The story ends with a functioning service and a smaller yard, not unanimous agreement.

Games-season pressure

The Hard Fork Games concentrate the faction’s dilemmas. Damaged arenas, hurried broadcasts and exhausted support crews arrive together. The Blockstars want a spectacle restored before the audience departs. A competitor’s attendant wants one ordinary piece of equipment returned before the next assessment. The greater fee rarely marks the greater need.

Mobile workshops earn premium rates partly because they carry stock into a crowded, politically dangerous place. They also gain information about the city’s vulnerabilities. W81 says some crews sell reports to the Hard Fork Rockers. That trade is a source of internal conflict: knowledge acquired during a repair may endanger the users who trusted the crew. The account preserves the conflict without providing practical intrusion methods.

Two different communities, two kinds of service

Repairs associated with the outside Bitcoin Kids are often improvised and repeatedly interrupted by movement. W81 portrays Alfie Blaze’s rebels as important, frustrating clients who need communication restored under pressure. The Army’s local quartermasters still have to negotiate labour, parts and payment; a rebel cause does not automatically buy unlimited workshop time.

The inside Bitcoin X Kids live within a different engineered and administrative system. Older passages call the inside population “compliant Kids” and reduce their lives to routine maintenance. The reconciled account keeps the identities separate and recognises that an administrative fault can affect a household’s food, schooling or care. Repair work does not establish that a crew has met every child or can remove every restriction.

Tool Return evening

At a local Tool Return evening, borrowed equipment is brought back, missing pieces are named and small repairs are done before another round of work. The ritual is practical and noisy. Someone always returns a better tool than they borrowed; someone else brings a story instead of the missing part. The shared meal begins only when the most urgent disagreement has been heard.

Apprentices learn by explaining an object to a patient listener. A small duck on Iven’s bench provides an audience when nobody is free. The joke preserves the faction’s playful imagery without turning every toy into a weapon. Humour lowers the cost of admitting confusion, though a cruel joke can make a junior worker hide precisely the mistake the yard needs to hear about.

Repairing records without repairing people into obedience

The Ducky Boys recover damaged records associated with the Sacred/Aether memory tradition. That work differs from maintaining Block Topia’s governing chain or reconstructing World Chain material after the 2880 catastrophe. A recovered fragment can be readable without being complete, authentic without explaining its own context and important without proving somebody’s preferred history.

The distinction becomes urgent where memory and personhood overlap. A workshop can restore access without claiming ownership of the person whose memories become accessible. Patch Yard borrows the established distinction between trace, fragment, interpretation and reconstruction when describing uncertain jobs. Iven’s repair notes say what changed; they do not announce that an entire identity has been made whole.

The Unfixed Shelf

NULL’s recursive contradictions undermine the assumptions on which ordinary diagnosis rests. W81 names the hoped-for answer an Anti-Antichain Protocol. Ducky researchers exchange equipment and observations with the Crypto Stoned Boys, whose willingness to doubt tidy explanations can reveal a question the fixers have overlooked. The partnership is awkward: contemplation frustrates workers waiting for a result.

At Patch Yard, the Unfixed Shelf holds documented failures that nobody is allowed to relabel as successful demonstrations. Some items may be repairable later; others are kept because their failures reveal limits in the crew’s understanding. The shelf is not an Antichain containment device. It is an admission that competence includes knowing when a confident answer has run ahead of the evidence.

Duck Ponds and the trickster version

The legacy archive describes Duck Ponds as maze-like refuges, a Laughing Shard that spreads uncontrollable laughter and a faction born directly from a catastrophic glitch. These stories explain why a harmless-looking duck can become an unsettling sign in old street tales. They remain attributed traditions rather than a settled origin or a promise of invulnerability.

The more durable tension is less spectacular. Fixers can be indispensable and still exploit an emergency. A workshop can shelter dissent while keeping an oppressive client’s machinery running. In the 3008 crisis, the Ducky Boys remain occupied with work whose outcome matters before any final revelation arrives. The Final Fork is unresolved; no permanent cure or universal rescue has been completed.

The Naked Wrench evening

Patch Yard's Naked Wrench evening began when Bea Socket arrived at a paid celebration wearing an expensive dress over work trousers, with a repaired wrench hanging from a silk loop. A patron called the effect indecent. Bea said he had hired people to keep his heat working and should be grateful they had dressed for dinner at all. The wrench was 'naked' because it had no decorative casing. The guests made it into a joke about the evening's clothes; the workers made it into a joke about the client's taste.

A few later gatherings kept the name. Adults came in absurd finery marked by one unmistakable tool or repair. Iven brought a borrowed formal collar and a component nobody else could identify without squinting. Bea refused a prize for best costume until its sponsor agreed to pay the actual cleanup crew. She then argued about which costume should win and accused Iven of sentimental favouritism. The evening was both a labour argument and an excuse to look magnificent after a dirty shift.

Craft, courtship and territorial hands

Ducky attraction often begins with competence witnessed at close range: somebody holding a light correctly, knowing when to stop explaining or bringing the part nobody else thought to look for. In Patch Yard's adult social circle, offering a partner access to a prized tool can be a more consequential gesture than promising a permanent room. Nobody's body is payment for access. A lover who borrows without asking may discover that a private quarrel has become a workshop story before lunch.

Bea's vanity concerns her hands. She wants clients to notice what she can do without interpreting skill as permission to demand one more unpaid favour. She can be generous with a stranger's repair and furious when a colleague alters a job she considered hers. Iven believes a repaired object should become less dependent on its original fixer. Bea agrees until a satisfied client starts calling Iven first. Their argument is about a real difference in craft culture: the pleasure of solving a failure versus the pleasure of being indispensable.

The Ducky taboo is a beautiful repair that conceals an unresolved danger from the next worker. Their less admirable tolerance is the ugly workaround everyone has become fond of because replacing it would erase a shared achievement. The Naked Wrench evenings celebrate that attachment. A tool can mean solidarity, flirtation, earned rank and a ridiculous amount of pride before it ever turns another bolt.
