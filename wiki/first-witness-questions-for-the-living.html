Questions for the Living

First WitnessThinking practice · Section 42Last updated: October 2026

The First Witness often teaches by refusing to resolve a problem too quickly. These questions are used because the wrong answer can reveal as much as the right one.

Witness

What evidence would make you admit you were wrong?

Which fact about your own side are you most tempted not to preserve?

When does privacy become secrecy that protects abuse?

Power

Which temporary power would you refuse to give back if you had it?

What rule do you support only because your side currently controls the rule?

How would your system behave under a leader you hate?

Belonging

Can somebody leave your group without becoming an enemy?

Which outsider does your community use to explain every internal failure?

What part of your identity would survive if the group stopped praising it?

Money

At what price would you call exploitation “the market” because you benefited from it?

Which debt deserves repayment and which merely continues because the debtor is weaker?

How much is enough reserve, and when does reserve become hoarding?

Work

What breaks if you disappear for a month?

Who does essential work around you without receiving status?

What skill are you refusing to teach because being needed feels safer?

Technology

Which system could lock you out of your own life?

What have you delegated to a machine that you no longer know how to do?

When does verification become surveillance?

Legacy

What are you trying to preserve because it matters, and what are you preserving because you cannot bear being forgotten?

What should the next generation be allowed to change about your work?

If your descendants must lie about you to honour you, what exactly did you leave them?

The final question

What are you holding that should be passed on, and what are you holding that should be released?
