Proverbs and Street Sayings

First WitnessOral and wall tradition · Section 28Last updated: October 2026

The Books are long enough to argue over. Street culture shortened them. By 3008, many people know First Witness ideas through one-line sayings without knowing which Book produced them.

Common sayings

“A clean record can still be a dirty truth.” — Used when technical accuracy is hiding moral deception.

“If only one key works, everybody is locked.” — Keeper proverb about single points of failure.

“The wall remembers who paid to clean it.” — GraffPUNK saying about official history.

“A fork without food is just an argument.” — Ducky Boy and Salvager version of Book VI.

“Hold the reason, not the wreckage.” — Used after projects, relationships or institutions fail.

“No crown survives a missing archive.” — Chain Scribe warning to rulers.

“If the rule needs darkness, inspect the rule.” — Popular anti-corruption line.

“A score is a tool until it learns your name.” — Late World Chain-era proverb.

“Every safe wall has an inside and an outside.” — Used in Block Topia arguments.

“The loudest witness still needs evidence.” — Common courtroom and street saying.

“You can leave the empire and keep its furniture.” — Mocking version of the Fork's warning about reproducing old systems.

“Your enemy has a mother somewhere.” — Common Blood street teaching, sometimes dismissed as sentimental until war begins.

“Repair gets no statue.” — Maintenance crews use it with pride and resentment.

“A backup nobody can restore is a prayer, not a backup.” — Chain Scribe technical proverb.

“Three copies, two hands, one truth still uncertain.” — Warning that redundancy protects records, not interpretation.

“Do not make a throne from a temporary chair.” — Emergency-power proverb.

“A prophet who needs silence needs more than faith.” — Used against charismatic leaders who cannot tolerate challenge.

“The dead need memory, not obedience.” — Unwritten Block funeral saying.

“If everybody agrees too quickly, count who is missing.” — Council proverb.

“Freedom still needs someone to fix the roof.” — Street Kingdom saying.

Wall versions

Graffiti culture makes sayings shorter again. “NO SINGLE KEEPER” becomes NSK. “Hold what must survive us” becomes HOLD IT PAST YOU. “The wall remembers” becomes simply WALL KNOWS.

Some compressed phrases eventually outlive their original meaning. In several Street Kingdoms, “EMPTY CHAIR” is sprayed beside abandoned civic buildings even when nobody remembers the council parable that produced it.

The danger of a good slogan

The First Witness itself warns against slogans because compressed language removes conditions. “Hold” can become stubbornness. “Fork” can become permanent rebellion. “No single keeper” can become an excuse for nobody taking responsibility.

One commentary advises: when a saying becomes too easy to shout, go back and read the paragraph it escaped from.
