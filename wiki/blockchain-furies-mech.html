Life on the Waterfront

The Blockchain Furies Mech carries equipment for public claims hearings. Its crew is drawn to disputes that leave a gap between the tidy record and a person’s damaged property or lost wages. The white chassis has a distinctive Furies badge and enough space to bring documents and a fold-out table to a place both parties can reach.

The edge of a receipt

The operator began as the person asked to load records after the people arguing about them had gone home. They kept finding good-looking sequences that started a shift too late to explain the damage. An older checker taught them to ask which person had been released from duty before the missing receipt turned up. Some of the mech’s first folding tables were built from a discarded supervisor’s desk. They still carry one wedge to steady the legs on wet stones.

Danny Rankz values the crew’s ability to run a hearing somewhere witnesses can reach. The operator likes that work and has little patience for the assumption that the person holding the pen gets the last word. They leave the first page of each notebook blank because they got tired of opening a customer’s business with the wrong question. They will lend one to anybody who promises to put the pencil back.

The missing handover

One evening its crew arrived with a convincing sequence of freight receipts. A worker standing at the edge of the table pointed out that the sequence started after a load had already changed hands. The crew reopened the case. The worker agreed to give evidence there after a second person offered to cover her shift.

Off duty

The operator dislikes the sound of a stamp hitting empty paper. Off duty they spend time binding discarded reports into blank notebooks. The first page of each is left for someone else to write their own account.

An account outside the queue

During the trial at the Ninth Berth the mech put Danny’s table outside the line of waiting ears. The operator thought people would be grateful for the privacy. One worker told them it also made the table look shut. Moving it half a pace and asking Gentleman Bear to wait by it was enough to make the next person approach. The returning stallholder got part of her property back and was able to file her claim without giving the whole queue her name.

The operator will ask that future trials budget time for a witness who needs their shift covered. They hope the record will make arguing for the time easier. The next employer might just refuse to send the witness. Ask the operator what a chain of receipts proves and they will usually ask which event happened before the first one.
