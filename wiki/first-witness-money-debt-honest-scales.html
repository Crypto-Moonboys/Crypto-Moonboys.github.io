Money, Debt and Honest Scales

First WitnessEconomic ethics · Section 33Last updated: October 2026

The Covenant does not condemn money. It condemns pretending that because something has a price, the price is the whole truth about it.

Honest scales

The image of unequal scales appears across ancient cultures because trade creates one of morality's simplest temptations: use information differently depending on which side of the counter you stand.

First Witness merchants traditionally disclose the unit, fee, timing and condition of settlement. A technically true price hidden behind unavoidable charges is treated as a crooked scale.

Debt remembers

Debt is memory turned economic: a record that something given has not yet been returned. This makes debt legitimate and dangerous. The more perfectly a system remembers, the easier it becomes for one mistake to follow a person forever.

Several traditions therefore distinguish debts that preserve fair exchange from debts that have become mechanisms of permanent control.

The Release Ledger

Some communities developed periodic review days on which old debts were inspected for exploitation, impossibility, mistaken identity, accumulated penalties or social harm. Not every debt was cancelled. Every old debt had to justify why it still deserved enforcement.

Finance Guild orthodoxies often oppose broad release customs; reformers argue that a ledger unable to distinguish repayment from lifelong extraction has forgotten why settlement exists.

Reserves

Book III's instruction not to eat the seed becomes a financial rule: keep enough reserve that a single bad week does not force a desperate decision. The idea applies to households as much as treasuries.

Reserves are not worshipped. Hoarding everything while neighbours starve can violate Keeper duties just as surely as spending everything today can violate duties to tomorrow.

The price of desperation

A contract freely signed under extreme need may still be exploitative. The tradition asks whether one party's emergency became the other party's business model.

This idea creates endless faction arguments because nearly every market contains unequal urgency. The line between opportunity and predation is rarely as clean as either side wants.

What money cannot settle

Some wrongs can be compensated financially but not reduced to compensation. Paying for damage does not purchase innocence. Funding a memorial does not buy ownership of the dead person's story.

The honest scale measures what money can measure and refuses to pretend the rest weighs nothing.
