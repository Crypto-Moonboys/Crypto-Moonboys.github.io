Children and the First Lessons

First WitnessTeaching tradition · Section 44Last updated: October 2026

Children's teaching became one of the hardest First Witness problems: how do you pass on values without turning inheritance into obedience?

The Broken Cup

A child breaks a cup and hides the pieces. The parent asks two questions: “Did you break it?” and “Can we repair it?”

The lesson is that truth comes before punishment because repair is impossible while everybody is protecting a false story.

The Borrowed Marker

Two children share one marker. One uses all the ink drawing a huge name. The other complains there is nothing left.

The teacher does not say “sharing means equal use every time.” The teacher asks what the first child should have considered before using a shared resource.

The Three Stories

Three children describe the same playground argument. Each story contains something true and something missing. The class is asked to build the fullest account without deciding one child must therefore be entirely good and another entirely bad.

This is the child's version of the Concordance.

The Door Key

A parent shows the child where an emergency key is kept. The child asks, “Why tell me if you are still here?”

The answer is: “Because keeping you safe includes teaching you what to do without me.”

The New Rule

A class has a rule nobody remembers creating. The teacher asks whether the rule is still useful. Some children defend it because it is old. Others reject it because it is old.

The lesson is that age is evidence of survival, not proof of wisdom or proof of stupidity.

The Quiet Question

Children are taught one sentence that can be used with teachers, parents and faction elders: “Can you explain why?”

Adults are not required to debate every urgent instruction. But a culture that never returns later to explain itself teaches obedience rather than judgement.

The first anti-prophecy lesson

Many schools teach children that no hero is above questions. Even admired figures are introduced with at least one mistake, doubt or failure.

The practice became especially important after Child of Fire devotion intensified around Alfie Blaze. A hero children are forbidden to question is considered a dangerous teaching tool.

The lesson teachers are meant to remember

The First Lessons are not supposed to produce miniature adults who can recite doctrine. They are meant to produce children who can tell the truth, share responsibility, ask questions, repair mistakes and eventually disagree intelligently with their teachers.

A final teacher's note says: If they only repeat you, the lesson is unfinished.
