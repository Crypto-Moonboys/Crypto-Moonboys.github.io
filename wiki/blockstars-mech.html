Life on the Waterfront

The Blockstars Mech began as a touring platform that could carry its own lamps. Its gold and red frame still looks ready to roll into a broadcast. On the Waterfront it brings lighting for small shows, announcements and recordings made amid forklift noise and wind off the water.

The work after the credits

The mech’s operator liked the idea of a touring stage until they realised all their memories of touring ended with themselves waiting for a lift home beside a dead lamp. The opportunity to bring their own light was as much about getting safely clear as putting themselves in the picture. They began rehearsing the closing credits before the first number after missing the people who had given them that lift.

Old tickets keep them company on the dashboard. They can tell Teddy what a show sounded like from a cheap seat and are less good at telling him when it is time to turn the microphone off. Working with the Boombox has made them more willing to stand in a dark corner and listen. They still feel a professional itch to point a lamp at the loudest person.

Who gets the closing credit

A promoter once credited its operator with staging a whole evening alone. Three people spent the hour after the show coiling cable and carrying lamps to the repair room. Their names never made the broadcast. The operator asks crews to prepare the closing credits while there is still time to check who is present.

Off duty

A service panel holds ticket stubs from shows the operator attended as an audience member. They prefer the ones with the seat number torn away. They can remember the performance without remembering whether they were supposed to sit somewhere better.

The lamps stayed on

At the Ninth Berth they left working light across the cable run after setting the lamps on Teddy’s stage. That left the lighting crew visible while it was still being paid and the time sheet could still be amended. The operator heard one surname corrected from the floor and remembered a show where they had let the same error go because the presenter was already moving through the credits.

Next they want an agreed hour for packing up, including the cost of a second lamp if the road is still in use. A sponsor has already asked whether a prettier image of the stage could be arranged with an earlier shutdown on the service side. They know why the shot appeals. Ask them about the best gig they’ve ever seen and they will describe it from the seat they actually paid for.
