Life on the Waterfront

The Moongirls Mech runs supplies between service crews on Lumina’s Waterfront. Its bright pink and green panels are easy to identify when it arrives with clean containers or a replacement fitting. Its operator was taught to record who receives a load because a storekeeper cannot replace what a record says is still on the shelf.

The room named on the slip

The operator spent her first weeks with the Moongirls stores trying to make the stockroom match the picture of it she carried in her head. An assessor took her on the last collection of the night and made her wait by the chair behind the clerk until two people arrived with the same request. She understood later why the ledger was read aloud one line at a time. She began folding models of public rooms small enough to fit in the pocket where she had kept her old room list.

She likes ZARAH8’s habit of walking to the equipment before deciding what the label means. She finds it harder to admit when a slip she wrote is the reason somebody has to make that walk. Gentleman Bear saves her blanks from discontinued pads. She uses those for models, with enough of the original rule printed on the wall to remind her who had to ask for a place to sit.

The wrong service room

A worker once collected the same spare twice after an early receipt named the wrong service room. The operator had been in a hurry and filled in the destination from memory. The corrected receipt stayed pinned above the bench well after the spare was returned. The operator still carries blank slips and gives anyone time to read their own before signing.

Off duty

She builds small paper models of overlooked public rooms, including the chair behind the clerk who does the evening count. The models go on a shelf that is gradually beginning to resemble the city by the places where people wait.

A spare lamp goes upstairs

The new lamp’s slip sent the stores to the closed lower room. She recognised the mistake before anybody collected it. In front of both shifts she asked to have the old line struck through and the upper bay written next to it. She worried the depot would treat the correction as a mark against her delivery record. Zarah asked her to leave both versions legible so the night shift could see why the route had changed.

She has started a scale model of the upper landing with two spots for a custodian’s key and a chair she has yet to finish. She wants the depot to keep the reserve stock near the people who work after closing. It will mean finding space somebody else will have to give up. Ask her about the spare lamp and she may ask which room you would go to first if all you had was the slip.
