⚔️ The SALVAGERS

⚔️ Lore 📅 Last updated: October 2026 📂 Lore

The Salvagers keep Year 3008 settlements supplied with recovered materials, usable equipment, power and cooling. Their work connects ruined infrastructure to everyday survival, while their trade forces constant choices about ownership, fair payment, dangerous journeys and the people hidden by a successful delivery.

The people who keep the world supplied

The Salvagers recover, sort, repair and transport the material resources on which Year 3008 life depends. Their dedicated archive account calls them the digital logisticians of the Grid and insists that power, cooling and usable hardware remain necessary beneath the world's elaborate images of abundance. The faction's characteristic achievement is something unglamorous arriving where it is needed before a more celebrated system stops working.

They operate across the Street Kingdoms and in relationships with Block Topia, wealthy enclaves, workshops and competing faction camps. Independence does not mean isolation from customers. A crew can dislike a client's politics and still need its payment to keep equipment working. That practical dependence is one reason the faction is described as a necessity in some accounts and as amoral in others.

The First Witness commentary connects them with Book III's Keeper: waste becomes survival when someone knows how to recover value. It also names their danger precisely. Salvage logic becomes cruel when applied to living people, treating a person as a useful part, an inconvenient load or material to be claimed because somebody else abandoned them.

Leda Gauge and the copper hand

Leda Gauge wears a copper-coloured handprint on her work coat, a local mark copied by adults who want to be recognised as people who have actually handled the material. Some copies are earned through Rattle Yard's work; others are bought. She can tell the difference in a conversation and occasionally lets a flattering mistake stand. The Salvagers' glamour is arrival: the component everyone declared impossible to find, brought in dirty and usable enough to change a week.

Their decisive role is the material limit under every other faction's claim. A commander, performer or archivist can promise almost anything before asking for a replacement seat. Leda can say there is no seat. She can also choose which customer hears about the recovered one first. After Cinder Shed, Etta's adapted task remains limited and paid work still competes for the bench. An adult patron offers a high price for a recovered decorative fitting; an ordinary workshop wants its functional casing. Suri takes the patron's job to pay wages and cannot pretend the workshop's delay is imaginary.

Workers divide the extra money, complain about the patron and buy things they needed. A later customer brings a gift for Leda so that his name will be remembered next time. She accepts the food and states no priority. He behaves afterwards as though one has been purchased. The copper hand becomes an icon of competence and a target for pressure because scarcity makes even a careful worker's attention valuable. The Salvagers can keep a world moving without being outside its bargaining.

Scarcity after the Triple Fork

The World Chain's catastrophic Triple Fork occurred in 2880, followed by Chainfire and the wider Great Unravelling. The collapse of trusted records did not make every physical object disappear. It made ownership, maintenance, routes and supplies unreliable at the same time. A machine could remain standing while the institutions required to operate it had failed.

The Salvagers' dedicated account says their consciousnesses came from engineers, heavy equipment operators and logistics specialists who understood that problem. This is a post-collapse origin tradition within the unresolved Genesis Kernel complex, not a verified census of restored minds. It does not prove that every worker arrived through the same process or that practical knowledge belongs exclusively to the faction.

By 2930 fewer than a dozen secure regions remained; Queens became the basis of Block Topia. The Salvagers' present work spans the later settlements, ruins and routes around that changing world. Accounts disagree about the extent of its digital and physical layers. The faction's material work establishes dependence on maintenance without settling a universal theory of consciousness or reducing the Street Kingdoms to an abandoned city basement.

The Salvage Code

The archive's Salvage Code measures work through physical effort and verifiable yield. To its adherents, a promised fortune matters less than a delivery that can be used. A bundle of recovered fibre, a serviceable seal or a dependable cooling component can keep people alive while a magnificent theoretical resource remains inaccessible.

The code produces skill and pride, but yield alone can become a misleading measure. A crew that returns with less because it helped an injured traveller may have done better work than a crew that brought every item home. A difficult inspection that prevents a dangerous purchase can produce no visible pile at all. Salvagers argue over whether their own measures recognise these forms of effort.

Their concise speech often mixes quantities, condition, location and dry humour. A boast may receive a question about who carried the load. Ornamental equipment is not forbidden, but a decoration that obstructs work is likely to be removed. The humour is affectionate when everyone can answer back and cruel when a newcomer is expected to endure a joke that a senior worker would not accept.

Life at Rattle Yard

Rattle Yard is a local Salvager sorting and repair place beside a route used by several work companies. Its name comes from the uneven sound of arrivals over patched ground. It is not the faction's headquarters. Crews use it because enough people can repair things there, compare information and provide somewhere to wait when a journey cannot continue.

Suri Clamp coordinates work at the yard. Her authority comes from knowing what is in progress and who has accepted responsibility for it. She can postpone a local departure when essential work is unfinished, but she cannot order every Salvager company to obey. Crews that reject her judgement can leave; they must then answer for their own promises to customers and companions.

The yard's living spaces show how rarely work ends at the end of a shift. A damaged carrier occupies room that someone wanted for a meal, and a late arrival wakes a household that starts before dawn. Shared washing, food preparation and quiet corners are negotiated repeatedly. Rattle Yard survives because people do that negotiation, not because practical workers have somehow escaped the inconveniences of community.

Leda Gauge and the sorting floor

Leda Gauge sorts incoming material at Rattle Yard. She distinguishes things ready for use, things worth repairing, things retained for study and things whose condition remains uncertain. The distinctions are working judgements, not immutable descriptions. A piece considered worthless can become useful when another worker recognises it; an impressive arrival can prove too costly to restore.

Leda's skill includes refusing a celebrated find. A damaged object may require more power, time and replacement parts than the yard can spare. The worker who carried it home has reason to resent that conclusion, especially after a dangerous journey. Leda tries to explain the cost without belittling the labour already spent. Not every disappointment is resolved politely.

Sorters also hear claims that a find belongs to a lost household or still serves a nearby group. An abandoned appearance does not settle ownership or need. Leda keeps disputed objects out of ordinary sale until the limited information available can be compared. That delay ties up space and irritates customers, but it prevents the yard's haste from turning someone else's absence into permission.

The Return Weight custom

Return Weight is Rattle Yard's way of recognising what a journey consumed as well as what it brought back. A crew's report includes damaged equipment, unpaid assistance, exhausted reserves and work now owed to companions. The practice is local and imperfect. Its purpose is to prevent the arrival pile from becoming the only story told about an expedition.

Enno Pike, a carrier operator, pressed for the custom after a celebrated recovery left two ordinary carts unusable. The recovered components drew applause while their transport costs disappeared into the next crew's budget. Enno was accused of spoiling a good story. Suri backed the change because the next departure had already been delayed by the missing carts.

The new report did not make every journey profitable. It made some unprofitable journeys easier to justify and some apparently profitable ones harder to defend. Returning with a companion could be recorded as part of the task rather than a private detour. Even then, arguments continued over who should absorb the cost. Naming a burden is only the beginning of sharing it.

The Cold Cart delay

The Cold Cart delay began with a promised cooling delivery that arrived late at a neighbourhood service room. An important fitting had been judged usable at departure and failed before the journey was complete. Enno brought the load back rather than risking the rest of the route, leaving the intended recipients with less time to arrange an alternative.

The household representatives were angry that the first message called it a minor delay. At Rattle Yard, minor meant the equipment could probably be repaired; at the destination, it meant people had already changed work, care and sleeping arrangements in expectation of the delivery. Suri had to recognise that the same word had concealed different consequences.

The yard paid part of the alternative transport cost and corrected its account of the incident. The failed fitting remained available for examination, but nobody claimed one object explained the whole failure. Pressure to depart, the inspection judgement and the misleading message each mattered. Cold Cart is remembered as a case where repair of the equipment preceded repair of the relationship.

Machines, skill and limits

W81 names Crawler-Bots for ruined tunnels, Geothermal Tappers for energy recovery and Anti-Debris Code for moving obstructions. These are fictional equipment traditions associated with the faction. Their presence does not mean every crew owns every device, that abandoned infrastructure can be entered safely or that a phrase in a chronicle supplies a complete engineering design.

The practical work depends on people recognising limits. A carrier sized for one passage may not suit another. A part recovered from a sophisticated installation may be useless without the missing system around it. Work can stop because of tired operators, poor information or the absence of one mundane replacement component. No amount of admiration for improvisation changes those constraints.

The Salvagers' relationship with the Ducky Boys follows from that overlap of materials and repair expertise. The source account describes hardened, self-contained nodes built together during the antichain crisis. Collaboration is more credible than a universal claim that one faction alone understands the physical world. Each depends on work the other cannot simply replace.

Block Topia and unequal customers

The dedicated account gives the Salvagers major contracts supplying the Crypto Moongirls with power and coolant, and work maintaining the High Hats' Cloud Palaces and the Finance Guild's Audit Towers. It also describes Squeaky Pink scrutiny of the routes. Those specific architectural and contract traditions remain attributed; they are not a complete verified plan of every city installation.

A wealthy customer can pay enough to keep a work company equipped while demanding priority over everyone else. A city order may arrive with predictable settlement and intrusive conditions. An outside settlement may need the same goods more urgently but lack a payment the company can use. Salvagers who claim to be above politics still choose among those pressures each time they accept a load.

The True Bitcoin Fork is Block Topia's protected identity and governance anchor. Supplying the city does not merge it with the older Sacred/Aether-Chain tradition or the collapsed World Chain. Nor does taking a city contract make a Salvager crew part of the Moongirls. The relationship is one of dependence and bargaining, sometimes cooperation, and sometimes a dispute over whose survival takes precedence.

Routes, protection and the cost of passage

The archive describes Aztec Raider attacks on recovered resources, Tuskon OG protection and expensive warnings purchased from Information Mercenaries. A Gasless Ghost account adds protection of Salvager supply lines. These different stories describe possible relationships rather than a single permanent escort arrangement that makes every route secure.

Protection consumes the same supply it is meant to preserve. Guards need equipment and payment; a warning may arrive too late to matter; a safer journey may take longer than the customer can afford. A company that promises certainty can end by concealing the very doubts its workers need to discuss. Enno prefers an honestly limited route estimate to a confident speech that leaves nobody responsible for the return.

The Nomad Bears can provide local knowledge and also dispute recovery from places they value. Shiver Cut, where observers and a work party argued over fittings in a damaged passage, remains an example of negotiated use. A guide's familiarity does not confer ownership, and a recovery crew's tools do not prove that every untouched object ought to be removed.

The Unclaimed Shelf and the danger of salvage thinking

At Rattle Yard, the Unclaimed Shelf holds small personal belongings recovered with ordinary material. A worn token, a repaired cup or a fragment of labelled clothing may have little sale value and considerable meaning to someone absent. Leda separates those objects because the person who can identify them may arrive long after the company that found them has moved on.

The name is deliberately imperfect. Unclaimed means nobody at the yard has established a claim, not that no claim exists. Some pieces must eventually be moved or disposed of when space runs out. Keeping everything forever would consume the very room needed for present work. The yard records those decisions and accepts that preservation has limits without pretending the loss becomes meaningless.

The harsher question concerns people. A rescued traveller is not payment for the transport they required; an injured worker is not a failed tool. Suri's insistence on those boundaries has cost the yard attractive contracts. She has also had to be challenged when she described an experienced worker only in terms of the skill the yard could no longer use. The First Witness warning applies within respectable workshops as well as among open predators.

Miners, Rockers and rebel demand

The Rugpull Miners are a particularly bitter enemy because deception can consume real work while leaving nothing recoverable. The dedicated Salvager account describes sharing information with the Blockchain Furies. That does not make every Fury action proportionate or every accusation correct. A worker needs the missing payment or component back; a spectacular punishment may not provide either.

Relations with the Hard Fork Rockers are more ambivalent. Salvagers respect their engineering and resent the repair burdens created by ideological rupture. A new system may be necessary, but its advocates still have to answer who keeps services functioning through the change. Conversely, maintenance can become an excuse to preserve an abusive arrangement forever. Keeping a machine running does not settle whether it should retain its power over people.

The archive portrays Alfie's outside Bitcoin Kid Army as an unpredictable demand on local resources. That is a supplier's perspective, not a complete account of the Kids' contribution. The separate Bitcoin X Kids remain inside Block Topia. Calling their maintenance passive in an old source does not erase the real work of their households, teachers and service workers described elsewhere in the current history.

NULL and the unfinished supply line

NULL's antichain threat reaches the Salvagers through accounts of erratic failures in power and cooling systems. The dedicated text interprets these as physical consequences of recursive contradiction. Workers can observe a failure without proving that NULL caused it; worn components and neglected maintenance do not disappear from the world when a new enemy becomes famous.

The source says the faction reserves solar cells, hydrogen storage and cabling for the Final Fork. Its further claim that whoever controls power will control the outcome is a faction expectation, not a settled future. Reserves may preserve a neighbourhood or become leverage against it. The old Keeper question returns in material form: who can use what has been saved, and who decides when the emergency is real?

Collective HODL mobilisation can increase the demand on Salvager crews while reducing the time available for ordinary maintenance. Keeping civilian needs visible is therefore part of defence. HODL Warriors and HODL X Warriors remain distinct traditions. The Salvagers' contribution to a shared emergency does not resolve that relationship or complete the Final Fork in the Year 3008 present.

A recovered casing and a missing seat

During the White Shutter, recovered pump casings become more valuable because new stock cannot cross the closed Exchange. A casing is not a complete pump. Three Pump's repair still needs a ceramic valve seat produced and inspected on the city line. The Salvagers can clean, compare and test recovered material. They cannot turn a worn seat into an accepted precision part by relabelling it as salvage or invoking the urgency of the clinic route.

A customer offers extra payment for an immediate certification. Refusal loses the sale while preserving the distinction between found, tested and accepted. Less careful stock remains available elsewhere, and some buyers take it. The Salvagers are neither the only resourceful crew nor automatically immune to the temptation. Their bench record makes the decision examinable after the reopened route delivers the missing manufactured part.

The recovered casing helps the repair succeed once that part arrives. Guild-backed money has paid for the part; a Ducky worker has fitted it; a carrier has delivered it under the compact. No single crew manufactures the whole success. The Salvagers keep their contribution and the outstanding payment separate. The reopening also makes some urgently purchased old stock less valuable, leaving a trader with a loss that the earlier heroic recovery account had not anticipated.

Ghost finds and living inheritance

Earlier stories tell of data spirits guiding caravans, the Ghost Mint, the Eternal Cache and a Ghost Ledger containing ancient avatar material. The Node Purge of Cycle 28 describes a recovered server changing a caravan's fortunes. These remain relic and caravan traditions. Their exact calendars, inventories and powers are uncertain, and no discovery establishes a right to overwrite another person's identity.

Such tales persist because a salvage arrival can change a local future. A usable component can reopen a workshop; a recognisable personal object can reconnect a household with its history. The mistake is to assume every find must be a world-changing treasure before it deserves attention. Most of Rattle Yard's useful arrivals will never acquire a legend.

Leda keeps a plain repaired container beside the sorting floor. Enno used it to return the failed Cold Cart fitting, and later crews borrow it when an object needs careful attention rather than a triumphant unveiling. It is neither sacred nor uniquely powerful. Its small history reminds the yard that bringing something back includes being willing to explain why it did not work.

The Cinder Shed load and the hand no invoice replaces

Suri Clamp is 45 and Leda Gauge is 41 in the Black Dividend account. Rattle Yard supplies recovered fittings for a small carrier's hire through Cinder Shed. The job is useful, ordinary work. Etta Lorn, 34, accepts it because the agreed wages can cover a room payment and because the alternative Night Cover charge would eat the company's remaining margin. She is not told she has signed up to become an example in a protector's revenge.

The fittings burn when Rovan's crew attacks the shed. Etta gets out with severe burns and later cannot use one hand as she did before. Salvager testimony records which goods the yard sent, who accepted the load and which parts had been checked. A correctly sorted fitting is still worthless to the person whose workshop never receives it. Leda hates how often a sound inspection mark survives beside a wrecked obligation.

Kesh offers Rattle Yard a replacement hire through another carrier. Suri accepts a limited portion because the yard has wages due and usable stock waiting. She refuses to describe the old load as never received. Etta hears that the new work has begun and says, 'Good. Tell the new parts my fucking hand says hello.' Suri answers that the workers need paying. It is the truth she has ready and not the answer Etta needed.

The yard contributes materials and two paid work intervals to Etta's household arrangement. It cannot recreate the destroyed load, force the patron's reserve into their hands or promise that her former capacity will return. Suri keeps earning from freight that still touches Kesh's buyers. The help matters; the continuing trade matters too. The yard has not found a clean outside economy while everybody else was being compromised.

The Borrowed Grip and the worker who will not pose

An adult maker adapts a holding grip for one of Etta's remaining tasks. She learns enough to sort certain smaller components with breaks. Some days the arrangement is useful and some days pain and fatigue make it useless. The new piece has not reversed her injuries or made the old loading job appropriate. Leda puts a changed work description beside the bench instead of pretending the worker has been fully restored by workshop ingenuity.

A buyer offers money for an image of Etta using the grip beside a statement about rebuilding stronger. The fee could pay for several weeks of necessities. Etta refuses the statement and offers to show the task with its actual limits. The buyer loses interest. Sella is furious at the lost money for an evening, then ashamed of having wanted it. Etta says she wanted it too. Their argument does not require one wife to be nobler than the other.

Leda initially saves Etta a bench interval another worker expected. The other worker has a painful wrist and no dramatic attack story. Being pushed out leaves his paid sorting unfinished. When he challenges the change, Leda restores a smaller shared interval and loses some of the help she wanted to offer. The yard's scarce adaptations have begun ranking injuries by public familiarity, even though its keepers know better than to call that fairness.

Etta once pocketed three tools during an unsettled hire and returned none. She admits it when a worker recognises two among the objects she is selling. The old offence does not make her burn deserved or invalidate the unpaid wages. It does mean the return negotiation is unpleasant and involves someone with a real earlier grievance. Injured people do not become spotless stock for everybody else's sympathy.

Night money at Rattle Yard

Sevrin's account of Teren's killing reaches Rattle Yard through the receiving hosts. A departing crew asks whether a night guard might still be worth the fee. Suri says the crew must be one they can refuse later. A worker answers that the later refusal is exactly when people start using force. The yard has shutters, ordinary watches and neighbours with their own jobs; it has no effortless substitute for the guards whose work it now distrusts.

One night a watch worker admits that he misses Rovan's crew because he slept when they were outside. Another says he could not sleep while they were there. Both had been living in the same yard. Leda asks which intervals need covering now and is told she has made the whole thing sound like a rota. She agrees. A rota is still better than asking the most frightened person to stay awake forever for free.

Etta and Sella occasionally eat at the yard after a paid interval. Their conversation includes absurd buyers, bodies that ache and a neighbour who claimed his coat was a family relic after three people recognised their own patches. Etta's best insult is reserved for a patron who wants urgent work priced as a charitable opportunity: 'May your arse acquire its own invoice.' The laugh belongs to adults sharing a meal; it is not a guarantee that the next hire is safe.

Rattle Yard's original Cold Cart, Unclaimed Shelf and missing ceramic-seat limits remain. This later history adds what useful labour can become under a hired threat. A repairer may keep a rival's equipment functioning, a caretaker may keep a partner alive and a company may stay open. Those achievements do not make the people taking a dividend from fear less powerful. The yard keeps working because its workers need another day, not because it has reached an acceptable ending.

The Second Funeral dance

A salvage crew's adult return celebration in the earlier 3008 season used fittings recovered from a disused workroom as percussion. Someone called it the Second Funeral because the building's contents were making a final magnificent noise before being divided into ordinary repairs. Leda liked the phrase until an adult former occupant asked why strangers were dancing on the useful remains of a room his friends had maintained.

The crew invited him to join the meal and show which pieces he recognised. He attended, enjoyed some of the company and claimed one particular panel as property he had never relinquished. The crew's account had treated the whole load as abandoned. Other receivers had already paid for parts. Leda returned the contested panel and lost that sale; two colleagues accused her of letting a good story alter the inventory. The man left without thanking the dancers for remembering his former room more entertainingly than he did.

Magnificence made from somebody else’s yesterday

Salvager adults wear things whose second purpose can be seen: polished work scraps, a repaired fastener displayed like a jewel, a beautiful surface still carrying an old instruction. Their parties are loud because recovered objects make excellent instruments and everybody has an opinion about what an object used to be. They desire people who can identify value without pretending it was always meant for them.

Leda's copper hand can look like authority in a room full of recovered metal. She enjoys the admiration and hates a patron who treats her as a picturesque explanation of how his new room became affordable. Her colleagues disagree about whether recognising the previous keeper of an object makes their work more honest or indefinitely less secure. A thing can be genuinely discarded, unofficially surrendered under pressure or simply difficult for an absent owner to retrieve.

The Second Funeral continues as a celebration of useful transformation. Some hosts now leave time for recognisable claims before the loudest part begins. Others regard that pause as a sentimental tax. Salvagers give the world material it cannot simply commission anew, and their swagger comes from knowing how many elegant institutions depend on what they carried back. Their fear is having yesterday's useful wreck become somebody else's untouchable relic just as they have finally made it feed today's workers.

Etta at the receiving bench

Last Loading belongs to the later Black Dividend sequence after Split Current Night. At Rattle Yard, Etta is doing narrower work than the hire that brought her to Cinder Shed. She can compare selected small fittings with breaks. She cannot restore the destroyed load or guarantee the same capacity every morning. Her place at the final receiving bench follows this established work. It is paid employment she accepts, not an organiser moving an injured woman into danger to make the yard's refusal look important.

Leda wants to keep Etta away from Nine Rung and says the yard can send somebody healthier. Etta hears another person replacing her while claiming to protect her. Their argument ends with a smaller task, a planned relief worker and an actual stopping point. Leda is still frightened and stops calling fear a complete explanation of somebody else's choice. Etta is still angry and accepts the relief because wanting the task does not make fatigue a lie.

The tools for the job are ordinary inspected fittings and a limited comparison support. No newly recovered machine heals Etta or defeats Night Cover. The Borrowed Grip has not become a weapon or a proof that all disabled workers can resume their old jobs. Its most useful contribution is allowing a narrower piece of work to be paid honestly. Etta likes the sound of the accepted task less than the old loading work and wants it anyway.

Before departure, Sella asks Etta to say whether she would still choose the job if nobody were watching. Etta says yes, then admits that being watched makes her want it more. Sella says she has the same problem with refusing the private settlement. They kiss while a sorting worker complains that the doorway is needed for paid traffic. Etta tells him to invoice the fucking romance. The laugh does not remove the decision either woman has made.

Rattle Yard's price at Dry Supper

Suri Clamp supplies only the modest load the new receiving advances can purchase. She does not quietly turn the whole yard into a relief fund. A repair customer whose interval is postponed complains that the famous burn has acquired another claim on everybody else's bench. He is right about the delay and wrong when he describes Etta as the person who chose it for him. Suri has made the scheduling decision. She tells him the lost interval is her responsibility and keeps the payment question open instead of asking Etta to apologise for surviving visibly.

Several yard workers dislike the new receiving plan because they had slept better when Night Cover stood outside. Others refuse any watch funded through Rovan's old terms. Their shared meal is loud, vicious and full of people reminding one another of earlier cowardice. Suri pays the named replacement watches for particular intervals and refuses the suggestion that anyone with an objection should volunteer overnight. The room begins making more practical offers when free moral superiority stops buying it another person's labour.

Sella takes carrying work under her own accepted hours. A crew member assumes her wages are part of Etta's support arrangement. She makes him correct the booking. At home she is a wife; at the yard she has a load and a price. Mixing the two is convenient for people who want the household to be grateful twice for one payment. Etta initially jokes that the new entry makes them look terribly respectable. Sella says respectable people appear able to stop working when their bodies hurt.

After the gate fight, the relief worker finishes Etta's task and Sella misses work she had intended to complete. The load still arrives. Rattle Yard pays its actual obligations and refuses a publicity line saying ingenuity has restored two injured women. Suri keeps the remaining stock for work the next day. A yard that has taken a stand must still decide which less famous delay to tell an impatient customer about first.

The Borrowed Curtain return

In the weeks after Last Loading, Etta and Sella buy limited help from a neighbour for tasks neither can now manage reliably. They also provide company, gossip and food in return when they can. The agreement is not indefinite care bought by a heroic debt. Etta wants the neighbour to stay longer on a bad day and resents paying the additional time. Sella asks why people doing essential work should accept precisely the gratitude they refused from the patrons. Etta says she can be a hypocrite in her own room without opening a fucking faction about it.

Leda visits with a useful smaller fitting and sees the new curtain fall from its support. All three laugh before deciding who can actually put it back. The curtain is attractive, bought because the women wanted something attractive, and incapable of proving their household has recovered. Leda has brought no speech. That makes the visit one of the easier ones. Etta asks after the worker whose earlier bench interval was displaced and hears that he still complains about it when the story comes up.

A buyer asks the yard for a commemorative piece made from Cinder Shed material. Suri refuses to sell disputed remains as freely recovered stock. Etta says she would prefer the buyer commissioned an ordinary shelf and paid the maker on time. The lost shed has become an image more readily than a completed obligation. Rattle Yard's work after the local ending is to keep useful transformation from stealing another person's unfinished claim.

The customer whose power can be cut

Digital Logisticians of the Grid specifies scarce materials beneath the broad salvage image: rare earth metals, high-grade fibre and geothermal or solar-hydrogen power. Its city contract imagines guaranteed power and coolant for sensitive orbital servers; its wealthy clients buy shielding and geothermal taps for Cloud Palaces and Audit Towers. Those are the dossier’s installation claims. They do not turn Rack Three’s ground test into a completed orbital consciousness transfer.

The source’s refusal is sharper than a supplier asking customers to behave. It warns the outside rebels to reduce demand and threatens to cut power when their operations jeopardise the supply workers’ survival. A movement fighting coercion can depend on people unwilling to be consumed by that fight. The conditional threat can also punish households around the crew accused of excess. Material competence gives the faction leverage as well as a job.

Its Antichain account blames seized taps, leaking cooling and violent conduit failures on recursive contradiction, and claims only Salvagers can enter the affected zones.
