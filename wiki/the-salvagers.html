⚔️ The SALVAGERS

⚔️ Lore 📅 Last updated: March 2026 📂 Lore

The Salvagers are the physical logisticians and recovery crews of the post-Unravelling world. They reclaim power systems, fibre, metal, machines, transport routes and usable components from infrastructure everyone else has already written off.

The Physical Chain

The Triple Fork broke consensus, but people still needed electricity, cooling, water, shelter and hardware. Salvager culture begins with that material fact.

They are suspicious of factions that speak as if a digital civilisation floats above physical scarcity. Every ledger, archive and broadcast still needs something somewhere to provide power and survive weather.

Extract, Repair, Move

Salvagers recover useful material from failed financial districts, server sites, transit systems, industrial ruins and abandoned networks. Their expertise combines heavy logistics with enough technical knowledge to know what is worth carrying.

They do not keep everything. Good salvage requires judgement about recovery cost, contamination, future use and whether an object is safer left where it failed.

Necessary to Everyone

Block Topia needs stable supply. Street Kingdoms need parts official supply chains will not send. High-status factions need protected infrastructure. Repair crews need raw material. That makes Salvagers unusually hard to isolate politically.

Their bargaining power comes from physical necessity rather than ideology.

Raiders, Fraud and Dangerous Ruins

Routes attract Aztec Raiders and other forces that would rather seize a resource than negotiate for it. Rugpull Miners can poison markets with fake components or fraudulent claims. Ruins themselves contain structural collapse, old malware, chemical hazards and machines still operating without human context.

Salvager competence therefore includes knowing when not to recover something.

Data Spirits and Ghost Ledgers

Old public pages turned dangerous old machines into data spirits and every rare cache into a supernatural relic. Those stories can remain part of Salvager folklore.

The convergence core is more useful: people who spend their lives in abandoned infrastructure inevitably develop names, rituals and cautionary myths around systems that behave unpredictably.

The Faction Weakness

The Salvagers prove that discarded things can still have value. Their danger is extending the same logic to people—treating lives, identities or cultures as inventories to be stripped for useful components.

Recovery is stewardship only while the thing recovered is more than the value extracted from it.
