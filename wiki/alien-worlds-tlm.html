🪐 Alien Worlds / TLM

✅ Source-cited🎮 Gaming🪙 Cryptocurrencies📅 Last updated: June 2026

Alien Worlds is a blockchain game and metaverse economy built around planets, mining, NFTs, missions, player activity and the token Trilium, usually shown as TLM. For the WAX ecosystem, Alien Worlds is one of the clearest examples of why WAX matters: it shows how a blockchain can support repeated game actions, digital item ownership, marketplace trading and token rewards at consumer scale.[1] [2]

What TLM is

Trilium, or TLM, is the token associated with Alien Worlds. WAXP is WAX’s native chain token; TLM serves the separate Alien Worlds game economy. Market profiles describe it as the utility token of the Alien Worlds metaverse and connect it to gameplay, planet governance and reward mechanics.[2] [3]

For collectors and players, this distinction is important. Buying TLM is not the same as buying WAXP. Holding Alien Worlds NFTs is not the same as holding TLM. Using WAX for Alien Worlds activity is not the same as investing in WAXP. These are connected layers, but they are not interchangeable.

Why WAX matters

WAX matters to Alien Worlds because WAX is built around high-volume digital commerce and NFT activity. Official WAX documentation highlights WAX as a chain for games, collectibles, tokens, meme coins and NFTs, and specifically names Alien Worlds among WAX-supported game ecosystems.[4]

A game like Alien Worlds needs repeated actions: mining, claiming, moving assets, interacting with NFTs, checking inventory, and participating in game-specific mechanics. These flows would become painful if every action required the user to calculate a high gas fee. WAX’s model is better suited to that pattern because it is designed around resources and account activity rather than only per-transaction gas thinking.[5]

Game loop and economy

The broad Alien Worlds loop is based on player participation in a metaverse economy: users interact with planets, use NFTs, mine or earn Trilium, and participate in systems that connect game actions to token flow. Public profiles describe TLM as tied to planet governance and gameplay incentives, while the project’s own site presents Alien Worlds as a metaverse with missions, mining and player participation.[1] [2]

NFTs and digital items

Alien Worlds also matters because of its NFT layer. WAX NFT infrastructure supports collections, schemas, templates and structured asset data. For a game, that structure can represent tools, land, characters, equipment, rewards or other game objects. The WAX docs describe NFTs through these structural concepts, which help marketplaces and apps understand what an asset is and how it should behave.[6]

In a normal collectible project, an NFT may be mostly visual. In a game economy, an NFT can also carry function. It can affect gameplay, rarity, progression, reward rate, appearance, access, status or strategy. Alien Worlds helped popularise that idea to a broad WAX audience by tying digital items to repeated gameplay.

Risks and caveats

This page is informational and not financial advice. Game tokens are high risk. Their value can depend on player retention, emissions, game balance, market liquidity, exchange support, bot pressure, reward design, regulatory changes and team execution. A token can be attached to a known game and still decline sharply if the economy weakens.

Blockchain-game users should also separate entertainment value from investment assumptions. Playing a game, collecting NFTs and buying a token are three different decisions. The fact that Alien Worlds is important to WAX history does not mean TLM is guaranteed to perform well in the market. The wiki records relevance, not price prediction.
